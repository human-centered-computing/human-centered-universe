# Human-Centered Universe

**Human-Centered Universe (HCU)** is an open, living, unbounded narrative universe built as a connected graph of story nodes, observer choices, and relationships.

The universe begins from one shared observation point:

> **BRG-0002 — First Vibration: Purify Yourself and Return to Your Essence**

From this origin observation node, every story is positioned inside a triangular state space defined by three fundamental centers:

- **HUMAN CENTER**
- **LIGHT CENTER**
- **DARK CENTER**

These centers are not good/evil categories. They are three independent dimensions used to describe how a story relates to human meaning, order, and possibility.

---

## Purpose and vision

Human-Centered Universe is both a **book** and an **open thought laboratory**.

Its purpose is to create a shared fictional space where people can explore ideas freely before those ideas become technologies, institutions, laws, educational models, cultures, social systems, or lived realities.

HCU is designed to make room for radically different questions and futures involving:

- artificial intelligence and technology,
- law, justice, governance, and institutions,
- education, science, and knowledge,
- belief, mythology, philosophy, and culture,
- cities, environment, and sustainability,
- freedom, security, power, and responsibility,
- family, memory, identity, death, and legacy,
- cooperation, conflict, social order, and transformation.

The universe does not require every idea to be comfortable, immediately correct, compatible with the current canon, or acceptable to everyone.

> **The right to imagine is broader than the canon.**

A story may question the canon.  
A branch may test another possibility.  
A fork may become an alternative universe.

Creative freedom, however, does not remove responsibility for consequences.

> **Imagine freely. Examine the consequences. Preserve human dignity. Transform what harms.**

### Transforming harm instead of merely hiding it

One of HCU's central aims is to examine ideas, systems, conflicts, and behaviors that appear harmful, dark, destructive, or even evil and ask:

- What real human need, fear, value, desire, or possibility may exist inside this form?
- Can the valuable part be separated from the part that causes harm?
- Can that value be redesigned in a human-centered form?
- Can the good be increased without reproducing the harm?

The purpose is **not** to romanticize evil, normalize harm, erase moral differences, or excuse responsibility.

The purpose is to understand difficult realities deeply enough to transform them.

Where possible, HCU tries to separate the human need or value hidden inside a harmful form from the mechanism that causes harm, then imagine a form in which the value can survive without reproducing the damage.

### A universe in which different cultures can recognize themselves

HCU is intended to be culturally plural.

Stories may emerge from different peoples, languages, places, belief traditions, philosophical systems, memories, myths, and ways of understanding existence.

The aim is not to reduce them to a single doctrine or claim that they all come from one invented historical or theological source.

Instead:

> **See the similarity without erasing the difference.  
> Explore the connection without inventing a single origin.**

A reader from a different culture should be able to encounter something recognizable without requiring another culture to disappear.

Connections between traditions may appear as:

- recurring human questions,
- narrative echoes,
- parallel symbols,
- shared structural patterns,
- contrasts,
- transformations,
- or different responses to similar human experiences.

### Science, philosophy, belief, and fiction

HCU can bring scientific ideas, philosophical questions, belief traditions, mythology, and science fiction into the same narrative universe.

But their epistemic boundaries must remain visible.

The project distinguishes among:

- established scientific knowledge,
- real research and engineering,
- interpretation,
- philosophical speculation,
- belief or cultural meaning,
- and science fiction.

Quantum concepts may therefore be used both in their established scientific meaning and as clearly identified narrative metaphors.

A **Quantum Echo** or **Quantum Time** relation in HCU is not, by itself, a claim of physical quantum entanglement or a new law of physics.

### From real projects to narrative possibilities

Human-Centered Universe is the speculative and narrative layer of the wider Human-Centered Computing ecosystem.

Real open research, software, artificial intelligence, education models, and human-centered transformation projects may enter HCU as:

- narrative artifacts,
- future technologies,
- alternative institutions,
- fictional consequences,
- or speculative extensions.

This allows real work and fictional possibility to illuminate one another without presenting fiction as current technical capability.

### Open contribution and visible transformation

HCU is designed as an open and continuously evolving universe.

People may contribute stories, translations, relations, alternative paths, questions, forks, and new interpretations.

Git records this process as part of the universe's memory.

A contribution is not only an addition to a repository. Once connected, it can change how previously existing nodes are understood.

> **Commit creates reality. Connection transforms meaning.**

The goal is not to finish the universe.

The goal is to create a structure in which new stories, new observers, new cultures, and new relationships can continue to reinterpret it.

## Core model

### HUMAN CENTER

**Human meaning, dignity, agency, relationship, responsibility, identity, and wellbeing.**

Primary question:

> **What does this mean for the human being?**

HUMAN CENTER includes:

- dignity and personhood,
- agency and choice,
- consent, privacy, and boundaries,
- relationship, love, and care,
- justice, rights, and fairness,
- responsibility and repair,
- identity, belonging, and culture,
- emotion and lived experience,
- meaning, memory, and mortality,
- wellbeing and vulnerability.

Its shadow may include paternalism, loss of boundaries, excessive anthropocentrism, or suppressing difficult truths in the name of harmony.

### LIGHT CENTER

**Order, knowledge, visibility, safety, structure, coordination, and verification.**

Primary question:

> **How can this reality be understood, ordered, protected, and verified?**

LIGHT CENTER includes:

- order and structure,
- knowledge, truth, and evidence,
- visibility and explainability,
- measurement and standards,
- safety and protection,
- predictability and planning,
- rules and institutions,
- coordination and efficiency,
- continuity and preservation,
- verification and auditability.

Its shadow may include excessive control, surveillance, forced transparency, rigid standardization, or treating one model as the only acceptable reality.

### DARK CENTER

**Possibility, freedom, uncertainty, creation, disruption, plurality, and transformation.**

Primary question:

> **What else could become possible?**

DARK CENTER includes:

- uncertainty and the unknown,
- freedom and alternatives,
- creativity and invention,
- exploration and experiment,
- dissent and resistance,
- rupture and disruption,
- transformation and change,
- risk and volatility,
- plurality and forking,
- emergence and surprise.

Its shadow may include irresponsibility, fragmentation, instability, destruction without repair, or escape from consequences.

## Triangular state space

Every story node has a relationship to all three centers.

The three normalized center weights always satisfy:

```text
HUMAN + LIGHT + DARK = 100
```

Example:

```json
{
  "center_weights": {
    "HUMAN": 30,
    "LIGHT": 10,
    "DARK": 60
  }
}
```

This does not mean that the story belongs to only one category. It means that its current position in the HCU state space is:

- 30% HUMAN,
- 10% LIGHT,
- 60% DARK.

The three vertices represent pure conceptual extremes:

```text
                    HUMAN CENTER
                       100
                        ▲
                       / \
                      /   \
                     /     \
                    /       \
                   /         \
                  /           \
       LIGHT CENTER ─────── DARK CENTER
            100                  100
```

The center of the triangle is not a morally perfect state. It simply represents a relatively balanced relationship among the three dimensions.

## 30-criterion classification system

Stories are not assigned percentages by intuition alone.

Each center contains **10 criteria**, and each criterion is scored from **0 to 10**.

Therefore:

```text
HUMAN raw score = 0–100
LIGHT raw score = 0–100
DARK raw score  = 0–100
```

The three raw scores are then normalized so:

```text
HUMAN% + LIGHT% + DARK% = 100
```

1. Dignity and personhood
2. Agency and choice
3. Consent, privacy, and boundaries
4. Relationship, love, and care
5. Justice, rights, and fairness
6. Responsibility and repair

A **coverage audit** should also check whether an important narrative element falls outside the current 30-criterion ontology. If so, the system flags it for human review instead of inventing a new center automatically.

## Origin node and unlimited growth

`BRG-0002 — First Vibration` is the **origin observation node**.

It is the shared starting point from which the reader first observes the universe.

It does not have to be the metaphysically earliest event in every interpretation. Future stories may describe states, memories, echoes, or possibilities that appear to precede the First Vibration while `BRG-0002` remains the canonical starting observation point.

The universe is unbounded by design.

A canon may contain a finite number of nodes at any particular observation state, but there is no predefined final number of stories, cultures, languages, relations, forks, or observer paths.

> **The universe is not only what has already been written. It also contains the space of relationships and possibilities that can still be created.**

## Story-node rule

Every new canonical story must enter the universe as a node.

Each new node:

1. receives HUMAN / LIGHT / DARK scores,
2. is positioned in the triangular state space,
3. connects to the universe through its relationship to the three centers,
4. may also reference existing story nodes when a direct narrative relation is useful.

No story has to be imprisoned inside one category.

Two stories become related when their positions, themes, observer paths, or explicit narrative relationships bring them close enough to be meaningfully connected.

## Observer model

The reader is an **Observer**.

Every observer begins from the First Vibration, but different observers may experience different story sequences.

At the end of a story, the reader may be presented with meaningful choices.

Each choice has an effect on:

```text
HUMAN
LIGHT
DARK
```

Example:

```json
{
  "label": "Explore the possibility outside the rules.",
  "effects": {
    "HUMAN": 3,
    "LIGHT": 1,
    "DARK": 10
  }
}
```

As the reader makes choices, an **Observer State** develops.

Example:

```json
{
  "observer_state": {
    "HUMAN": 28,
    "LIGHT": 51,
    "DARK": 21
  }
}
```

The system can then recommend the next unread story whose center profile best matches the observer's current tendency.

If LIGHT is dominant, a high-LIGHT unread story may be recommended.

If DARK is dominant, a high-DARK unread story may be recommended.

If HUMAN is dominant, a high-HUMAN unread story may be recommended.

However:

> **The universe recommends a path; it never imprisons the observer within it.**

The reader may always explore another available story.

## Quantum Time

HCU does not require one absolute narrative timeline.

The order in which an observer experiences story nodes becomes that observer's personal narrative time.

Therefore:

> **Nodes create the space. Choices create movement. Observation creates time.**

A path may look like:

```text
First Vibration
→ Story A
→ Story C
→ Story F
→ Story B
```

Another observer may experience:

```text
First Vibration
→ Story D
→ Story E
→ Story C
```

The universe can contain the same nodes while different observers experience different temporal sequences.

This is the HCU **Quantum Time** model: relational narrative time created by observation paths, not a claim that the story system is implementing a new physical law of quantum mechanics.

Technical infrastructure still uses normal chronological time for Git commits, builds, deployments, and releases.

## Git as narrative architecture

Git is not only a development tool in HCU. It is also part of the narrative model.

- **branch** = a possible reality
- **commit** = a reality recorded into the universe
- **pull request** = a proposed reality seeking connection with the shared universe
- **review** = observation and examination by other participants
- **merge** = a reality entering the shared canon
- **conflict** = incompatible realities meeting at the same point
- **fork** = an alternative universe
- **commit history** = the memory of the universe
- **revert** = a new reality that changes the effect of a previous reality without erasing that it occurred

A new commit does more than add content.

Once a new reality enters the graph, it may change how existing nodes, relationships, and earlier stories are interpreted.

> **Every commit creates a reality. Every new reality enters into relation with what already exists. Connection can transform the meaning of the whole universe.**

Short form:

> **Commit creates reality. Connection transforms meaning.**

## Language policy

**English (`en`) is the canonical and default language of HCU.**

Rules:

- every canonical story contains `content/en.md`,
- metadata and core system rules are written canonically in English,
- stories may be submitted in any source language,
- if the source is not English, a faithful canonical English form is created,
- the original language may be preserved as a translation layer,
- translations keep the same story ID,
- missing translations fall back to English,
- cultural adaptation is not a translation; it becomes a new story node.

Example:

```text
STORY-ID/
├── meta.json
├── analysis.json
└── content/
    ├── en.md   # canonical
    ├── de.md   # translation
    ├── ar.md   # translation
    └── ...
```

The interface language of authoring tools may remain English while source stories can be provided in supported languages or with a custom language code.

## Story Node Builder

The Story Node Builder provides a structured workflow for adding new stories to HCU.

Expected path:

```text
web/story-node-builder.html
```

Workflow:

1. Select the story's source language.
2. Paste the story and optional metadata.
3. Create the AI analysis prompt.
4. Send the prompt to an AI system that can return JSON.
5. Paste the AI JSON response back into the builder.
6. Validate the 30 criteria.
7. Calculate HUMAN / LIGHT / DARK center weights.
8. Visualize the story in triangular state space.
9. Generate observer choices.
10. Run the coverage audit.
11. Export a GitHub-ready ZIP containing the story files.

The builder does not need to expose an API key in the public GitHub Pages frontend.
