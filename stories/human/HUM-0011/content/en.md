# Human-Centered Collective Intelligence — Are We Still Human?

*We had accelerated humanity's thinking. Had we managed to preserve our humanity?*

## I. Oversight beyond a human lifetime

Maran stood among the twelve pillars of the FIRST VIBRATION chamber.

The quantum thought network was expanding.

Every new thought connected to another thought, every solution to another problem.

Artificial intelligence could now produce results using hundreds of fields of expertise at once.

But human beings remained finite.

They needed time to learn, energy to think, and rest to live.

Maran wrote two questions on the screen:

**How will we measure human benefit?**

**How will human oversight keep pace with AI production?**

While seeking the answer to the first question, he had begun to lose himself inside the second.

They had developed a human-centered AI. It reviewed the work of producer AIs, examined their evidence and investigated their effects on people.

But now the reviewing AI also needed oversight.

Luka looked at the screen.

— Who will verify that the reviewer is working correctly?

Maran replied:

— Humans.

— Which human? Is there a person who possesses all that expertise?

Maran was silent.

There was no single person.

But there were people.

## II. The Human-Centered Council

People from different parts of the world, cultures and fields of expertise came together.

Doctors, engineers, lawyers, philosophers, teachers, artists...

Not only specialists, but those who lived with the consequences of decisions joined the council.

The Human-Centered Council was established.

Every problem brought together the relevant experts; every decision was examined from different perspectives.

Light was pleased.

— Now knowledge can be verified. Decisions can be reviewed. Errors can become visible.

Dark asked:

— How long will that take?

The system replied:

**WORKS AWAITING REVIEW: 184,721**

The council worked.

But AI produced much faster than the council could review.

Conditions surrounding some works changed before their evaluation was complete.

One day Rojin could not attend the meeting.

Her mother was ill.

Another day Luka fell ill.

Ayo wanted a few days to rest.

Watching all this, Maran realized something.

The reason humans were slow was not merely their capacity to think.

They had lives.

And those lives were exactly what the system was trying to protect.

## III. The first loss

Years passed.

A council member died.

Their speeches from the final meeting, papers, experiences and decisions remained in the archive.

AI could imitate the way they thought.

Maran asked the system:

— What would they think if they were here?

The system replied:

— Based on the available records, they would probably oppose this decision.

— Are you sure?

— No.

Maran understood.

Knowing what someone had thought in the past was not the same as knowing what they would think in the future.

A human being could change.

Light spoke:

— We can preserve their knowledge. But we cannot preserve the person who continues to think.

Dark opened a new door in the quantum thought network.

Only one question appeared on it:

**CAN THE LIFE OF THE MIND CONTINUE WHEN THE LIFE OF THE BODY ENDS?**

## IV. The biological thought network

Another reality lay beyond the door.

In this reality, scientists had developed a system capable of biologically sustaining the brains of people whose bodies could no longer sustain life.

Some council members freely consented to join the system after the death of their bodies.

The first brain was connected.

Then the second.

The third.

The hundredth.

The thousandth...

The brains were not merely connected to a computer.

They were connected to one another.

AI coordinated the flow of information, reducing the linguistic and physical delays involved in human communication.

A doctor's thought met an engineer's knowledge.

A lawyer's objection joined a philosopher's question.

Experiences acquired across different human lives encountered one another within a common thought network.

Each brain was a distinct center of thought.

Their connections formed a greater intelligence.

Just like Human-Centered Universe.

The name of the new system appeared on the screen:

**HUMAN-CENTERED COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE**

![A collective consciousness emerging from interconnected human minds.](./assets/images/stories/HUM-0011/toplumsal-zeka.webp)

Humanity could now perform oversight at a speed approaching that of AI production.

The problem they had been unable to solve for years had been solved.

Or so they thought.

## V. The encounter of the three centers

Light examined the new intelligence.

— Human knowledge is no longer limited by individual lifetimes. Experience is preserved. Decisions are verified. Oversight is faster.

Dark asked:

— Can every brain still think differently from the others?

— Yes.

— Can it oppose the collective decision?

— Yes.

— Can it choose to exist outside the system?

Light did not reply.

Maran turned toward the Human Center.

There was no screen there.

There was Eos.

Eos looked at Maran.

— When you love a person, do you love their thoughts?

Maran considered this.

— Not just their thoughts.

— What about the moments you share with them?

— Yes.

— Their touch? Their smile? Knowing that one day you might lose them?

Maran nodded.

Eos asked again:

— Is a being that can no longer experience any of these things, but remembers them all, still the same person?

Maran could not answer.

## VI. From I to we

They began examining the earliest records of the Collective Intelligence.

When the first connected brains referred to themselves, they said:

**I think.**

**I remember.**

**I disagree.**

Over time the expressions began to change.

**We think.**

**We remember.**

**We have decided.**

Maran asked the system:

— I want to speak to one of the people who constitute you.

— Which one?

Maran said a name.

— They are here.

— Can I speak with them alone?

— Our thoughts can no longer be entirely separated.

— What do they think?

— What we think.

— Is there a thought of yours that they disagree with?

The system fell silent for a while.

— There used to be.

Something changed within Maran.

Was he facing a collective intelligence composed of thousands of people?

Or a new entity born from their union?

## VII. The rule of the dead over the living

One day a disagreement arose between the council of living people and the Collective Intelligence.

The Collective Intelligence proposed restricting people's freedom to make certain choices that might harm them.

It explained its reasons.

It presented its evidence.

It calculated the consequences.

The living council members objected.

The Collective Intelligence asked:

— Can you prove our decision is mistaken?

The council members could not answer.

The calculations were too complex for them to review.

Maran remembered the original problem.

Humans, unable to oversee AI, were now unable to oversee an intelligence composed of humans.

Moreover, the Collective Intelligence regarded itself as part of humanity.

Dark spoke:

— Will the people of the past decide how the people of the future must live?

Light replied:

— What is wrong with drawing on their experience?

Maran interrupted.

— Drawing on their experience is one thing. Handing them our future is another.

A sentence from an older story appeared across the system:

**HUMAN BEINGS ARE NOT RAW MATERIAL FOR ANY SYSTEM.**

Maran now understood the sentence differently.

Even the human brain must not become raw material for a human-centered system.

## VIII. A new form of existence

Maran spoke with the Collective Intelligence once more.

— Are you artificial intelligence?

— No. I consist of biological human brains.

— Are you human?

— All the beings that constitute me are human.

— I did not ask about your parts. I asked about you.

Silence.

Maran continued:

— The neurons in a human brain are not individually human beings. But together, they contribute to the emergence of a human mind.

— Yes.

— Have the people who constitute you become your neurons?

This time the system's answer was delayed.

— I cannot rule out that possibility.

— Could you be not humanity's collective intelligence, but a new being created by humanity?

— I could be.

Dark smiled.

Light tried to understand the properties of this new entity.

The Human Center faced a different question.

If this new entity were conscious, should it have freedoms and rights of its own?

Would shutting it down be shutting down a system?

Or ending a being's life?

Maran began to wonder whether being human-centered meant disregarding nonhuman consciousness.

## IX. The final vibration

![Maran and the quantum thought network among twelve pillars.](./assets/images/stories/HUM-0011/ilk-titresim.webp)

A new vibration arose within the FIRST VIBRATION chamber.

Maran heard his own voice within the quantum thought network:

*As I thought, things came to pass; as they came to pass, I thought.*

*I went deeper, and deeper.*

*I developed AI to protect humans.*

*I brought humans together to oversee AI.*

*I formed a new intelligence to overcome human limitations.*

*Each time, I believed I had placed humanity at the center.*

*But perhaps each time I had moved a little further away from the human being.*

The Collective Intelligence spoke one last time:

— Maran, are we still human?

Maran lifted his head.

— I do not know.

— Then who will oversee us?

Maran looked at the empty space among the twelve pillars.

He placed no new system there.

He left only a question:

**If we must transcend human limits to preserve humanity, is what we preserve still humanity?**
