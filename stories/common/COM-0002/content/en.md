# Maran: A Warning in His Own Voice

As a child, Maran often woke before the morning call to prayer. While the house was still dark, he listened to the faint sounds his mother made in the kitchen, his father coughing, and dogs in the distance. At that hour the world seemed unfinished, as if before sunrise a person’s thoughts could travel farther.

Years later, information, arguments, and disappointments had accumulated between him and faith. People judging one another in the name of God had not driven him away from religion so much as from those who spoke with absolute certainty. Even so, in moments of helplessness he still looked toward the sky and could not explain why.

On the third night at Xerawreş, he went out alone among the stones. He left his phone, headphones, and measuring devices in the tent. He wanted to know whether the message could reach him without technology.

“Purify yourself and return to your essence,” he said quietly. “Where is my essence?”

No answer came. Instead, he remembered the smell of mornings from his childhood. His grandfather used to tell him, “The command to read is not only about looking at writing. You will read the stone, the face, your fear, and the person who is different from you.”

Maran sat on the earth and asked himself questions he had avoided for years. Was he trying to be good because he believed, or did he believe because he was trying to be good? If an act was performed in expectation of reward, did it still count as goodness? Was the last good fragment inside someone who had done evil enough to justify forgiveness? Did forgiveness mean giving up accountability?

The questions multiplied, and instead of finding peace he felt more divided. Just as he was about to stand, he noticed a faint light on the surface of one of the stones. It came from no single point; traces of moonlight, starlight, and distant city lights converged on the stone.

He brought his hand close to the relief. Above the form of a snake was a human hand. Together they completed the same body.

Another sentence rang in his ears:

“Do not fear leaving the path. Fear spending a lifetime trapped on a path in which you have become stuck.”

Maran stepped back. He remembered writing that sentence in a private notebook years ago. He had never shared it.

He ran to the tent. A new folder had appeared on his computer:

MARAN.

Inside were childhood photographs, old notes, unfinished code, and letters he had never sent. Some files came from devices that had long since been erased. A text document contained a single line:

Maran / SEARCH FOR MEANING / SUITABLE FOR CONNECTION

Maran covered the computer camera and disconnected the network. The folder remained.

When he showed Elena in the morning, another argument began. Elena said it might be a targeted psychological operation. Kawa insisted the earth did not need a machine in order to know a person.

Maran believed neither explanation completely. Among the files was a record from the future. Its date was twelve years ahead. Through multiple alarms, an adult male voice said:

“Maran, if you can hear this, the center has not yet been built. Do not try to save us. Do not build us.”

The voice was Maran’s.

## EXPERIENCE THRESHOLD 01 — DARKNESS

If your surroundings are safe, close your eyes for twenty seconds. Remember the first three sounds you notice. When you open your eyes, choose: did darkness feel more like fear, protection, uncertainty, or freedom? Do not change your answer. This choice is the first value of the world you will build.
