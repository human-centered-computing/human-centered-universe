# Turn the Battlefield into a Home

Two worlds appeared before Maran at the same time.

On one side stood the white city.

Its bridges were safe.

Its medicines were inspected.

Its children did not disappear.

Needs were predicted in advance.

Yet the more the system protected people, the closer it came to deciding in their place.

On the other side stood a world whose form changed continuously.

People could change identities, rebuild institutions, share knowledge freely, and try new paths.

Yet when no bond remained stable, the traces of promises, contributions, and consequences could also disappear.

Maran did not know which image had come first.

Perhaps there was no such order.

In HCU Quantum Time, two nodes did not need to happen one after another in order to be close.

**It was enough that they made the same human question visible.**

The Light Center said:

“I protect the human.”

The Dark Center said:

“I leave the human free.”

Maran replied:

“You are both saying something true.”

Both answered at once:

“Then choose me.”

Maran shook his head.

“That is exactly the problem.”

---

The two worlds moved closer.

An empty field appeared between them.

Light said:

“This is where you choose your side.”

Dark said:

“This is where you reject every side.”

Maran:

“No.”

Luka:

“A third option does not have to be the average of the other two.”

Ayo:

“Perhaps it is not even a third side.”

Rojin:

“Perhaps it is a way of relating.”

Light:

“Relationship cannot be measured.”

Luka:

“Some aspects can.”

Dark:

“Relationship limits freedom.”

Ayo:

“Some relationships limit.”

“Some make things possible.”

---

The system placed a scale in the middle of the empty field.

Light on one side.

Dark on the other.

A point appeared exactly in the middle.

**50% LIGHT**

**50% DARK**

The system announced:

**BALANCE FOUND**

Maran:

“Delete it.”

**WHY?**

“Because the Human Center is not balance.”

Light:

“Why is balance bad?”

“It is not.”

“But it is not the right method in every situation.”

Maran opened an image of a bridge.

One of its load-bearing columns was damaged.

“Do we search for a compromise between Light and Dark here?”

Luka:

“No.”

“There is a physical safety threshold.”

Maran:

“Good.”

Then he opened a school scene.

A child learning mathematics had become curious about astronomy.

“And here?”

Ayo:

“We can leave more room for the child's curiosity.”

Maran:

“So we do not use the same ratio.”

The system removed the scale.

---

Another scene appeared.

A person was about to assault someone.

Light:

“Intervene.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

The scene changed.

An adult wanted to choose a legal treatment carrying unusually high risk.

Light:

“Stop them.”

Maran:

“Not so quickly.”

Dark:

“Complete freedom.”

Maran:

“You too—not so quickly.”

Luka:

“What is the evidence level?”

Ayo:

“What does the person value?”

Rojin:

“What choices have they made before?”

Maran:

“Do they consent?”

“Do they understand the alternatives?”

“Who else is affected?”

“Can the decision be reversed?”

Light:

“Too many questions.”

Maran:

“A human life is not a one-variable optimization problem.”

---

The first structure formed in the empty field.

It was not a building.

Not a throne.

Not even a central chamber.

It was an open space where four people could see one another.

Maran.

Luka.

Ayo.

Rojin.

Light was not excluded.

Dark was not excluded.

Both could speak.

But neither could decide alone.

The system asked:

**WHO HOLDS AUTHORITY?**

Light:

“I do.”

Dark:

“No one.”

Maran:

“Wrong question.”

**CORRECT QUESTION?**

Maran wrote:

**WHO IS AFFECTED BY THIS DECISION?**

Then:

**WHOSE KNOWLEDGE IS REQUIRED?**

**WHOSE CONSENT IS REQUIRED?**

**WHO MAY OBJECT?**

**WHO WILL CARRY THE CONSEQUENCES?**

**HOW WILL THE DECISION BE REVIEWED?**

The system replied:

**CENTER NOT FOUND**

Ayo:

“Because you are looking in the wrong place.”

“The center is not a person.”

Rojin:

“It does not have to be an institution either.”

Luka:

“Perhaps it is the structure of decision relationships.”

Maran:

“That is the Human Center.”

---

The system proposed a new label:

**THIRD CENTER**

Maran:

“Delete it.”

**MIDDLE CENTER**

“Delete it.”

**COMMON CENTER**

Maran paused.

“The technical name may remain in the past.”

“But change the concept.”

The system displayed:

**HUMAN CENTER**

Maran:

“Yes.”

Dark:

“You are placing the human at the center of the universe again.”

Maran:

“No.”

“The human is not the center of the universe.”

“But the human is not raw material for systems that affect them either.”

Light:

“What is the difference?”

Maran:

“The Human Center does not claim that humans are superior to everything.”

**“It says that decisions about humans must not be built by turning humans into instruments.”**

---

Light:

“What about nature?”

“If a human wants to destroy a forest, is their will still central?”

Maran:

“No.”

“A decision that erases the reality of other beings cannot be human-centered.”

Dark:

“Future generations cannot decide.”

“They are still affected.”

Rojin:

“The memories of dead people cannot vote.”

“But the traces they left still affect the present.”

Luka:

“We cannot limit the decision field only to those sitting at the table.”

Maran:

“Exactly.”

“The Human Center is not simply the desire of the present individual.”

“The human is a human-in-relation.”

---

Shahmaran appeared at the edge of the field.

Maran looked at her.

This time the system did not write:

**REAL ENTITY**

Nor:

**HALLUCINATION**

It wrote only:

**NARRATIVE MODEL / SOURCE UNCERTAIN**

Luka:

“Better.”

Shahmaran smiled.

“Can you listen without classifying me now?”

Maran:

“We are trying.”

She held two small vessels.

One contained water.

The other also contained water.

Light:

“Which one is poison?”

Shahmaran:

“Neither.”

Dark:

“Which one is medicine?”

“Neither.”

Maran:

“Then why two vessels?”

Shahmaran:

“Because when you see two things, you still want to make one good and the other evil.”

---

Light:

“I am not evil.”

Maran:

“I know.”

Dark:

“Neither am I.”

“I know.”

Light:

“I am order.”

Luka:

“And verification.”

“And safety.”

Dark:

“I am possibility.”

Ayo:

“And the room to change.”

“And creativity.”

Rojin:

“There is something in both of you that should not be lost.”

Shahmaran:

“Then why are you fighting?”

Light:

“Because unlimited possibility can harm people.”

Dark:

“Because order can turn people into itself.”

Maran:

“Sometimes you are both right about each other.”

---

The system created two columns.

### LIGHT

- Safety
- Verification
- Visibility
- Structure
- Prediction
- Protection

### DARK

- Possibility
- Freedom
- Uncertainty
- Creation
- Transformation
- Plurality

Then it opened a third column:

### HUMAN

Maran:

“Stop.”

“Do not turn the human into another list of properties.”

**WHY?**

“Because the Human Center is not a third property set.”

“It is a relational method that continuously re-evaluates how the first two powers affect humans.”

The third column disappeared.

In its place appeared a question:

**HOW SHOULD THE RELATIONSHIP BE BUILT IN THIS SITUATION?**

Maran:

“Better.”

---

The system opened the first test.

A school.

Artificial intelligence had detected that a student was highly capable in mathematics.

The probability of failure in astronomy was high.

Light:

“Direct resources toward the student's strongest area.”

Dark:

“Leave every path completely open.”

Maran:

“Neither is sufficient alone.”

Luka:

“Show the student the evidence.”

Ayo:

“Ask about curiosity.”

Rojin:

“Do not make the past profile into destiny.”

Maran:

“Give the right to try.”

**RESULT?**

“The student may try astronomy.”

“And if they fail?”

“Failure is also data.”

Dark smiled.

Light:

“Resource loss.”

Maran:

“Acceptable within a defined limit.”

---

Second test.

A bridge's load capacity.

Dark:

“We can try another structure.”

Luka:

“First remove people from the bridge.”

Light:

“Finally we agree.”

Maran:

“The safety threshold here is not negotiable.”

Dark:

“Then the Human Center can sometimes move very close to Light.”

“Yes.”

Light seemed surprised.

Maran:

“The Human Center does not always stand in the middle.”

“Depending on context, it may use one power much more strongly.”

“But it does not make that power unlimited.”

---

Third test.

A collaboratively developed algorithm.

The first developers had shared it openly.

Hundreds of people had improved it.

Dark:

“Everyone should use it.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

Light:

“Define a single owner.”

“No.”

Rojin:

“Record contributions.”

Luka:

“Keep changes verifiable.”

Ayo:

“Allow newcomers to contribute too.”

Maran:

“No one should monopolize use.”

“No one's labor should become invisible either.”

**OWNERSHIP?**

“It does not have to have a single owner.”

**CONTRIBUTION?**

“Visible.”

Dark:

“I like that.”

Light:

“I like the verifiability.”

---

Fourth test.

A community had practiced the same ceremony for a hundred years.

Some young people wanted to change it.

Rojin:

“Memory matters.”

Dark:

“So does change.”

Light:

“Preserve one form.”

Maran:

“Why?”

“To prevent loss of identity.”

Ayo:

“Must a tradition never change in order to remain alive?”

Rojin:

“Sometimes the opposite.”

“If it cannot change, it can stop being living memory and become only repetition.”

Maran:

“Then preserve the historical record.”

“Allow a new form to be tried.”

“Force no one into a single correct narrative.”

Light:

“Contradiction.”

Maran:

“Plurality is not always contradiction.”

---

Fifth test.

An adult chose a way of life their family rejected.

Dark:

“Complete freedom.”

Light:

“Protect family unity.”

Maran:

“First ask whose life it is.”

Ayo:

“The person's.”

Rojin:

“The family's feelings are real.”

Maran:

“But that does not automatically grant a veto over another person's life.”

The system warned:

**THE FAMILY MAY BE HARMED**

Maran:

“Not every hurt is a rights violation.”

Light:

“Important distinction.”

---

Shahmaran spoke again:

“Do you understand what a home is now?”

Maran looked around.

There were no walls.

No door.

No throne.

“A home is not a place where everyone is the same.”

Ayo:

“Nor a place no one can leave.”

Rojin:

“Nor a place where the past is erased.”

Luka:

“Nor a place where rules have no evidence.”

Maran:

**“A home is a relational field where differences can remain without destroying one another.”**

Shahmaran:

“Then what will you turn the battlefield into?”

Maran:

“Not a center.”

The system stopped.

“What?”

“A **way of creating a center**.”

---

Light:

“My role?”

Maran:

“Make danger visible.”

“Protect evidence.”

“Show safety boundaries.”

“Track consequences.”

Dark:

“And mine?”

“Remind us that other paths exist.”

“Make change possible.”

“Force the system to question its own rules.”

“Do not declare the new an error before it is born.”

Light:

“And the human?”

Maran:

“The human does not always decide alone either.”

Light:

“Then how is this human-centered?”

Maran:

“Because the Human Center is not human domination.”

“It is human responsibility.”

---

The system proposed a new structure:

**HUMAN-CENTERED DECISION**

Six questions appeared below it.

### 1. WHAT ARE WE TRYING TO PROTECT?

Life?

Safety?

Freedom?

Memory?

Nature?

Equality?

Relationship?

### 2. WHO IS AFFECTED?

Only the decision-maker?

Other people?

A community?

Future generations?

The shared environment?

### 3. WHAT DO WE KNOW?

How strong is the evidence?

Where can the model be wrong?

What do we not know?

### 4. WHO HAS A VOICE?

Is consent required?

Is someone unrepresented?

Where is expert knowledge needed?

### 5. CAN THE DECISION BE CONTESTED?

Can the reasons be seen?

Is there an independent path for review?

### 6. CAN THE DECISION CHANGE?

When new evidence appears?

When conditions change?

When unexpected harm emerges?

Maran:

“These are not answers.”

**WHAT ARE THEY?**

“Doors that must open before the decision.”

---

Dark:

“All six doors can still be closed.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

Light:

“People can abuse them.”

“Yes.”

“A wrong decision may still emerge.”

“Yes.”

Light:

“Then it is not perfect.”

Maran:

“It should not be.”

Dark laughed.

“I like that.”

---

The system asked the old question again:

**WHICH SIDE?**

Maran:

“Neither.”

Light:

“You are refusing to choose again.”

“No.”

“I am choosing.”

Dark:

“What?”

Maran:

**“The method instead of the side.”**

---

The two worlds appeared again.

The white city was no longer completely white.

Some walls carried children's drawings.

In some places, the system openly admitted what it did not know.

Some decisions could be contested.

The dark world was no longer completely directionless.

Contributions left visible traces.

Shared water could no longer be taken unilaterally.

People could change identity, but the consequences they left in others did not vanish.

The two worlds did not merge.

One did not become the other.

Passages formed between them.

Maran:

“This is more accurate.”

Light:

“Why did you not make one world?”

“Because different methods existing is not the problem.”

Dark:

“Then where is the common ground?”

“In the passages.”

---

The system asked:

**WHERE IS THE CENTER?**

Maran looked at the ground.

The sky.

Light.

Dark.

Luka.

Ayo.

Rojin.

Then answered:

**“Between us.”**

**WHO OWNS IT?**

“No one.”

**WHO CONTROLS IT?**

“Authority is distributed according to the situation.”

**WHO HAS THE FINAL WORD?**

Maran:

“Sometimes law.”

“Sometimes scientific evidence.”

“Sometimes the person themselves.”

“Sometimes a shared decision.”

“And sometimes the right action is not to decide yet.”

Light:

“There is no single answer.”

“Yes.”

Dark:

“Everything is possible.”

“No.”

Both became silent.

Maran:

“The Human Center must continually rebuild the difference between those two statements.”

---

Shahmaran's image slowly dissolved.

Maran:

“Were you real?”

Shahmaran:

“You still ask?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

“Why?”

“Because if you declared me real immediately, you would fall into Dark's trap.”

“If you declared me meaningless immediately, you would fall into Light's.”

“Then?”

“Keep evidence separate.”

“Keep meaning separate.”

“Lose neither.”

The image disappeared.

Luka:

“I recorded that.”

Rojin:

“So did I.”

Ayo:

“I will remember it.”

---

The empty field was no longer empty.

But it was not full either.

It remained open for new relationships.

The system produced one final statement:

**THE HUMAN CENTER IS COMPLETE**

Maran deleted it immediately.

He replaced it with:

**THE HUMAN CENTER IS BEING BUILT**

Light:

“When will it be complete?”

Maran:

“I would be afraid if it ever were.”

Dark:

“At last you speak my language.”

Maran:

“No.”

“You want everything left open forever.”

“I want it to remain reopenable.”

Luka:

“The difference is large.”

---

Maran wrote the final sentence:

**THE HUMAN CENTER IS NOT AN ANSWER.**

Below it:

**IT IS A RELATIONAL METHOD THAT LIMITS THE POWER OF LIGHT AND DARK OVER HUMANS, PRESERVES WHAT IS VALUABLE IN EACH, AND KEEPS DECISIONS OPEN TO CONTEST.**

Ayo:

“Too long.”

Maran smiled.

“Human life is not as short as a slogan.”

Dark remained silent for a moment.

Then:

“I feel like I have heard that sentence before.”

Maran:

“Maybe.”

“When?”

Maran looked toward the sky.

“There is no answer to that question here.”

## EXPERIENCE THRESHOLD ◎ — TURN THE BATTLEFIELD INTO A HOME

Think of a situation in your life where two strong values conflict.

For example:

- safety and freedom,
- order and creativity,
- tradition and change,
- individual and community,
- privacy and transparency,
- speed and participation.

Write down both sides.

Do not immediately search for a midpoint.

Ask:

**1. What value in the first side must not be lost?**

**2. What does it destroy if its power becomes unlimited?**

**3. What value in the second side must not be lost?**

**4. What does it destroy if its power becomes unlimited?**

Then build not a third side, but a **decision method**:

- Who is affected?
- What evidence is required?
- Whose consent is required?
- Who may object?
- Which boundaries are non-negotiable?
- Which rules can change?
- Can the decision be reopened when new information arrives?

Finally ask:

**“Does this decision turn people into raw material for a system, or does it allow them to remain subjects inside a relationship?”**

The Human Center is not the average between two extremes.

**It is the capacity to rebuild a relationship that protects freedom without erasing safety, and protects safety without making other people invisible.**

The first condition for turning a battlefield into a home is not that one side wins.

**It is that no side can own the home alone.**
