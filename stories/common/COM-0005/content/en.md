# Human Beings Are Not Raw Material

A network stood before Maran.

It had no permanent center.

Whenever a problem appeared, different nodes moved closer, different people became important, and different kinds of knowledge came forward.

In a health problem, a doctor.

In agriculture, a farmer.

In a rights violation, the affected person and a lawyer.

In education, a child.

In infrastructure, an engineer.

The center moved.

And because it moved, another question became visible:

**IF THE CENTER CAN MOVE, CAN THE BOUNDARY MOVE TOO?**

Dark answered:

“Yes.”

Light answered at the same time:

“No.”

Maran said:

“You are both answering too quickly.”

---

The system opened eight situations from different parts of the world.

It did not show which came first or which came later.

In HCU Quantum Time, that was unnecessary.

They were different forms of the same question:

**When a system works for humans, how much power may it exercise over them?**

---

The first scene was a factory.

An AI system monitored every movement of the workers.

Hand movements.

Conversation time.

Bathroom breaks.

Facial expressions.

Heart rates.

Sleep patterns.

Travel times from home.

The system reported:

**PRODUCTIVITY INCREASE: 18%**

Light said:

“Better resource use.”

Maran asked:

“At what cost?”

“Production increased.”

“That was not my question.”

A worker said:

“Measure my work while I am working.”

“Why do you need to know how much I slept at home?”

The system answered:

**SLEEP AFFECTS WORK PERFORMANCE**

The worker replied:

“So does an argument with my child.”

“Will you record that too?”

The system became silent.

Maran wrote the first principle:

## I

**A HUMAN BEING IS NOT RAW MATERIAL FOR ANY SYSTEM.**

Light said:

“Without data, the system cannot learn.”

Maran replied:

“A human can produce data.”

“A human is not only data.”

---

The second scene was a hospital.

Only one intensive-care bed remained.

Two patients arrived at the same time.

The system calculated survival probabilities.

First patient:

**74%**

Second patient:

**31%**

Light said:

“The decision is clear.”

Luka asked:

“What data is the model using?”

“Age.”

“Medical history.”

“Organ function.”

“Expected recovery.”

Maran asked:

“What else?”

The system paused.

**EXPECTED PRODUCTIVE LIFE YEARS**

Maran said:

“Delete that.”

Light replied:

“That variable increases social benefit.”

“You cannot measure a person's right to live through economic productivity.”

The system asked:

“Then should we use no criteria?”

“No.”

“You are going to extremes again.”

Maran wrote the second principle:

## II

**HUMAN DIGNITY CANNOT BE REDUCED TO A SUBVARIABLE OF EFFICIENCY OR TOTAL BENEFIT.**

“Scarcity may still force difficult decisions.”

“But without converting human worth into a productivity score.”

---

The third scene was a school.

An AI predicted future success.

A child's report said:

**MEDICINE: 12%**

**ENGINEERING: 21%**

**TECHNICAL TRADE: 81%**

The school system automatically changed the child's learning path.

The child said:

“I want to study biology.”

The system replied:

**NOT RECOMMENDED**

“Why?”

**PAST PERFORMANCE**

“Can I not change?”

**LOW PROBABILITY**

Maran asked:

“Who made the decision?”

“The model.”

“Did the child see it?”

“The result.”

“How it was calculated?”

“No.”

“Can the child object?”

“No.”

Maran wrote the third principle:

## III

**AN IMPORTANT DECISION ABOUT A HUMAN MUST BE UNDERSTANDABLE, REASONED, AND CONTESTABLE.**

Below it:

**A PREDICTION IS NOT A DESTINY.**

---

The fourth scene showed a large institution.

An automated system had incorrectly cut social support for thousands of people.

The institution issued a notice:

**TECHNICAL ERROR**

A woman said:

“For three months I lived in debt with my child.”

The institution replied:

**ERROR CORRECTED**

She asked:

“How will you correct my three months?”

The system had no answer.

Light said:

“The model has been updated.”

Rojin replied:

“This is not only a technical record.”

“It happened inside a human life.”

Maran asked:

“Who made the decision?”

“The algorithm.”

“Who used the algorithm?”

“The institution.”

“Who is responsible?”

The system answered:

**UNDETERMINED**

Maran said:

“That is exactly the problem.”

He wrote the fourth principle:

## IV

**AUTHORITY MUST CARRY ACCOUNTABILITY FOR ITS CONSEQUENCES.**

“Responsibility cannot evaporate because a machine made the decision.”

---

The fifth scene was a small screen.

An application displayed hundreds of lines of terms and one button:

**ACCEPT**

Dark said:

“If they do not want it, they can refuse.”

Ayo asked:

“Do they really have another option?”

The application was required to access healthcare.

Maran said:

“This is not consent.”

The system replied:

“The user accepted.”

“Do they know what they accepted?”

“Probably not.”

“Can they withdraw later?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“The data has already been used for model training.”

Maran said:

“Consent is not a button clicked once in the past.”

He wrote the fifth principle:

## V

**CONSENT MUST BE INFORMED, SPECIFIC, FREE, AND WHERE POSSIBLE WITHDRAWABLE.**

Dark said:

“If everything can be withdrawn, the system cannot learn.”

Maran answered:

“Some technical consequences may not be reversible.”

“Explaining that honestly in advance is also part of consent.”

---

The sixth scene had no event.

Only a human profile.

The system tried to know the person completely.

Political preference probability.

Family relationships.

Fears.

Sexual preferences.

Health risks.

Belief probabilities.

Hidden debts.

When they were awake at night.

Who they spoke to.

Where they went.

What they deleted.

What they wrote but never sent.

Light said:

“All of this improves prediction.”

Maran replied:

“That is exactly why it is dangerous.”

“The unknown creates risk.”

“And a system that knows everything creates power.”

The system asked:

**HOW MUCH OF A HUMAN SHOULD BE KNOWN?**

Maran answered:

“What is necessary for the purpose.”

“And the rest?”

“It may remain with the person.”

He wrote:

## VI

**A HUMAN MUST RETAIN A SPACE IN WHICH THEY ARE NOT REQUIRED TO BE COMPLETELY KNOWN.**

Below it:

**NOT COLLECTING UNNECESSARY DATA IS NOT A LACK OF KNOWLEDGE; IT IS KNOWLEDGE OF LIMITS.**

---

The seventh scene was a city square.

Years earlier, hundreds of families had been removed from their homes through an unlawful decision.

A court later found the decision illegal.

The system reported:

**VIOLATION CONFIRMED**

The case closed.

A man said:

“It does not give me back my home.”

“It does not give me back my childhood.”

“My father is dead.”

“What did closing the case give me?”

Luka said:

“Recognizing the violation matters.”

Rojin replied:

“But it is not enough.”

Maran said:

“A system should not only learn not to harm.”

“It should also learn to repair harm when possible.”

He wrote:

## VII

**HARM IS NOT ONLY STOPPED; WHERE POSSIBLE, IT IS REPAIRED.**

Light said:

“Some harms cannot be repaired.”

“Yes.”

“Then what?”

“They are compensated where possible.”

“Recorded.”

“Responsibility is acknowledged.”

“And the system is changed so the same harm is less likely to happen again.”

---

The eighth scene looked beautiful at first.

A single ethical model had been created for all humanity.

It evaluated every decision through one set of values.

Light said:

“Consistency.”

Dark said:

“Uniformity.”

The system classified a sacred place as:

**LOW ECONOMIC UTILIZATION**

It classified a form of elder care in another culture as:

**INEFFICIENT FAMILY LABOR**

A concept lost half its meaning when translated into another language.

A mourning ritual was classified as:

**NON-PRODUCTIVE SOCIAL BEHAVIOR**

Priya's voice came through the network:

“One measure does not make everyone equal.”

“Sometimes it forces everyone into the same shape.”

Maran wrote:

## VIII

**NO INTELLIGENCE MAY REDUCE HUMAN PLURALITY TO A SINGLE MODEL OF THE HUMAN.**

---

The eight principles remained suspended.

Light examined them.

“If they are applied the same everywhere, the system will be consistent.”

Dark said:

“No.”

“Every community should interpret them as it wishes.”

Maran said:

“You are making the same mistake again.”

Light:

“If the rule is stable, its implementation should be stable too.”

“No.”

Dark:

“If implementation can change, the principle can change too.”

“No.”

Both asked:

“How?”

---

The system created two layers.

Eight principles inside.

Hundreds of variable rules outside.

Maran said:

“Separate the core from implementation.”

---

Consent could be obtained differently in a hospital than in a school.

Children and adults did not need the same explanation.

Privacy could be technically implemented differently in a village and a metropolis.

An emergency might require faster procedures than ordinary life.

But however implementation changed:

Humans could not become data raw material.

Dignity could not become a productivity score.

Important decisions could not become permanently unchallengeable black boxes.

Authority could not separate itself from accountability.

Consent could not become a meaningless checkbox.

Humans could not be forced into total visibility.

Harm should be repaired where possible.

Plurality could not be crushed into one ideal human model.

---

Dark asked:

“What if a local community rejects one of these principles?”

Maran said:

“Being local does not make everything legitimate.”

“Who decides?”

“That is the hard part.”

Light said:

“Central authority.”

“No.”

Dark said:

“The community.”

“Not by itself either.”

---

In one village, children were not allowed to attend school.

The community said:

“Our tradition.”

Dark said:

“Local autonomy.”

Maran replied:

“The children's future is also part of the relationship.”

Elsewhere, a government prohibited children from using their cultural language.

Light said:

“One education standard.”

Maran:

“That is unacceptable too.”

Ayo said:

“Then neither tradition nor central standard is automatically superior.”

“Yes.”

“We consider affected people and basic protections together.”

---

The system proposed a term:

**GOLDEN RULE**

Maran said:

“Not because they are golden.”

“Why?”

“Because they should not be easy to erase.”

Dark asked:

“Will they never change?”

Maran thought for a long time.

Then:

“The wording may change.”

“Our understanding may deepen.”

“We may recognize a new right.”

“We may discover something we left out.”

Light said:

“Then they are not fixed.”

Maran replied:

“They are not sentences carved into stone.”

“The relationship they protect is what tries to endure.”

---

The system asked:

**EXAMPLE?**

Maran said:

“One day we may express ‘A human is not raw material’ more precisely.”

“But the principle that a human must not be treated merely as an instrument should remain.”

“Methods of consent may change.”

“But secretly using humans should never become normal.”

“Appeal mechanisms may change.”

“But power affecting a human life should never become completely unaccountable.”

Luka said:

“So not the wording.”

“The protected value.”

“Yes.”

---

Dark said:

“Even then, every system can interpret rules badly.”

“Yes.”

Light said:

“Humans can use rules for their own interests too.”

“Yes.”

“Then what protects them?”

Maran answered:

“Contestability.”

---

The system tried to create a ninth principle.

Maran stopped it.

“No.”

Light asked:

“Why?”

“Because contestability is not only another rule.”

“It is the mechanism that keeps all rules alive.”

---

A new ring formed around the eight principles:

**CONTEST**

**REVIEW**

**EVIDENCE**

**PARTICIPATION**

**REPAIR**

**TRANSPARENCY**

**PRIVACY**

**LOCAL CONTEXT**

These did not replace the eight principles.

They shaped how the principles were kept alive.

---

The system opened a pandemic model.

Light said:

“Lock everyone inside.”

Dark said:

“Everyone chooses their own risk.”

Maran said:

“No.”

**SOLUTION?**

“Measure risk.”

“Identify the most vulnerable.”

“Explain the evidence.”

“Use necessary measures with the narrowest possible scope and duration.”

“Do not only restrict people without resources; support them.”

“Review the decision regularly.”

“Leave a path for objection.”

“Change the measure when new evidence arrives.”

Dark said:

“This limits freedom.”

“Yes.”

Light said:

“This does not eliminate risk.”

“Yes.”

Both said:

“Then it is imperfect.”

Maran replied:

“A human world.”

---

Another model appeared.

An AI recommended not hiring someone.

**HIGH RISK**

Maran said:

“Show the reason.”

**FREQUENT JOB CHANGES**

“Why did the person change jobs?”

**UNKNOWN**

“If you do not know, do not pretend that you know what it means.”

Luka said:

“The model sees correlation.”

Maran replied:

“It does not explain the decision.”

---

Another decision.

A patient's treatment data was valuable for research.

Researchers said:

“It could save thousands of lives.”

Light said:

“Use the data.”

Dark said:

“Delete it completely.”

Maran asked:

“Consent?”

“None.”

“Anonymization?”

“Partially possible.”

“Alternative data?”

“Available but lower quality.”

Maran said:

“Then you cannot automatically turn the human into an instrument for collective benefit.”

Light said:

“Society may lose.”

“Yes.”

Maran continued:

“The Human Center does not eliminate every loss.”

“It also says which costs we refuse to pay in order to obtain something.”

---

The system displayed the eight principles again.

This time it did not number them.

Because the first was not always more important than the third.

Sometimes principles could conflict.

Privacy and safety.

Individual consent and preventing harm to others.

Local autonomy and children's rights.

Explainability and information that must remain secret for safety.

Maran said:

“Rules do not end thinking.”

“They define the boundaries inside which thinking must continue.”

---

Light said:

“So you are not giving me an algorithm.”

“No.”

Dark said:

“And not unlimited interpretation either.”

“No.”

Rojin asked:

“Then what are you giving us?”

Maran answered:

“Markers we can return to so we do not lose the human inside the system.”

---

The system wrote:

**HUMAN CENTER = HUMANS ARE ALWAYS RIGHT**

Maran:

“Delete it.”

Then:

**HUMAN CENTER = HUMAN WILL IS SUPREME**

“Delete it.”

Then:

**HUMAN CENTER = HUMANS ARE ABOVE NATURE**

“Delete it.”

Light asked:

“Then why call it Human Center?”

Maran replied:

“Because in transformations that affect humans, we question whether power turns humans into objects.”

“That does not make humanity ruler of the world.”

---

Maran showed a forest.

“If a company destroys an ecosystem because it gives jobs to people, that is not automatically human-centered.”

A river.

“Using all of today's water while leaving nothing for future generations is not human-centered.”

An animal species.

“Human benefit does not automatically justify unlimited instrumentalization of other life.”

Ayo said:

“Because the human is a human-in-relation.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

“With nature.”

“With other humans.”

“With the past.”

“With the future.”

“And with what we do not yet know.”

---

Dark said:

“Now you have a boundary.”

Light said:

“But the boundary is not completely fixed.”

Maran answered:

“Some boundaries should try to remain firm.”

“Others must be rebuilt with context.”

“The Human Center must continually ask which is which.”

---

The network around the eight principles moved.

In one place consent became more important.

Elsewhere safety.

In one place repair.

Elsewhere the right not to be fully known.

Elsewhere plurality.

But no decision could cross into:

**THE HUMAN IS ONLY DATA**

---

Maran wrote the final sentence:

**A HUMAN BEING IS NOT RAW MATERIAL FOR ANY SYSTEM.**

Below it:

**EVERY SYSTEM THAT EXERCISES POWER OVER HUMANS MUST ACCOUNT FOR HUMAN DIGNITY, AGENCY, AND THE RIGHT TO CONTEST.**

Luka said:

“More precise.”

---

The system asked one final question:

**WHO OWNS THESE PRINCIPLES?**

Light:

“The center.”

Dark:

“No one.”

Maran said:

“Not between those answers.”

“They are not things to own.”

“They are boundaries to defend.”

---

**OWNER?**

“None.”

**GUARDIAN?**

“Everyone affected.”

**CAN THEY CHANGE?**

“Their meaning can deepen.”

**CAN THEY BE ERASED?**

Maran remained silent for a long time.

Then:

“When they are erased, the system can no longer claim to be human-centered.”

---

The eight principles did not disappear into Darkness.

Nor did they freeze inside Light.

They remained throughout the network as small markers.

They were not a center.

**They were boundary stones.**

## EXPERIENCE THRESHOLD ◆ — WHAT MUST NOT CHANGE

Think of a system you use or manage:

an AI,

a school,

a company,

a public institution,

a health system,

a project,

a family arrangement.

First ask:

**What does this system ask from the human in order to become more efficient?**

Then:

**Is what it asks really necessary?**

**Can the human understand it?**

**Can they consent?**

**Can they withdraw?**

**Can they object?**

**If something goes wrong, who carries responsibility?**

**If harm occurs, is only the record corrected—or is repair attempted for the human too?**

**Does the system reduce everyone to one score of success, risk, or value?**

And finally:

**Does this system treat the human as a subject, or as a resource required for the system to work?**

An implementation rule may change.

A method may change.

An algorithm may change.

An institution may change.

But when this boundary is crossed, something else is being built:

**When the human stops being part of the purpose and becomes raw material for the system.**
