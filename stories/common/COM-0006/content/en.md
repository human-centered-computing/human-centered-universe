# Creation Is Unfinished

Maran woke at sunrise. People across the excavation site were silently watching the sky. No one knew how much time had passed. According to the devices, the connection had lasted only three seconds. Maran felt as if he had spent years living in other worlds.

Elena handed him water.

“What did you see?”

Maran did not know where to begin.

“That a system can remove the human from a person in order to protect them,” he said at last. “And isolate them in order to call them free.”

“Did you find the source of the message?”

“No.”

Elena’s expression showed relief rather than disappointment.

“Maybe that is a good thing. Certain answers become institutions very quickly.”

In the months that followed, the Alpha One network grew. People came together not only to investigate the source of the message, but to transform problems in their own fields through human-centered methods. Each effort began with a real need, examined it through different forms of knowledge, and tried to produce an open model, a measurable experiment, and a usable result. Technology was used not to replace the human, but to strengthen judgment, creativity, safety, dignity, and the ability to act.

Elena returned to Bologna, Amara to London, Kenji to Kyoto, Lucía to Bogotá, Priya to Kochi, and Aroha to Aotearoa. Kawa remained in Riha. Amina established a local circle in Dakar, Mateo in Cusco, Lejla in Sarajevo, Nergüi in Ulaanbaatar. Maran moved back to Berlin, but he no longer understood return as separation. People working around the same question did not have to live in one center, govern one another from a distance, or become the same in order to build something together.

Seven-year-old Murat wrote the ninth rule of the Rih Bridge in large letters with Maran and hung it on the wall of the study:

“Children have the right to forget, grieve, and become people different from their father.”

Three-year-old Rodi drew two small figures beneath the sentence, standing apart but with their hands touching. Every morning, as Maran passed that wall, he remembered that the project was not meant to carry him into the future. It was meant to allow the future to remain free from him.

The network did not turn itself into one institution. In one city a group worked on education; elsewhere on justice, agriculture, disaster management, or health. Each began from a local problem and tried to share its method universally.

Maran opened the first record:

RECORD 0001

Date: 29.07.2026

Status: Human-centered computing network active.

Principle: Develop locally. Design universally. Publish openly. Transform humanely.

Beneath the record was a link Maran had not added:

https://github.com/human-centered-computing/human-centered-universe

There was a second door in the repository for readers:

https://human-centered-computing.github.io/human-centered-universe/

Three words stood at the top of the page:

READ / EXPLORE / CREATE

A reader did not have to follow the story only in sequence. They could follow connections, choose another language, store a World Seed in their browser, and, if they wished, open a new branch through GitHub and become part of producing the universe.

When Maran opened the repository, the page was still small. A handful of repositories, a short manifesto, and a great deal of unfinished work. He could not tell whether he was seeing the beginning of the future he had witnessed, or the source from which everything had already come.

The time became 17:25.

A new notification appeared:

“First reader connected. World Seed received.”

The blank from the dream opened again:

RİHÊ ______

“Riha is the spirit of what?”

Maran looked into the camera. He expected to see his own reflection in the dark surface of the screen.

This time, you were there.

## REALITY THRESHOLD

This first version brings mythic narratives, questions of belief, human-centered design principles, mathematics, quantum physics, and fictional technologies into the same world. The narrative is organized around four movements: superposition, entanglement, measurement, and decoherence. Some structures described in the text are possible today; some remain at early research or design stages; some work only within the rules of this fictional universe.

Similarities among religions and cultures in the story are not claims that all beliefs historically derive from one source or teach the same doctrine. Similar motifs may arise independently, influence one another, or respond differently to common problems of human life. The novel does not attempt to resolve theological differences. It asks how different answers might remain around the same human question without erasing one another.


The **Tapetum Sentinel** used in *The Stone Network* is a story-world advanced field deployment based on the intended architecture of the real open-source project. The project is designed to combine drone and field imagery, Retinex Tapetum low-light enhancement, task-specific detection, tracking and mission metadata, an evidence index, and local question answering within one architecture: https://github.com/human-centered-computing/tapetum-sentinel. The archaeological mission in the novel fictionalizes a later, field-adapted form of that architecture. Visual patterns flagged by the system are not treated as archaeological conclusions; raw data, enhanced imagery, inference, and uncertainty remain separate.

Quantum concepts are not presented as evidence that human consciousness can create the universe according to desire. Entanglement is not faster-than-light communication, uncertainty is not mere ignorance, and observation is not reduced to a human looking at something. The book deliberately keeps a boundary between established physics, philosophical interpretation, and science-fiction extension. It does not claim that those layers are identical.

The phrase “biological quantum center” in Record X is likewise a character’s hypothesis. Established science has not shown that the human brain is a quantum computer. Although atoms in the brain obey quantum laws, that does not imply that thought is processed through qubits, that consciousness provides quantum computational advantage, or that the brain by itself can create a new physical universe. In the novel, the brain is used not as a proven quantum processor, but as a biological center capable of carrying contradiction, memory, emotion, and value within one relationship. Its coupling to a quantum field and self-activation through an unknown light belong to the science-fiction mystery.

The Rih Bridge is also not presented as a technology available today. Limited movement or speech intentions can be decoded from neural activity in living people, and AI can help translate such signals into text. In animal studies after death, some cellular and circulatory functions have been partially restored under specific experimental conditions. These studies did not revive a conscious person, make personal memories speak, or demonstrate a continuing self after death. A long-preserved human brain communicating through a computer, guiding children personally, or continuing as the same individual belongs to the speculative layer of the book.

For this reason, the story keeps biological tissue, personal archive, and AI inference visibly separate. A voice resembling someone we loved does not prove that person has returned. The central problem of the Rih Bridge is not immortality, but identity, consent, grief, children’s autonomy, and the point at which the influence of a dead person ought to end.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06377-x

The story does not label every line internally as fact, interpretation, or fiction, because its fundamental question is not only:

“Is this real?”

The deeper question is:

“What structure are we building today for the future we want to become real?”

Creation is unfinished.

It begins again when the reader connects.
