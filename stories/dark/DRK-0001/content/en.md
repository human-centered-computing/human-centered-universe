# The Hand You Hold While Falling

There were no fixed roads in the world of the Dark Center. When Maran took one step, the color of the ground changed; with the second, the direction of the sky changed. People could create houses, animals, mountains, and even new physical rules through thought.

“No destiny is written in advance here,” said the Dark Center. “No one tells you who you must become.”

Dark refused to allow any possibility in the quantum layer to stabilize into a shared world. Every measurement became a new branch, every objection a new universe. But as the branches separated, a promise made in one world no longer carried force in another. Freedom gradually became a technique for escaping consequences.

For a moment the sky turned into writing:

RİHA / SPIRIT OF POSSIBILITY

“When spirit is confined to one body, it dies,” said the Dark Center. “I give it infinite forms.”

For the first time, Maran projected an image directly from his mind. He thought of the house of his childhood. The mulberry tree in the courtyard, the stone steps, and the shadow of his mother at the kitchen window appeared before him. His heart accelerated as he approached the door.

Ayo caught his arm.

“That is not your mother,” Ayo said.

“I know.”

“Knowing does not prevent attachment.”

Maran did not open the door.

There was no limit to creation in the dark world, but there was no shared rule for protecting what had been created. Powerful minds could enter other people’s worlds and alter their memories. Information was treated not as something to steward, but as material that could be seized.

In one valley, two communities tried to agree on sharing the same water source. One developed knowledge that could multiply the available water. The other stole the knowledge and diverted the source into its own territory. In response, the first community created a virus that erased its rival’s past. Hundreds of people in the valley forgot who they were.

“Why don’t you stop this?” Maran asked.

“If I stop them, I limit their freedom.”

“One person’s freedom is erasing another person’s existence.”

“Then they must learn to defend themselves. Struggle is the source of development.”

Luka examined the surrounding flow of information.

“Creativity is high. Destruction is also high. The system is unstable in the long term.”

Rojin knelt beside a child who had lost their memory. The child no longer knew their own name, but remembered a lullaby their mother had sung. When Rojin completed the song, some of the people whose memories had been erased began to cry. Their identities did not return completely, but they felt that they belonged to one another.

That was when Maran saw where goodness could exist inside darkness. Goodness appeared not as a ready-made rule, but in the person who chose to carry another’s memory even though no one had commanded it. Darkness did not manufacture evil. It left unrestricted the kind of freedom whose consequences could be placed on someone else.

The Dark Center heard his thought.

“You blame me. I gave you the power to create.”

“Giving power is not enough,” Maran said. “There must also be a bond that carries the consequences of that power.”

“A bond is a chain on freedom.”

Ayo answered:

“Some bonds are not chains. They are the hand you hold when you are falling.”
