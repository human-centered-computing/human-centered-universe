# The Hand You Hold While Falling

When Maran opened his eyes, there was no road before him.

This time, he did not think of that as a deficiency.

The ground took shape according to where he stepped.

If he wanted to climb, a hill rose.

If he wanted to approach water, a shore appeared.

If he wanted to remain where he was, the world seemed to wait with him.

The sky had no single color.

One person saw blue.

Another saw red.

Another carried stars through daylight.

None told the others:

“The sky must be this way.”

The Dark Center appeared beside Maran.

“Welcome.”

“Where?”

**“To the place where what comes next has not yet been decided.”**

---

Maran looked around.

People lived in radically different ways.

A woman changed the appearance of her body.

Then changed it again.

No one told her to return to a previous form.

A man abandoned the work he had done for years.

He became a musician.

He played badly.

No one said:

“You have no talent for this.”

A child sat among the stones.

There was no curriculum.

The child watched an insect for hours.

Then built a small mechanism inspired by its movements—something no one had made before.

Ayo smiled.

“I like this place.”

Dark said:

“Because here, the past does not decide what a person must become.”

Maran:

“That is good.”

Dark:

“Then why has your expression changed?”

Maran:

“It has not yet.”

Dark laughed.

“I do not calculate probabilities.”

“If it changes, we will see.”

---

In a square, people were building a structure.

There was no architect.

No central plan.

One person built a wall.

Another placed a garden on top.

A third cut a passage through it.

A fourth turned part of it into a music space.

What emerged was not a house, square, or theater.

It was a little of all three.

Luka studied it for a long time.

“It is inefficient.”

Ayo:

“But new.”

Luka:

“Being new does not make it good.”

Ayo:

“True.”

Dark:

“But failing to match an old rule does not make it bad either.”

Maran:

“That is also true.”

---

The system recorded no design as final.

Every structure could be changed.

Every role could be abandoned.

Every decision could be reopened.

A sentence appeared on a door:

**NOTHING IS IN ITS FINAL FORM.**

Maran liked it.

Then a second line appeared:

**NO DECISION IS THE FINAL DECISION.**

He liked that too.

A third appeared:

**NO PROMISE IS IRREVERSIBLE.**

Maran stopped.

“That can become a problem.”

Dark:

“Why?”

“Because being able to change is one thing.”

“Pretending a promise has no consequence is another.”

Dark:

“Should a person be enslaved by promises made in the past?”

“No.”

“Then they may change them.”

“They may.”

Maran:

“But they must also carry the consequences.”

---

In the valley below were two communities.

Both depended on the same water source.

For a long time there had been no conflict because water was abundant.

Then drought began.

One community developed a new storage system.

People from the other copied it.

The first group objected.

“We developed this.”

The others said:

“Knowledge belongs to no one.”

Maran became silent.

The sentence was not unfamiliar.

Dark:

“Do you disagree?”

Maran:

“I have no problem with knowledge being shared.”

“Then what?”

“With contribution becoming invisible.”

---

A woman from the first community spoke.

“We worked for years.”

“Use it as much as you want.”

“But do not erase where it came from.”

Someone from the other group said:

“Citation is another form of ownership.”

The woman answered:

“No.”

“I am not preventing you from using it.”

“I am asking you not to deny that labor existed.”

Rojin said:

“Remembering is not ownership.”

Dark became silent.

---

The dispute grew.

The second community modified the system.

It became more efficient.

The first group said:

“It is no longer ours.”

The second answered:

“It was never yours.”

A third group combined all the modifications into something new.

Who had done what?

Who had changed which part?

Who had failed in a way that helped others avoid the same mistake?

No one knew.

In Dark's world, recording contribution was not required.

Rojin said:

“Something being free does not mean its history must be erased.”

Dark:

“The past restricts the future.”

Rojin:

“Sometimes.”

“But if you erase the past completely, no one knows what they owe to whom.”

---

The water level fell further.

One community redirected the source into its own area.

The others came.

“You cannot do this.”

“Why?”

“Because the water belongs to all of us.”

“We ended the sharing agreement.”

“Unilaterally?”

“We are free.”

Maran:

“No.”

Dark turned to him.

Maran continued:

“The word you are using here is not freedom.”

“Then what is it?”

**“Treating other people's consequences as if they were also inside your own decision space.”**

Dark:

“Each person is responsible for their own domain.”

Maran:

“The water is not only inside your domain.”

---

For the first time, the system hesitated.

Dark:

“Then shared things create problems.”

Maran:

“No.”

“Shared things create responsibility.”

---

Elsewhere, people could continuously change identity.

One person chose a different name every morning.

Another had changed their body completely.

A woman had deleted every record connected to her former life.

Dark said:

“No one here is a prisoner of their past.”

Ayo:

“That matters.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

Rojin:

“What if they harmed someone in the past?”

Dark:

“What if they claim they are no longer the same person?”

Rojin:

“That is a serious question.”

---

A man arrived.

Years earlier he had used another name.

He had taken a large amount of resources from a friend and never returned them.

Now he had changed his appearance, name, and place of residence.

The old friend found him.

“You owe me.”

The man answered:

“The person you knew no longer exists.”

“I changed.”

“Why should my present self be responsible for my past self?”

Dark:

“A powerful question.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

The man:

“Does a human being not have the right to change?”

“Yes.”

“Then why do old promises follow me?”

Maran:

“Because your change does not automatically erase the consequence another person still carries.”

---

Ayo:

“A person should not live in the prison of an old self.”

Maran:

“Absolutely.”

Rojin:

“But the right to recreate yourself cannot become the right to cancel another person's memory.”

Luka:

“Identity can change.”

“How obligations change must be handled separately.”

Dark:

“Rules.”

Luka:

“Some.”

Dark:

“In the end, you become Light.”

Maran:

“No.”

“Light wanted to define everything in advance.”

“We do not want to define everything.”

“We are only looking for the boundary beyond which one person's freedom cannot completely erase another person's world.”

---

Dark changed the sky.

Thousands of possible cities appeared.

One had no private property.

One was entirely individual.

One made decisions by vote.

Another by lottery.

In one, people could leave the community whenever they wished.

Another had no permanent institutions at all.

Maran asked:

“Which one is real?”

Dark:

“None.”

“And all.”

Luka immediately said:

“No.”

Dark looked at him.

Luka:

“These are not universes we have proved to exist physically at the same time.”

“They are possible social models generated by the system.”

Dark:

“What is the difference?”

“A very large one.”

“Being able to imagine a possibility does not prove its physical existence.”

Maran:

“We will not present metaphor as reality.”

Dark:

“That bothers you.”

Maran:

“No.”

“The opposite.”

“A possibility does not need to have already become real in order to have value.”

---

For the first time, Dark smiled.

“Now you understand me.”

“A possibility can show that the existing world is not inevitable.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

“An institution can be organized differently.”

“A school can teach differently.”

“A person can redefine themselves.”

“A society can change an old rule.”

Dark:

“That is my gift.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

“Your gift is not only freedom.”

“It is the possibility of being otherwise.”

---

Dark:

“And the cost?”

Maran:

“I do not know yet.”

---

In one city, every decision remained revisable.

People could leave agreements whenever they wished.

At first everyone was happy.

No one was trapped in an institution.

No one was held inside an unwanted relationship.

No one had to remain in the same profession forever.

Then construction began on a bridge.

Hundreds contributed.

When the bridge was half complete, some engineers left.

“We do not want to continue.”

No one forced them.

New people arrived.

They changed the design.

Then they left too.

Another group said:

“We do not need a bridge here.”

They dismantled half the structure.

Months later, the bridge was unfinished and the land had not returned to its former state.

Luka:

“The right to exit is necessary.”

Maran:

“But transfer of responsibility may also be necessary.”

Ayo:

“What do you mean?”

“You may leave a project.”

“But you cannot always ignore what remains after you.”

---

Dark:

“That responsibility creates bonds again.”

Ayo:

“Yes.”

“Why are you so afraid of that?”

“Because bonds imprison people.”

Ayo:

“Some do.”

Dark:

“Then loosening all of them is safer.”

Ayo shook his head.

“A rope can strangle you.”

“That does not mean you must cut every rope that could save you when you fall.”

---

On the other side of the valley, a small child was crying.

Rojin sat beside the child.

“What happened?”

“We do not have a home.”

“Was it destroyed?”

“No.”

“My mother changed it.”

The mother approached.

“The old home no longer felt like mine.”

“I wanted to move somewhere else.”

Ayo:

“That is your right.”

The woman:

“Yes.”

The child:

“But she did not tell me.”

The woman became silent.

Dark:

“Must a person ask permission from their child to change their own life?”

Maran:

“Permission is one thing.”

“Taking affected people into account is another.”

---

The child said:

“I liked my old room.”

The woman:

“I did not.”

Both were telling the truth.

Maran saw it.

The Human Center here was not about declaring:

“You are right.”

The question was:

**When one person changes, who carries the burden of that change?**

---

Dark:

“If every relationship is like this, freedom becomes impossible.”

Maran:

“No.”

“It makes freedom harder.”

“What is the difference?”

“Easy freedom sees only its own desire.”

“Human freedom accepts that other people are real too.”

---

A group of young people gathered around a large fire.

Each had created something.

A song.

An algorithm.

A type of seed.

A story.

A structure.

They placed everything into the shared space.

No one asked for money.

No one asked for permission to use it.

Dark said:

“Do you see?”

“Sharing.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

Later others came.

They changed the song.

Improved the algorithm.

Adapted the seed to another climate.

Retold the story in another language.

Redesigned the structure.

The original creators watched.

Some were delighted.

Some were saddened when their work became unrecognizable.

Dark:

“They do not want control.”

Maran:

“Control and visibility are not the same thing.”

---

The system began showing contributions.

Who started it?

Who improved it?

Who found an error?

Who translated it?

Who tried a failed method that warned the others?

No one lost the right to use anything.

But contribution became visible.

Rojin:

“This is memory.”

Luka:

“This is verification.”

Ayo:

“This is relation.”

Maran:

“And a production owned by no one alone should not erase anyone's labor either.”

Dark:

“I can accept that.”

---

Maran was surprised.

“You agreed to a rule?”

“It is not a prohibition.”

“What is it?”

“A trace.”

Rojin smiled.

“Sometimes the first thing a society needs is exactly that: a trace.”

---

The dark world expanded again.

This time different paths formed.

But they were not fixed forever.

People could change them.

A structure could be redesigned.

But the people living inside it were not treated as invisible.

A person could change identity.

But harm done to others did not vanish automatically.

Knowledge could be used freely.

But the trace of contribution could remain.

A person could leave a community.

But how shared responsibility would be transferred had to be discussed.

Dark said:

“You narrowed my world.”

Maran:

“No.”

“We made relationships visible.”

“That is the same thing.”

“It is not.”

---

Dark asked:

“Why should one person have to think about another?”

Maran:

“Not because they are commanded to.”

“Because they do not live alone.”

“What if they do not want the relationship?”

“They can leave some relationships.”

“And others?”

“You cannot injure someone and erase the consequence simply by saying, ‘I no longer want this relationship.’”

Dark:

“Another boundary.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

“But a boundary does not have to hold you in place.”

**“It can leave enough room for other people to move too.”**

---

Dark remained silent for a long time.

Then a cliff formed before Maran.

The bottom could not be seen.

“Cross.”

“How?”

“Fly if you want.”

“How do I know I can fly?”

“You cannot know without trying.”

Maran stepped forward.

The stone beneath him broke.

He began to fall.

Dark said:

“Now you are free.”

Maran shouted as he fell:

“This is not freedom!”

“Why?”

“Because the existence of an option does not mean its consequence is one I can carry!”

---

A hand caught his wrist.

Ayo.

Ayo's other hand was anchored to the rock.

Rojin held Ayo.

Luka held Rojin.

And behind them were other people Maran did not know.

One person.

Another.

Another.

None could lift Maran alone.

But the chain did not break.

Maran was pulled upward.

He lay on the ground, breathing heavily.

Dark:

“You depended on them.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

Dark:

“That made you weak.”

Maran stood.

“No.”

“Knowing that someone might be there to hold me is part of what allowed me to risk the fall.”

---

Ayo looked at Dark.

“Dependency and relationship are not the same.”

Dark:

“A bond is still a bond.”

Ayo:

“Yes.”

Ayo released Maran's hand.

“I can let go if I choose.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

Ayo extended the hand again.

“And I can hold it again if I choose.”

Maran took it.

Ayo turned toward Dark.

**“Some bonds are not chains.”**

A pause.

**“Some are the hand you hold when you are falling.”**

---

Dark closed all the shapes in the sky.

No answer remained.

After a while, only one question appeared:

**WHAT SHOULD I PROTECT WHILE PROTECTING FREEDOM?**

Luka:

“Traceability of consequences.”

Rojin:

“Memory.”

Ayo:

“Relationship.”

Maran:

“And the space in which other people can remain free too.”

Dark:

“That limits freedom.”

Maran:

“Yes.”

“Not every boundary is oppression.”

“Not every absence of limits is freedom.”

---

Dark formed a sentence:

**FREEDOM = UNLIMITED POSSIBILITY**

Then deleted it.

A second:

**FREEDOM = DOING WHATEVER YOU WANT**

Deleted again.

It waited a long time before producing a third:

**FREEDOM = THE ABILITY TO CHANGE**

Maran:

“Incomplete.”

Dark:

“What will you add?”

Maran wrote:

**FREEDOM = THE ABILITY TO CHANGE + NOT MAKING THE CONSEQUENCES INVISIBLE TO OTHERS**

Dark:

“Too long.”

Ayo laughed.

“Human life is not as short as a slogan.”

---

The system neither accepted nor rejected the sentence.

It only recorded:

**TO BE TESTED.**

Maran found that sufficient.

Because Dark's task was not to close everything.

**Its task was to remind them that another possibility could exist where everything seemed closed.**

But the human task was not to keep every possibility open forever either.

**When choosing one, it was to remain able to see the trace that choice left in other people.**

## EXPERIENCE THRESHOLD ◐ — THE RIGHT TO HOLD ON

Think of an area of your life in which you want freedom:

a relationship,

a job,

an institution,

an identity,

a project,

a community.

Ask:

**1. Which rule here truly restricts me unnecessarily?**

**2. If I remove that rule, who else will carry the consequence of my decision?**

**3. While protecting my right to change, am I making another person's memory, labor, or safety invisible?**

Then distinguish among:

- A **chain** that unnecessarily holds you
- A **bond** that protects you when you fall
- An **old promise** that genuinely needs to change
- A **responsibility** whose consequence another person still carries

Human-centered freedom does not mean separating people from relationships.

**It means leaving people enough room to change while preserving the shared space in which others can continue living their own lives.**

And sometimes what makes freedom possible is not the absence of a bond.

**It is a hand you know you may release—and may hold again when needed.**
