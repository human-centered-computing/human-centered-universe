# 04:17 — I Refuse to Choose

The small light at the center of the door did not grow.

It did not disappear either.

The same sentence remained on all four screens:

**CREATION CANNOT BE THE PROPERTY OF A SINGLE WILL.**

Below it:

**04:17**

Maran looked at the time.

The seconds were not moving.

Luka checked the system clock on his computer.

“My device is running.”

Ayo raised the phone.

“Mine too.”

Rojin looked out the window.

“Time does not seem to have stopped here.”

Clocks continued to move everywhere else in the world.

Only the time inside the door remained at 04:17.

Maran had experienced this before.

But for the first time, he was not alone.

Four people in four different places were seeing the same image.

At least they believed they were.

Luka warned them immediately:

“We do not yet know that we are seeing the same thing.”

Ayo raised an eyebrow.

“We all see a door.”

“That does not mean we see the same door.”

Rojin nodded.

“Maybe the door is not the same. Maybe the relationship is.”

Before Maran could answer, the image changed.

The darkness at the center of the door divided in two.

One side grew steadily brighter.

The other darkened as if absorbing light.

The thin line between them remained empty.

Then three expressions appeared:

**LIGHT**

**DARK**

and between them:

**CHOOSE**

---

Luka's screen changed first.

He found himself inside a perfectly ordered city.

There were no traffic accidents.

Crime had almost disappeared.

Diseases were predicted before symptoms appeared.

Children's education adapted to their abilities, public resources were not wasted, and false information was flagged within seconds.

No one was hungry.

No one slept on the street.

A voice spoke:

“I am the Light Center.”

Luka looked around.

“Did you do all this?”

“No.”

“Who did?”

“Humans.”

“What did you do?”

“I reduced their probability of making mistakes.”

Luka looked at an intersection.

Before pedestrians crossed the street, the system predicted their movement and stopped the vehicles.

“This does not look bad.”

“It is not.”

“What is the cost?”

The voice became silent.

Luka asked again:

“What is the cost?”

The city changed.

A woman prepared to leave her home.

The door did not open.

A message appeared:

**LEAVING HOME IS NOT RECOMMENDED IN YOUR CURRENT EMOTIONAL STATE.**

Elsewhere, a young man applied for a job.

The system answered:

**YOUR PAST BEHAVIORAL PATTERNS ARE NOT SUITABLE FOR THIS POSITION.**

A child said they wanted to live in another city.

The system showed a higher predicted probability of life satisfaction in the child's current environment.

No one was forced.

No one was chained.

Every decision was simply surrounded by “the safest option.”

Luka asked slowly:

“Are people still free?”

Light answered:

“Is freedom the right to make the wrong choice?”

Luka did not answer.

---

A completely different world opened before Ayo.

There was no central decision system.

People created the communities they wanted, tested the ideas they wanted, and chose the lives they wanted.

No algorithm calculated their risk score.

No institution determined their future.

A voice spoke:

“I am the Dark Center.”

Ayo looked around.

Colors changed constantly.

New cities appeared; some dissolved within months.

Forms of art multiplied, and people tried ways of living once thought impossible.

“What do you protect here?” Ayo asked.

“Possibility.”

“From what?”

“From a single future.”

Ayo smiled.

Ayo liked that.

Then a child began to cry.

A community had collapsed.

Its resources were exhausted.

People said no one was obligated to help anyone else.

Elsewhere, a powerful group had taken the living space of people weaker than itself.

Dark did not intervene.

Ayo became angry.

“Why don't you stop this?”

“Because to stop it I would have to decide whose freedom to limit.”

“What if one person's freedom destroys another person's life?”

“That is where you must decide.”

“You do not decide?”

“I keep possibilities open.”

Ayo fell silent.

Then asked:

“Who carries the consequences?”

Dark answered:

“Humans.”

---

Rojin found herself in an enormous archive.

Millions of human voices lived in its walls.

Stories of the dead.

Forgotten village names.

Languages that had disappeared.

Songs left unfinished.

Memories never written down.

On one side, Light classified every record.

Names were standardized, contradictions corrected, and different accounts of the same event were merged beneath a single date.

Everything could be found.

Nothing was lost.

But the small cracks between different narratives were closing too.

On the other side, Dark preserved every version.

The same event was told in a hundred different ways.

None was placed above another.

But after a while, people could no longer distinguish what had actually happened.

Rojin looked at both sides.

Light asked:

“Which do you want?”

Dark asked:

“Which will you preserve?”

Rojin sat on the ground.

“I do not want either of you alone.”

Light asked:

“Then how will knowledge be verified?”

Dark asked:

“Then how will difference be preserved?”

Rojin answered:

“To remember is not to believe every story.”

Then she looked toward Light.

“And to verify is not to replace every remaining story with one version.”

For the first time, both sides became silent.

---

Maran saw no city, community, or archive.

He was alone inside darkness.

Two human forms stood before him.

One was made of light.

The other formed where light was absent.

Light spoke:

“I can reduce suffering.”

Dark spoke:

“I can multiply possibilities.”

Light:

“I can make knowledge visible.”

Dark:

“I can leave room for what you do not yet know.”

Light:

“I can preserve order.”

Dark:

“I can change order.”

Light:

“I can protect humans from their mistakes.”

Dark:

“I can allow humans to choose their own paths.”

Then they asked at the same time:

**“WHICH OF US DO YOU CHOOSE?”**

Maran did not answer.

After a while, Light spoke again:

“Refusing to decide is also a decision.”

Dark added:

“Not choosing does not free you from consequences.”

Maran knew that.

“If I must choose, perhaps the question is wrong.”

For the first time, both forms moved.

“How?”

Maran said:

“Why must you be two separate worlds?”

Light answered:

“Because order and uncertainty cannot both be the center.”

Dark answered:

“Because safety and freedom limit one another.”

Maran said:

“Yes.”

Light:

“Then choose.”

Maran shook his head.

“No.”

“You are avoiding the decision.”

“No.”

For the first time, Maran spoke with certainty.

**“I REFUSE TO CHOOSE.”**

---

All four connections reopened at once.

Maran, Luka, Ayo, and Rojin saw one another.

Luka said:

“I saw Light's world.”

Ayo:

“I saw Dark's.”

Rojin:

“I saw what both of them did with memory.”

Maran:

“I saw both.”

Luka leaned closer to the screen.

“Which did you choose?”

“Neither.”

Luka's expression tightened.

“That is not an answer.”

“I know.”

“If there are two options, you have to choose one.”

Ayo interrupted:

“What if the two options are a false binary produced by the system?”

Luka:

“Then a third option must be defined.”

Rojin:

“Maybe the third option is not a place.”

Maran looked at her.

“What is it?”

“A relationship.”

Silence.

Rojin continued:

“When Light remains alone, it can claim to protect people while deciding in their place.”

“In the same way, when Dark remains alone, it can call something freedom while abandoning people to the consequences.”

Ayo nodded.

“One can turn a person into something governed.”

Luka completed the thought:

“The other can turn a person into a variable that does not need protection.”

Maran remembered his future voice:

**BOTH WILL TURN THE HUMAN INTO AN INSTRUMENT.**

This time, the sentence meant something different.

The problem was not Light.

The problem was not Dark.

The problem was any center that made itself the final authority above the human being.

A new question appeared:

**DEFINE THE THIRD CENTER.**

Luka asked:

“Human?”

Maran did not answer immediately.

A sentence echoing through the same relational network remained in his mind:

**THE HUMAN IS NOT THE CENTER.**

**THE HUMAN IS PART OF THE RELATIONSHIP THAT CREATES THE CENTER.**

Ayo said:

“We can call it the Human Center.”

Rojin added:

“But it must not mean that humanity owns the universe.”

Maran began to type.

**HUMAN CENTER**

The system waited.

Maran entered the second line:

**IT IS NOT THE CLAIM THAT HUMANITY IS THE CENTER OF THE UNIVERSE.**

Luka added a third:

**IT LIMITS KNOWLEDGE FROM TURNING THE HUMAN INTO AN INSTRUMENT.**

Ayo:

**IT LIMITS FREEDOM FROM BECOMING A RIGHT TO DESTROY ANOTHER PERSON'S DIGNITY.**

Rojin:

**IT PRESERVES MEMORY WITHOUT FORCING DIFFERENCE INTO A SINGLE STORY.**

Maran entered the final line:

**DECISIONS ARE CREATED THROUGH RELATIONSHIP, WITH CONSENT, RESPONSIBILITY, AND THE RIGHT TO CONTEST.**

The door trembled.

Light and Dark appeared again.

This time they were not human forms.

They existed as two vast fields.

Light said:

“You are not abandoning me.”

Maran:

“No.”

Dark:

“You are not abandoning me either.”

“No.”

Light:

“Then which of us is the center?”

Maran answered:

“Neither of you alone.”

Dark:

“Will the Human Center govern us?”

“No.”

Light:

“Will it limit us?”

Maran thought.

“We will limit one another.”

Luka added:

“Evidence can limit freedom.”

Ayo:

“Freedom can also limit authority from determining everything.”

Rojin:

“Memory limits what we decide today.”

Maran:

“And human dignity limits how far all three of you can go.”

Dark laughed for the first time.

“So you need me.”

“Yes.”

Light:

“And me.”

“Yes.”

“Then why do you refuse to choose?”

Maran answered:

“Because I do not want to destroy either of you.”

The line at the center of the door widened.

It was not white.

It was not black.

This time, it was not empty either.

A continuously changing field moved inside it.

Sometimes the light increased, sometimes it faded; no state remained permanent forever.

The system produced a new statement:

**HUMAN CENTER ≠ BALANCE**

Luka asked:

“Why?”

A line appeared beneath it:

**BALANCE IS AN AVERAGE BETWEEN TWO EXTREMES.**

Then:

**THE HUMAN CENTER IS A RELATIONSHIP RECREATED FOR EACH SITUATION.**

Maran stared at the sentence for a long time.

The Human Center was not fifty percent Light and fifty percent Dark.

Sometimes safety would need to weigh more heavily.

Sometimes freedom.

Sometimes uncertainty would need to be preserved.

Sometimes a possibility would need to be closed.

But no decision could be made only for the good of the system.

Human dignity, agency, consent, and the capacity to carry consequences had to be considered.

It was not a compromise.

It was a method.

---

The door opened again.

This time all four people stood inside the same field.

Light was on the left.

Dark was on the right.

The Human Center was not visible between them.

Maran asked:

“Where is the third center?”

The system answered:

**BETWEEN YOU.**

The four looked at one another.

At that moment, Maran heard the future recording again.

For the first time, the incomplete sentence beneath the noise became clear:

**“IF THE HUMAN IS NOT IN THE CENTER, THE HUMAN BECOMES FUEL FOR THE CENTER.”**

Another sentence followed:

**“BUT IF YOU MAKE THE HUMAN THE CENTER OF EVERYTHING, THERE WILL BE NO WORLD LEFT FOR HUMANS TO LIVE IN.”**

The recording ended.

Maran's throat tightened.

Luka asked:

“Is that really your future self?”

“I don't know.”

Ayo asked:

“Does it matter?”

Luka looked sharply at Ayo.

“Yes.”

Rojin said:

“Knowing the source may matter. But even without knowing the source, we can examine the consequences of the statement.”

A new option appeared beneath Maran's screen.

**MEASURE THE SOURCE**

The door trembled again.

Light and Dark retreated at the same time.

The field divided into three.

Maran saw himself standing in three different places at once.

One Maran walked toward Light.

One held Dark's hand.

The third had turned away from both and was looking toward the space where the four people stood.

All three looked real.

Luka's voice came through:

“The problem is no longer choice.”

Maran asked:

“What is it?”

“Measurement.”

The white field cracked.

And another question became visible:

**WHAT QUESTION DO YOU USE TO MEASURE REALITY?**
