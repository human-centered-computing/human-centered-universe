# 17:25

The network took the system offline to prevent the door from opening. Servers were shut down, power connections cut, storage devices removed. Yet the door remained visible on every screen: powered displays, dark displays, even the broken glass of an old device whose battery had been removed.

A counter beneath the door moved toward 17:25.

Amara told Maran to step away from the system. Elena tried to seal the ground above the void temporarily. Kenji physically separated every sensor. Lucía transferred the recurring forms from her dreams onto paper. Priya checked whether warnings in different languages were being forced into one shared meaning. Aroha worked with the local team to redraw safety boundaries so that neither the people nor the site would be treated merely as experimental material. Kawa watched the others run from task to task.

“We tried closing the door with earth before,” he said. “Twelve thousand years later, here we are again.”

At 17:24, the recording containing Maran’s future voice opened again.

“Maran, do not build the center.”

This time the recording continued.

“Light will want to end pain. Dark will leave choice free. Both will turn the human into an instrument. If you build the third center as a battlefield, everyone loses.”

“What is the third center?” Maran asked the screen.

The recording did not answer. Its final sentence disappeared beneath alarm sounds:

“If the human is not at the center, the human…”

At 17:25, the underground sphere emitted light for the first time. It did not pierce the soil. Instead, every shadow in the surrounding area changed direction for a moment. Human shadows stretched not toward the sun, but toward the center of the void.

Maran closed his eyes.

When he opened them, he was no longer in the laboratory.

He stood alone in an endless white field. He could see his body but felt no weight. Far away was a black point. As it approached it expanded and took human form.

“I am the Dark Center,” the figure said. “I will give you the freedom to create anything you want.”

A second voice came from behind him. A brighter human shape separated from the white field.

“I am the Light Center,” it said. “I will protect you from fear, error, and pain.”

A third space opened between the two forms. It was neither black nor white. It had no color because no decision had yet been made there.

Luka, Ayo, and Rojin appeared beside Maran.

“Choose your side,” the two Centers said at the same time.

Maran remembered his future voice.

“I choose neither,” he said.

The white field cracked.
