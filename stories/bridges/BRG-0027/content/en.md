# The Exception

Rules were strongest when everyone believed there could be no exception.

Yet exceptions existed.

Some people could arrive late. Some could leave early. Some could ignore procedures that others had to follow exactly.

Nobody called this unfair.

They called it experience, trust, necessity, or simply the way things worked.

One day I asked why a particular person was allowed to do something forbidden to everyone else.

The answer was immediate:

“He knows what he is doing.”

I looked at the rule again.

If judgment could replace the rule for one person, why could it not replace it for everyone?

No one answered.

I learned another lesson: institutions do not only have rules. They have people who stand outside the rules, and people who learn to live inside them.
