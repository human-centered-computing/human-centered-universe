# 04:17 — The Call from the Dark

Night fell early in Xerabreşkê.

This small village near Göbekli Tepe stood in an open, bare landscape by day. At night, it seemed to drift slightly away from the rest of the world.

Darkness here was not the darkness of a city.

It was not a shadow trapped between streetlights.

It was a vast emptiness that slowly absorbed the horizon, the stones, the houses, and the people.

Elena, Amara, Kenji, Lucía, Priya, Aroha, and Maran had come to the village from different countries and for different reasons.

Kawa hosted them in the stone house inherited from his family.

Each of the seven read the world differently.

Elena studied the past buried beneath the earth.

Amara studied the human brain.

Kenji studied vibration and sound.

Lucía studied how people turned what they lived through into stories.

Priya studied the invisible boundary between humans and technology.

Aroha studied the relations a person forms not only with the self, but with past and future generations.

Maran, among all of them, still did not know what he was searching for.

Perhaps that was what separated him from the others.

The others had questions.

Maran had only dreams.

For weeks, he had been seeing the same one.

There was no light in it.

No people.

No sound.

Only a vibration coming from an unseen center inside the dark.

It repeated three times.

Then nothing happened.

Every time Maran woke, he looked at the clock.

**04:17**

The first time he ignored it.

The second time he called it coincidence.

The third time he moved his phone away from the bed.

Still, he woke again.

**04:17**

On the fourth night he decided not to look at the time.

In the morning, when he turned on his phone, a frozen counter stood on the screen.

**04:17**

He had told no one.

On their first evening in Xerabreşkê, after Kawa cleared the table, the electricity went out.

The house disappeared into darkness.

Kenji reached for the light on his phone.

Kawa stopped him with a gesture.

“Let it stay like this for a while,” he said.

No one asked why.

The silence of the night grew.

A dog barked far away.

Then it too became silent.

The sky was clear, but the moon was nowhere to be seen.

Maran stepped into the courtyard.

The direction of Göbekli Tepe could not be distinguished in the dark.

For the first time, that did not disturb him.

The darkness was showing him nothing.

On the contrary, it was taking away everything he could see.

And perhaps for that reason, he began to notice something else.

A very faint tremor passed beneath his feet.

He stopped.

The vibration stopped with him.

Amara came out behind him.

“Did you feel it too?”

Maran did not answer.

Kenji’s voice came from inside the house.

“Come here.”

He placed a recorder on the table.

On its screen, three small rises broke an almost perfectly flat line.

Three pulses.

Almost evenly spaced.

Kenji frowned.

“There is no source.”

“What do you mean?” Elena asked.

“The device recorded a vibration. But there is no directional information. As if it did not come from anywhere.”

Aroha smiled.

“Perhaps not everything needs a direction.”

Elena objected at once.

“A measured vibration has a physical explanation.”

“It may,” Aroha said.

“I am only saying we do not know it yet.”

That night everyone withdrew to their rooms.

Maran remained awake for a long time.

At one point he thought he heard a very deep hum rising from the dark.

Then he slept.

---

In the dream, he was standing.

But there was no ground beneath him.

No below.

No above.

No distance.

Only darkness.

He raised his hand.

He could not see it.

He could not even feel his own body.

Then something moved at a point unimaginably far away in the dark.

It was not light.

It was not a shape.

It was as if one darkness had separated itself from another.

A center.

The center did not grow.

It did not approach.

Yet Maran knew it was there.

The first vibration came.

The entire emptiness trembled.

The second came.

Maran remembered where his heart was.

The third came.

And a single thought passed through the darkness:

**What else could become possible?**

Maran tried to answer.

No voice came out.

The center vibrated again.

This time the question changed.

**Who are you when everything you know disappears?**

The darkness split open.

Maran found himself inside a city.

Buildings were moving.

Walls cracked, objects fell, people sprang from their sleep.

The earth rolled with immense force.

Then the sounds merged.

Stone.

Concrete.

Glass.

Cries.

Silence.

He saw a clock.

There was no date.

No other number.

Only:

**04:17**

At that moment the world went dark.

But the dream did not end.

No light rose from beneath the ruins.

No answer descended from the sky.

No one told humanity what it had to do.

Instead, an invisible wave left by the rupture spread outward through the darkness.

Maran understood that it was not sound.

Nor light.

It did not behave like matter.

It was not thought either.

It was as though a great rupture had left behind a **trace of possibility**.

The wave expanded.

It crossed cities.

Mountains.

Seas.

Then it crossed time.

Maran did not know how he knew this.

But within the dream he felt it with certainty:

The call had not been left only for that night.

It could reach even those not yet born.

---

Elena was dreaming elsewhere.

Every layer she had ever excavated disappeared.

There was no past and no history.

Only an unexcavated field of darkness before her.

She had spent years trying to understand what had already happened.

For the first time the dream asked her something else:

**How do you excavate what has not happened yet?**

---

Amara saw her own brain from outside.

Billions of neurons flashed and faded.

Then every light went out.

One connection remained in the darkness.

At its other end was a person she had never met.

Yet she felt that stranger’s fear as if it were her own.

The dream asked:

**Must consciousness remain inside a skull?**

---

Kenji listened to the silence of the entire world.

There was no frequency.

Then he heard three pulses.

One.

Two.

Three.

He looked at his device.

Instead of a measurement, it displayed a time.

**04:17**

For the first time, he decided to listen to something he could not measure instead of silencing it.

---

Lucía saw the dreams of millions of people.

Each person inhabited a different story.

No two dreams were the same.

Yet in every dream, at some point, darkness expanded and a time appeared:

**04:17**

Lucía tried to combine the dreams into a single story.

The moment she did, they all vanished.

Only when she allowed them to remain separate did invisible connections begin to form between them.

---

Priya found herself in a dark corridor lined with an endless number of doors.

Behind each door was a different future.

In one, humans controlled machines.

In another, machines controlled humans.

In another, the two formed a new kind of consciousness together.

In one, technology had never developed.

In another, the being called human no longer existed in its present form.

The dream declared none of the doors correct.

It only asked:

**Who has the right to choose a future?**

---

Aroha saw nothing.

In the darkness she only felt hands.

The hands of the living.

The hands of the dead.

The hands of people not yet born.

They did not touch.

But whenever one moved, the positions of the others changed.

Then every hand vanished.

One question remained:

**Does a person’s choice belong only to that person?**

---

The seven woke at the same moment.

The house was silent.

Kawa had opened the courtyard door and was looking into the dark.

Without speaking, they gathered around the table.

Kenji looked at his watch.

**04:17**

Maran’s expression changed.

“Mine too,” he said.

Elena looked at him.

“What?”

“The time in my dream.”

Amara raised her head.

“Was it 04:17?”

Silence.

Lucía took a notebook from her bag.

In the middle of the page she wrote a single number.

**04:17**

Priya placed her phone on the table without saying anything.

She had taken a screenshot before going to sleep.

An alarm she had never created appeared in the alarm app.

04:17.

Aroha’s phone was off.

“I did not look at the time,” she said.

“I did not need to.”

“How did you know?” Elena asked.

Aroha looked into the darkness.

“There was no number in the dream. But when I woke, I knew it.”

Kenji turned on the recorder.

At 04:17 it had captured three pulses.

Evenly spaced.

One.

Two.

Three.

Then silence.

Elena stood.

“There has to be an explanation.”

Maran looked at her.

“There may be.”

“There has to be.”

“Those are not the same statement.”

Elena did not answer.

The question from her own dream was still inside her.

How do you excavate what has not happened yet?

---

By morning, the village was unchanged.

Stone houses.

Dirt roads.

Göbekli Tepe in the distance.

Sky.

Wind.

Nothing seemed different.

Yet a small rupture had begun inside each of the seven witnesses.

Elena began to read years of collected evidence differently. She started to think that findings could speak not only about the past, but also about how humans create meaning around the unknown.

When Amara returned home, she changed her research program. She began studying not only the individual brain, but patterns of synchronized fear, grief, and solidarity among people.

Kenji did not delete the three pulses.

He did not place them in a folder called “measurement error.”

He renamed the file simply:

**0417**

Lucía began collecting anonymous dream reports from different parts of the world.

At first she found only a few similarities.

Then hundreds of reports arrived.

Then thousands.

What people saw differed completely.

Some saw the sea.

Some their mothers.

Some a child who had never been born.

Some an empty city.

Some a forest.

Some only darkness.

Yet a surprising number of people said the same thing:

“When I woke, it was 04:17.”

Priya refused to use the reports to build a system that would interpret them for people.

“Systems may show possibilities,” she said.

“But the moment a system begins interpreting the dream in place of the human being, it begins choosing that person’s future as well.”

Aroha started no research project.

She returned to her community.

She repaired a relationship she had left unfinished.

She knocked on the door of a relative she had not spoken to in years.

She began teaching a student everything she knew.

When asked about her dream, she said only:

“Some calls do not change the world first. They change you. The world changes afterward.”

Maran remained in Xerabreşkê for several more days.

Every night he expected to wake at 04:17.

He did not.

A week passed.

Then a month.

Nothing happened.

Just when he believed it had ended, he dreamed again.

The Dark Center was before him.

This time it asked no question.

Maran waited.

“What do you want from me?” he asked.

No answer.

“What am I supposed to do?”

No answer.

“What is 04:17?”

The darkness did not move.

For the first time Maran realized the question itself was wrong.

He did not need to understand what 04:17 was.

He needed to understand what it had left inside him.

The great rupture had happened.

People had died. People had survived. Lives had been divided into before and after.

To reduce such suffering to a sign would diminish what had been lived.

But it was also impossible to deny the transformation suffering left behind in human beings.

Perhaps the call was not the earthquake itself.

Perhaps it was the trace left when millions of people met the same fragility at once.

Fear.

Loss.

Solidarity.

Helplessness.

Compassion.

Anger.

The will to live.

All of these possibilities had passed through humanity at the same time.

And something had remained.

Perhaps it was not energy.

At least not in the known physical sense.

Perhaps it was not a measurable field at all.

But it had left a **trace of rupture** in human consciousness.

Within HCU’s Quantum Time, that trace did not have to remain in the past.

A person could connect to the node years later.

Through a dream.

A loss.

A question.

A choice.

The time appeared again.

**04:17**

Then the digits dissolved.

One last question rose from the darkness:

**What will you make possible now?**

Maran woke.

This time he did not look at the clock.

---

As years passed, reports of 04:17 continued to emerge in different parts of the world.

Some searched for scientific explanations.

Some called it collective memory.

Some pointed to the transmission of trauma across generations.

Some called it coincidence.

Some a religious call.

Some a relation between consciousnesses not yet understood.

Others argued that people were producing similar stories because they had heard them from one another.

None of these interpretations was proved with certainty.

Perhaps one day one would be.

Perhaps never.

For the Dark Center, certainty was not the point.

The Dark Center was not the place where answers could not be found.

**It was the place where possibility had not yet been closed into a single answer.**

That was the power of 04:17.

It did not give everyone the same dream.

The same belief.

The same path.

It left only a threshold.

Some saw the threshold and turned back.

Some forgot it.

Some were frightened.

Some tried to explain it.

Some changed their lives.

A doctor made a decision postponed for years.

A mother called her child.

A scientist changed the question at the center of a research program.

A teacher truly listened, for the first time, to a student long silenced.

A man abandoned revenge.

Another began questioning every rule that had structured his life.

A child painted the dream.

There was no human figure in the picture.

No writing.

Only a vast dark field and rings spreading outward from its center.

No one had taught the child about 04:17.

But as the mother hung the painting on the wall, the digital clock in the room flickered for a moment.

**04:17**

Then it returned to normal.

No one knew whether anything similar was happening at the same moment anywhere else in the world.

Perhaps it was not.

Perhaps “the same moment” was already the wrong question.

Some calls do not travel through time.

They appear again when a person becomes ready to encounter them.

Night fell over Xerabreşkê once more.

Göbekli Tepe disappeared into the darkness.

The stones became silent.

The village became silent.

The world continued to turn.

And somewhere inside the dark, an unseen possibility remained open.

**04:17**

Not as the date of a catastrophe.

Not as an answer.

Not as a prophecy.

But as a question humanity had left for itself from within the dark:

**What else could become possible?**
