# The Clock

The House began with the clock. Before I knew the names of the people, I knew the hour. At eight, bodies appeared. At twelve, movement slowed. At five, the chairs emptied. No bell announced the beginning or the end. The clock did it silently.

I once arrived seven minutes late. Nothing had changed in the room. The work was waiting. The people were there. Yet my absence from the exact beginning seemed larger than the seven minutes themselves.

An older man told me, “The first lesson is punctuality.”

I asked, “And the second?”

He smiled. “You will learn it when you stop looking at the clock.”

Years later, I understood. We had stopped looking at the clock only after we had learned to carry it inside us.
