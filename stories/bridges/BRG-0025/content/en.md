# The Leave

People spoke about leave as though it were a small freedom.

A few days away. A permission. A space in which the house would continue without you.

The first time I asked for leave, I felt guilty.

The second time, I prepared an explanation.

The third time, I simply filled the form.

By then I had learned that absence had to be justified even when presence had never been questioned.

One person had accumulated so many unused days that everyone joked he could disappear for months.

He never did.

“Why?” I asked.

He looked surprised.

“Where would I go?”

The question stayed with me.

Perhaps the deepest form of captivity is not being unable to leave, but forgetting where you would go if you could.
