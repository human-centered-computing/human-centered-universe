# The Chair

There were chairs that nobody had assigned to anyone, yet everyone knew who belonged to them.

A chair near the door meant one thing. A chair near the supervisor meant another. A chair at the edge meant that your words rarely reached the center.

One morning I sat in a different place.

Nobody stopped me.

That was worse.

People looked at me, then looked away. The supervisor continued speaking. Yet the room had already corrected my mistake without saying a word.

At the end of the day someone quietly said, “That is not your place.”

I asked who had decided.

He pointed to the room.

“No one.”

I began to understand that institutions have rules that do not need to be written. A person can learn his position without ever being told where it is.

Years later, when I was finally given a better chair, I noticed that I no longer wanted to sit there.
