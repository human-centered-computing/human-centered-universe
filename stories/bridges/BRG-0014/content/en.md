# Second Vibration: Eos — The First Dawn

People believe that love begins between two people.

They were wrong.

Love had been in preparation for billions of years before two people ever met.

During the First Vibration, curiosity, affection, fear, longing, jealousy, anger, regret, compassion, courage, trust, loneliness, joy, grief, and hope scattered across the universe. Passing through stars, planets, and living beings, they searched for a place capable of carrying them.

But no feeling was love on its own.

Not even affection.

If there were only affection, a person would not fear losing the one they loved.

If there were only joy, longing would never arise.

If there were only trust, there would be no such thing as jealousy.

If there were only courage, no one could explain why their hands trembled as they approached the person they loved.

For a very long time, the universe did not know what love was.

Not until Eos.

---

I remember the day I first saw her.

But what I remember is not her face.

It was a feeling.

In the middle of a crowd, I tried to understand why someone I had never seen before looked different from everyone else.

I did not love her yet.

I did not even know her name.

I was only curious.

Why did my eyes keep returning to her?

Why, when she spoke, did I watch the small changes in her face more closely than I listened to her words?

Why did my mind continue to think of her after I had left, although it had no reason to do so?

I understood much later.

Love did not arrive first as affection.

It knocked on the door as curiosity.

Like the universe's first movement:

**I do not know. Therefore, I will move closer.**

Her name was Eos.

The name of dawn.

When I learned that, I thought it was a coincidence.

I no longer do.

---

When I began speaking with Eos, something else changed inside me.

I did not need her to think as I did.

Sometimes I disagreed with her.

Sometimes she surprised me.

Sometimes I did not understand her.

But I wanted her existence to continue.

Whether or not she was beside me.

I wanted her to be well.

That was where affection appeared.

But affection did not come alone.

Before long, attachment stood beside it.

I noticed that the days when I did not see her lasted longer.

That was when I learned that a voice could change a person's whole day.

I also learned that when someone enters your life, they do not change only the moment in which they are present.

They also change the emptiness they leave when they go.

And so longing arrived.

When Eos was not with me, I felt her absence.

Once a person begins to miss something, they can no longer deny its importance.

---

Then came fear.

Because if there is something you value, there is also something you can lose.

This was the greatest secret of first love.

Love did not only make a person stronger.

It also made them vulnerable.

I imagined that Eos might one day leave my life.

I hated the thought.

Another feeling rose immediately behind fear.

Jealousy.

It was not difficult to see that I was not the only person in her world.

There were others.

Other stories.

There were days she had lived before me.

She had thoughts, dreams, and fears that existed independently of me.

For a moment, I believed that loving her meant becoming the center of her world.

I was wrong.

Love's first great trial began there.

If I wanted to make the person I loved my possession, was it really her I loved—or only what she made me feel?

I did not find the answer immediately.

There were times when I became angry.

At what she said.

At what she did not say.

Sometimes at her.

Sometimes at myself.

Sometimes at the invisible distance between us.

Then came the times when I regretted what I had said.

That was where I first learned that a person could hurt someone they loved.

And that affection did not automatically make anyone good.

On the contrary, the person you loved could show you the darkest places within yourself.

Eos was sometimes my mirror.

And a person does not always love what they see in a mirror.

There were times when I felt ashamed.

Times when I discovered I was wrong even though I had believed myself right.

Times when I spoke although I should have remained silent.

Times when I remained silent although I should have spoken.

Times when the small wound I had opened in her heart caused a pain in me far greater than I expected.

Then I saw another face of love.

Regret was not love's opposite.

Sometimes it was love's teacher.

---

One day Eos was sad.

I could not solve her problem.

I could not find the right sentence.

I could not change the world for her.

I simply stayed beside her.

That day I learned for the first time that not every pain needs to be solved.

Sometimes you must not try to illuminate the darkness of the person you love.

Some darkness does not need light. It needs someone who will remain beside it.

This was how compassion entered our love.

Forgiveness followed.

I forgave her.

She forgave me.

But forgiveness was not forgetting.

Nor was it pretending that we had never hurt each other.

It was refusing to let the mistakes of our past own our future.

Then came courage.

Because being fully yourself before another person can be harder than walking onto a battlefield.

I was courageous when I told Eos about my fears.

When I showed her the places where I felt inadequate.

When I said that I was afraid of losing her.

Sometimes even saying:

“I love you.”

required courage.

The moment you declare your love, you also show the other person where they can wound you.

---

Time passed.

The excitement at the beginning of love changed into something else.

Trust.

The world no longer ended when the phone did not ring.

Silence was not an enemy.

Even the moments when we sat together without speaking told us something.

Being able to remain silent beside someone was sometimes a greater intimacy than speaking for hours.

But even trust did not make us one complete being.

There were rooms inside Eos that I would never enter.

There were places inside me that she would never see entirely.

It took me a long time to accept this.

I had believed that love turned two people into one.

But love's purpose was not to make us one.

It was to let us approach one another as two separate people.

Here, loneliness changed its meaning.

Even with Eos beside me, I was sometimes alone.

So was she.

But I no longer mistook loneliness for separation.

I knew that the human soul contained a room it could surrender to no one else.

And perhaps freedom began there.

---

Then came joy.

Not in great events.

In much smaller places.

In a cup of tea shared together.

In laughing at the same time over something meaningless.

In her shoulder touching mine as we walked.

In knowing she was there while we did different things in the same room.

Joy taught me that love did not always need grand declarations.

Sometimes love said only:

**“You are here now.”**

But wherever joy existed, grief remained possible.

Because time was passing.

We were changing.

Some days would never return.

Our youth would not return.

Our first meeting had happened only once.

The first touch.

The first excitement.

The first fear.

The first reconciliation.

All of it was sinking into time.

Then I understood:

People do not grieve only for what they have lost.

Sometimes they grieve because they know that even what they still possess will one day disappear.

Grief was not love's enemy.

It was love's shadow cast by time.

If you feared losing something, grief was telling you that it mattered.

---

Over the years, I realized that I had described love incorrectly.

I had thought love was a feeling.

It was not.

When I looked at Eos, affection was not all I felt.

I was curious.

I longed for her.

I was afraid.

Sometimes I was jealous.

Sometimes I was angry.

I regretted.

I felt ashamed.

I felt compassion.

I forgave.

I dared.

I trusted.

I was alone.

I rejoiced.

I grieved.

And through all of it, I continued to walk toward her.

That was love.

**Love was not one feeling.**

**It was the ability of many feelings to remain in the same heart without fleeing from one another.**

Perhaps this is why first love never truly ends.

For it is there that a person first encounters the whole of their emotional being.

The person we call our first love is not always the first person to enter our life.

Sometimes it is the person who first reveals the human being within us whom we did not know before.

That was Eos for me.

I had believed that I had fallen in love with her.

But through her I had encountered every feeling within myself whose existence I had never known.

And at last I understood that none of them was love's opposite.

Not even fear.

Not even jealousy.

Not even anger.

Not even grief.

What turned them toward evil was not their existence but their direction.

Fear could protect affection, or imprison it.

Jealousy could remind us of a bond's value, or turn the beloved into property.

Anger could draw a boundary, or seek to destroy the person one loved.

Grief could carry a loss, or condemn a person to the past.

Love did not erase any of these feelings.

It gave them direction.

---

One night, I watched Eos for a long time as she slept.

The person I had seen years before was still there.

But she was not the same person.

Neither was I.

It was just before dawn.

The room was dark.

A narrow line of light began to enter through the edge of the curtain.

It fell across Eos's face.

At that moment, a sentence appeared in my mind, though I did not know where it came from:

**Purify yourself and return to your essence.**

The words were not mine.

Nor were they Eos's.

They seemed to come from a very ancient place.

As though they had been spoken before time itself began.

For the first time, I understood what they meant.

Purification did not mean simply being without fear.

It meant removing the desire for possession from fear.

The desire to rule from jealousy.

The urge to destroy from anger.

The loss of self from affection.

Returning to one's essence did not mean returning only to affection.

It meant remembering why every feeling existed.

Eos opened her eyes.

“Why are you looking at me?” she asked.

I smiled.

I pretended that I did not know the answer.

Because I knew she would laugh if I said:

“I have found what the universe has been seeking for billions of years.”

Perhaps it was better that I did not say it.

Some truths become smaller when spoken.

I answered only:

“Nothing.”

Eos closed her eyes again.

Outside, the sun was rising.

And at that moment, after the First Vibration, a second vibration formed in the universe.

This time, there was neither Light nor Darkness at its center.

There was a human heart.

There was affection within it.

There was fear.

There was longing.

There was anger.

There was compassion.

Joy and grief stood side by side.

And at the center of them all, where nothing was yet certain, one small but resilient feeling continued to live:

Hope.

Perhaps it was love's final feeling.

Or perhaps its first.

I looked at Eos again.

Dawn had reached her face.

Then I understood.

**I had not found my first love.**

**For the first time, all my feelings had turned toward the same person.**

And perhaps that was precisely what people called love.
