# Shahmaran: Between Poison and Healing

Kawa did not smile when he saw the map. He served the excavation as an adviser on local memory. He held no university title, but he knew which stone was known by which name in which village, and which lament had been sung after which migration.

“Seven paths,” he said. “You heard this before. You thought it was only a story.”

That evening, around the fire, he told them of Shahmaran. In his version, the snakes had withdrawn underground not because they were punished, but because they protected knowledge that humans were not yet ready to carry. Shahmaran’s body marked a boundary between two worlds: below, the memory of the earth; above, human responsibility.

“How did Shahmaran know healing?” Elena asked.

“Because she knew poison,” Kawa replied. “Whoever searches for goodness only inside the good loses half of the medicine.”

Maran wrote the sentence down.

Kawa continued. In a time far away, a light falling from the sky had called both the snakes beneath the earth and the people above it to the same place. Together they built a bowl-shaped structure. The stones did not send light back to the sky; they gathered scattered light into the center. As humans and snakes carried one another’s knowledge, they became a new community called the Maran.

Then people began to possess knowledge instead of sharing it. They feared that the one who could heal might be greater than themselves. To prevent war, Shahmaran took the Maran down through seven layers of the earth. She left the protection of the temple to humans. Humans buried what they were afraid to protect.

“The story does not end there,” Kawa said. “One day a person falls underground. Shahmaran trusts him and gives him her knowledge. He cannot keep his promise. Shahmaran is killed; yet her body becomes both poison and medicine. Even those who killed her continue living through the goodness inside her.”

“What does that have to do with the excavation?” someone asked.

Kawa threw another branch into the fire.

“Perhaps nothing. Perhaps what you are excavating is not a room, but a promise.”

That night Maran could not sleep in his tent. At 03:17 his laptop turned on by itself. On the screen was the combined three-dimensional model of the seven underground zones. The file did not exist on the excavation network. The missing node at the center of the stone topology they had built during the day had been enlarged. Within it appeared seven narrow paths and a folded structure resembling a human brain.

Maran rotated the model. Part of the surface contained marks he could not read. When he increased the contrast to improve the image, the marks disappeared. When he reduced it again, they returned.

It was as though darkness was not hiding information, but protecting it from too much light.

When he moved closer to the center of the model, an audio file opened. The recording contained a sentence from the story Kawa had just told. But the file creation date was nine years earlier than the telling.

“Whoever searches for goodness only inside the good loses half of the medicine.”

In the morning, Maran played the recording for Elena. She remained silent for a long time.

“Kawa may have recorded this before,” she said at last.

“The file was created nine years ago. Kawa has never seen this computer.”

“Dates can be altered.”

“Then how did the file get here?”

Elena looked toward the void beneath the ground.

“That is what we are here to find out. But first we have to decide what we are trying to prove. People notice evidence that resembles the answer they are already looking for more easily.”
