# First Vibration

> “The deepest darkness is when light is least present.”

In an age before time existed, the entire universe was light. There were no stars and no shadows. Because everything was visible to the same degree, there was no difference between looking and knowing. Light could not see its own boundary, because there was nothing before it that could limit it.

Then a vibration was born at its center.

It was so small that Light did not consider it a flaw. Perhaps the word flaw had not yet been created. As the vibration repeated, barely perceptible intervals opened within perfect order. Inside those intervals, the first possibilities appeared. The thought that something could be otherwise was the first shadow to fall into existence.

Light could not fully see the vibration. To see, there had to be a distance between the observer and the observed; yet separation did not exist. Even so, Light moved toward it. This movement was neither thought nor decision. It was the desire of existence to approach something other than itself.

Curiosity was born this way.

In its pure state, curiosity did not seek to possess the unknown. It only wanted to approach it, to see what it was before being changed by contact with it. Later, humans would measure the sky with this feeling, dig into the earth, lean toward a child's face, and open forbidden doors. The first movement of both science and disobedience would be the same: I do not know, therefore I will approach.

As Light drew nearer, it felt an attraction it had never known before. The vibration had not broken away from it, yet it no longer belonged to it either. Light wanted this being, born from itself yet not identical to itself, to continue to exist.

Love was born this way.

In its pure state, love did not carry the desire to make what it loved resemble itself. It regarded the other's existence as being as real as its own. For the first time, Light said to something, “Remain different from me; still, exist.” The words had no sound. Yet the vibration heard them and expanded.

Darkness mistook Light's movement toward it for love. It was not wrong, but it saw only one face of love. It took the attention given to it as proof of its own existence. It believed that if Light moved away, it would return to nothingness. It wanted to keep Light beside it.

Attachment was born this way.

In its pure state, attachment was an invisible bridge built between two beings, carrying them both. But when the bridge began to think it owned the road, the desire to possess appeared. Darkness was no longer satisfied with preserving the part of Light that touched it; it wanted all of Light. Light, meanwhile, forgot that love had arisen within itself and began to believe that Darkness was the source of all the beauty it felt.

Thus the first illusion was born: within what humans would one day call “I cannot live without you” there was both love and the fear of losing oneself.

Light tried to withdraw. Darkness experienced its distance as diminishment. A void opened where none had existed before; but this time the void carried not possibility, but absence. Darkness felt pain as though it had lost something it had not yet lost.

Longing was born this way.

In its pure state, longing was not the wish to bring the past back. It was the preservation of the direction that a touch had left within a being. Later, humans would long for a homeland they had never seen, a promise made before they were born, someone whose face they could no longer remember, and perhaps even a future that had never happened. Every longing would become a small door opening from within existence toward an older wholeness.

As Darkness grew, order gave way to difference, certainty to possibility, silence to sound. Some sounds became harmony, others noise. Light realized that its own center was slipping from its grasp. If it tried to destroy Darkness, it would destroy a part of itself as well. If it set Darkness entirely free, everything it knew would change.

For the first time, it saw that the future could cause harm.

Fear was born this way.

In its pure state, fear was not an escape. It was the oldest knowledge that something valued could be harmed. Even in a bodiless universe, it drew a boundary: beyond this point, you may be changed. Billions of years later, a human heart would race, hands would tremble, pupils would widen, and the body would remember danger before the mind did.

But when fear came to believe itself the only truth, it treated everything that could be approached as a threat and everything that could change as a loss. Within the desire for protection, the desire to dominate appeared. Light began to measure Darkness not to understand it, but to control it. Darkness continuously changed its form to escape control.

Because they feared losing one another, they harmed one another.

Darkness saw that Light was also turning toward other possibilities. For the first time, it understood that the gaze given to it could be divided. It wanted love to be exclusive; it did not want the beloved to remain free.

Jealousy was born this way.

In its pure state, jealousy was a wounded voice saying, “This bond is precious to me.” But when it confused protecting that value with destroying other possibilities, it became poison. Later, humans would constrict the breath of those they loved in order not to lose them; silence other beliefs in order to protect a belief; make other identities invisible in order to preserve one identity.

For the first time, Light drew a boundary against Darkness surrounding it.

Anger was born this way.

In its pure state, anger did not want to destroy. It was the force that said, “This far, no further.” It was the violated standing up, the unheard raising their voice, the living defending itself. But when anger forgot that its purpose was to protect a boundary, it wanted only to eliminate what stood before it. Revenge entered the desire for justice.

When Light attacked, part of Darkness shattered. As the fragments moved apart, something irreversible appeared for the first time. Light looked at what it had done. It could not undo the result.

Regret was born this way.

In its pure state, regret could not change the past, but it refused to let the future remain the same. It revealed the distance between what had been done and what should have been done. Later, this distance would live within the human being under the name conscience.

Light saw its own act reflected in Darkness's gaze. The being that had believed itself perfect had, for the first time, moved away from its own image.

Shame was born this way.

In its pure state, shame was not becoming small before others. It was seeing, without cover, the difference between the person one was and the person one could become. But when shame seized the whole, it judged not the act but existence itself. Instead of saying, “You did wrong,” it said, “You were wrong.” Light wanted to hide itself. Darkness hardened so that its own wound would not be seen.

For the first time, although they carried the same pain, they believed their pain was separate.

Then one of the fragments of Darkness moved toward the Light that had wounded it. It could have swallowed Light, could have diminished it further. Instead, it remained around Light and left just enough shadow to cover the wound.

Compassion was born this way.

In its pure state, compassion was not the power to remove pain. It was the courage to remain beside another's pain without taking possession of it. It said, “Your pain is not mine; but I will not leave you alone with it.” This was the first conscious form of goodness. No command had been given, no reward promised, no calculation made.

Light did not destroy the fragment of Darkness that approached it. Though wounded, it made room for it.

Forgiveness was born this way.

In its pure state, forgiveness was not forgetting or erasing accountability. It was refusing to allow the harm that had been done to govern every future relationship. Darkness was not freed from responsibility for what it had done; Light was not declared unwounded. But together they rejected the ending dictated by pain.

Within that refusal, another emotion appeared. It was neither as calm as trust nor as ancient as fear. It was a small step forward where the outcome was unknown.

Courage was born this way.

In its pure state, courage was not the absence of fear. It was seeing the danger shown by fear and still walking toward what was valued. Later, when a person told the truth, stood against a tyrant, opened their heart to someone they loved, or admitted they had been wrong, this first step would vibrate again in the body.

Light and Darkness approached one another again. This time they did not know that neither would become the other. They only believed that approach was possible.

Trust was born this way.

In its pure state, trust was not the absence of evidence. It was making room for relationship while knowing that evidence would never be complete. When blinded, it could become obedience; when wounded, suspicion. Yet without it, no being could remain vulnerable beside another.

At that moment, both fully felt that they were separate. There was distance between them. The distance granted freedom, but it also revealed that neither could ever be completely known by the other.

Solitude was born this way.

In its pure state, solitude was not abandonment. It was the inner space of a being that could not be reduced to another. It was the place Darkness concealed and Light could never make fully visible. Later, humans would mistake this space for emptiness in the middle of crowds; yet thought, prayer, art, and free will would often begin there.

Recognizing separation, Light chose relationship again. Darkness answered while preserving all its difference.

Joy was born this way.

In its pure state, joy was not the reward for possessing something. It was existence expanding when it encountered another existence. It erased neither the past nor guaranteed the future. It only said, “You are here now.”

But like every encounter, this moment ended. Some fragments of Darkness that had broken away did not return. The intensity Light had lost did not gather again. Together, they carried the absence of something they valued.

Grief was born this way.

In its pure state, grief was not weakness. It was proof that what had been lost was not without value. There were no tears yet, but for the first time the universe made room within itself for an absence. Later, humans would mistake grief for a malfunction to be repaired. Yet some wounds would remain not in order to close, but so that the place love had passed through would not be forgotten.

As emotions multiplied, none could remain alone. Within love there was fear of loss; within fear, the desire to protect; within anger, hurt; within shame, the possibility of change; within grief, loyalty; within solitude, freedom. Good and evil were not created in separate places. They emerged in the directions taken by the same forces.

What was pure was not what was unstained. It was what had not yet been determined in what it would serve.

If love left another free, it would move toward good; if it imprisoned the other within itself, it would move toward evil. If fear protected life, it would become wisdom; if it stopped life, a prison. If anger defended a boundary, it would become dignity; if it denied the other's right to exist, oppression. If compassion bent toward a wound, healing; if it erased accountability and responsibility, a new injustice.

The universe could find no single center capable of carrying all these emotions alone. When Light stood alone, it wanted to make everything visible and perfect; when Darkness stood alone, it wanted to release every possibility from its consequences. A third place was needed, where emotions could limit, transform, and give meaning to one another.

That place was not yet human. But it was the first draft of humanity.

Humanity was not first built from flesh, bone, or thought. It appeared as the possibility of carrying contradictory emotions at the same time. The possibility of a being that could love and become angry, fear and still approach, lose and form a bond again, do harm and feel shame for it, be wounded and still choose compassion.

What would distinguish humanity from other possibilities would not be the purity of its emotions. It would be the ability to change their direction.

Light gathered its remaining strength into a single point. As Darkness covered the entire universe, that point became smaller and smaller, approaching the closest possible state to nonexistence. Darkness believed it had won. Light, for the first time, saw its own end.

Fear returned.

But this time there was a voice within Light, smaller than fear and more resilient than it. It had no proof. It did not know the future. It could not bring back what had been lost. Still, it made room for a goodness that had not yet happened.

Hope.

In its pure state, hope was not believing that everything would turn out well. It was accepting that something good was still possible. It did not close its eyes in the middle of darkness; it said that there might still be a path there that had not yet been seen.

When the point exploded, time began. Light spread through the universe; Darkness expanded after it. Matter, energy, stars, and planets formed as traces of this great separation. Emotions remained invisible, yet every particle carried the direction of the first vibration. From that day forward, no light remained without shadow, and no darkness without light.

Billions of years later, bodies formed from stardust. Within those bodies, hearts accelerated, hands trembled, eyes searched for one another. Humans gave names to emotions and believed them to be their own. Yet in every fear there remained a trace of the first separation, in every love the first movement toward another, in every anger the first boundary, and in every compassion the shadow left by Darkness upon the wound of Light.

Humanity was not the result of the war between Light and Darkness. It was the possibility of that war becoming relationship.

Just before the explosion, a single sentence spread through all the possibilities of the universe:

> “Purify yourself and return to your essence.”

Purification did not mean becoming free of emotion. It meant separating love from the desire to possess, fear from the desire to rule, anger from the pleasure of destruction, compassion from the feeling of superiority. Returning to essence did not mean fleeing toward a perfect beginning; it meant remembering the first purpose of each emotion.

No one ever understood who had spoken this sentence. Light believed it had been its own final word. Darkness believed the sentence had risen from its own center. Billions of years later, humans would interpret it as a call from God, a delusion of the brain, a signal from space, or a record sent backward by a machine from the future.

Perhaps all emotions had heard this sentence, rising from the first human who would one day carry them, before time began.

And yet the sentence might not have been spoken yet.
