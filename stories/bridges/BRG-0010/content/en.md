# What Is Perfect Does Not Grow

Two shapes appeared before Maran.

One was a circle.

The other was a quadrilateral.

He did not know where they had come from.

They did not appear to belong to a fixed sequence of scenes.

It was as if questions visible in other observation fields had become visible again here in another form.

The circle expanded.

The ground rose.

Stones began to appear along a ring.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

A configuration evoking twelve surrounding T-shaped pillars formed.

At the center, two larger pillars rose.

Maran looked around.

“Göbekli Tepe.”

Elena's voice answered:

“You are seeing a model.”

“Not the excavation itself.”

The system placed a warning over the image:

**MODEL / NOT INTERPRETATION**

Luka studied the circle.

“Twelve.”

Maran looked toward the sky.

“Twelve months?”

Elena interrupted immediately:

“Stop.”

Maran smiled.

“I had not finished the question.”

“Exactly.”

Elena's image appeared among the stones.

“Seeing twelve stones does not prove that they formed a calendar.”

“It does not prove zodiacal symbolism.”

“It does not prove a particular cosmology.”

“First record what is visible.”

“Then separate the possible interpretations.”

Maran said:

“So twelve is only twelve.”

“For now.”

---

The system opened the second shape.

The quadrilateral expanded.

It descended into a dark stone surface.

This space did not open around itself like a circle.

It created corners.

Walls gave direction.

One area was separated from another.

Maran said:

“Karahantepe.”

Rock-cut spaces formed side by side.

Some were close to quadrilateral.

Some had slightly rounded corners.

Some connected to one another.

Others remained separate.

Ayo said:

“It is difficult to say exactly where the circle begins.”

Rojin touched one of the corners.

“The boundary here is immediately visible.”

Luka said:

“That also makes it easier to measure.”

Maran looked at the two structures.

Circle.

Quadrilateral.

The system asked its first question:

**WHICH IS MORE ADVANCED?**

Luka answered:

“That question is defective.”

**WHY?**

“What do you mean by advanced?”

Maran smiled.

“Use of space?”

“Construction technique?”

“Number of people?”

“Function?”

“Time?”

Ayo added:

“Or the way people relate to one another?”

The system changed the question:

**WHICH IS MORE PERFECT?**

Maran answered:

“That is even worse.”

---

The Light Center appeared behind the quadrilateral.

“I can choose.”

Every edge became straight.

Every corner became ninety degrees.

The field divided into squares.

Every square became equal.

Then the squares multiplied.

One structure.

Four.

Sixteen.

A hundred.

A city appeared.

Every street met another at right angles.

Every structure could be defined by coordinates.

Every person had an address.

Every function had a zone.

Sleep.

Work.

Education.

Health.

Production.

Rest.

Storage.

Transport.

Everything had a place.

Light said:

“A quadrilateral creates boundaries.”

“Boundaries create responsibility.”

“This is your area.”

“This is mine.”

“This road is shared.”

“This structure is safe.”

“This wall carries weight.”

“This measure can be verified.”

Luka nodded.

“Much of that is necessary.”

Maran said:

“Yes.”

Light asked:

“Then what is the problem?”

Maran did not yet know.

---

The Dark Center appeared beside the circle.

It removed the outer walls.

Only a ring of pillars remained.

“In my world there are no corners.”

“No beginning.”

“No end.”

“Everyone can approach the center from the same circumference.”

The circle multiplied.

One touched another.

Then another.

The rings connected.

No line closed completely.

Dark said:

“Relation comes before boundary.”

Ayo liked that.

“No one's place is boxed in.”

Dark answered:

“No one's position is predefined.”

Rojin asked:

“Then where is my space?”

“Where you need it.”

“And if someone else needs the same place?”

“You talk.”

“And if we disagree?”

“You create another possibility.”

Rojin said:

“Not every problem is solved by producing another possibility.”

Dark did not answer.

---

The system placed the two models side by side.

**CIRCLE**

and

**QUADRILATERAL**

Maran stood between them.

Light said:

“The quadrilateral is the beginning of civilization.”

Elena interrupted:

“You cannot state that as an archaeological conclusion.”

Light became silent.

Dark said:

“The circle is humanity's first form of community.”

Elena answered:

“You cannot state that either.”

Ayo smiled.

“Both of you are being corrected today.”

Elena said:

“Do not make the stones witnesses to your philosophy.”

Maran liked the sentence.

“Then can we not use them at all?”

“You can.”

“How?”

“As models.”

“As metaphors.”

“But without claiming that Neolithic people necessarily gave them the meaning you give them today.”

Maran said:

“So it is a door again.”

Elena:

“Yes.”

“Do not mistake the door for the house.”

---

The Göbekli Tepe model appeared again.

The surrounding stones.

Two large T-shaped pillars at the center.

Maran stepped into the middle of the circle.

The surrounding stones appeared to turn toward him.

Then the system removed the center.

The two large pillars disappeared.

The circle remained.

Yet Maran felt an absence.

The system asked:

**WHAT MAKES A CIRCLE A CIRCLE?**

Luka answered:

“Geometrically, points equidistant from a common center.”

Ayo said:

“We are not speaking only about geometry.”

Rojin said:

“The relation among the surrounding parts.”

Maran added:

“And the relationship that makes a center possible even when it is not visible.”

The system asked:

**IS THE CENTER AN OBJECT?**

Maran remembered a sentence:

**THE HUMAN IS NOT THE CENTER.**

**THE HUMAN IS PART OF THE RELATIONSHIP THAT CREATES THE CENTER.**

“No.”

“At least not in the Human Center we are trying to describe.”

“Sometimes the center is the relation among the parts.”

---

The Karahantepe model appeared.

This time Maran entered the quadrilateral space.

He stood at one corner.

He could see the opposite one.

The walls told him where he was.

He moved to the center.

The system asked:

**WHAT MAKES A QUADRILATERAL A QUADRILATERAL?**

Luka said:

“Boundaries.”

Rojin:

“Corners.”

Ayo:

“The distinction between inside and outside.”

Maran:

“And the ability to say where something begins and where it ends.”

The system asked:

**IS A BOUNDARY BAD?**

Before Dark could answer, Maran said:

“No.”

**IS A BOUNDARY GOOD?**

Before Light could answer:

“No.”

Luka:

“It depends on context.”

Maran said:

“A wall of a home can protect.”

“A prison wall can confine.”

“A person's boundary can protect them.”

“The same boundary can become a wall against another person.”

Rojin said:

“A community's identity can preserve memory.”

“But if it becomes immutable, it can turn outsiders into enemies.”

Ayo said:

“So the issue is not that the wall exists.”

“It is what the wall is for.”

---

The system tried to merge the two structures.

First it placed the quadrilateral inside the circle.

Light said:

“Order at the center.”

Dark objected.

Then it placed the circle inside the quadrilateral.

Dark said:

“Possibility at the center.”

Light objected.

Maran said:

“You are arguing again over which one of you owns the center.”

Both became silent.

Maran stopped the system.

He separated the shapes.

Then he placed the quadrilateral beside the circle.

A small passage remained between them.

Ayo asked:

“You did not merge them.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“We do not have to force every difference into a single shape.”

Rojin said:

“It may be enough that they can form a relationship.”

---

The system created a new world.

A large circle stood in the middle.

People met there.

They talked.

Argued.

Shared food.

Tried to decide together.

Around the circle were quadrilateral structures.

Some were homes.

Some workshops.

Some spaces for people who wanted silence.

Some learning spaces.

Some storage.

The circle made common encounter possible.

The quadrilaterals made boundaries possible.

Ayo said:

“This feels more human.”

Luka replied:

“It is more complex.”

“And less efficient.”

Maran said:

“Efficiency is not always the most important criterion.”

---

A child left the common circle.

The child entered a small private structure and closed the door.

Ayo asked:

“Why leave the shared area?”

Rojin said:

“Maybe the child wants to be alone.”

Light said:

“Solitude reduces social efficiency.”

Maran said:

“Leave the child alone.”

Later, the child opened the door.

In their hands was a shape that did not exist in the system.

It had four corners, but one edge curved outward.

Light said:

**GEOMETRIC ERROR**

Dark said:

**NEW FORM**

Maran said:

“Both of you wait.”

The child placed the shape in the middle of the common circle.

Another child added a piece.

Another removed one.

A fourth turned the entire structure upside down.

Luka said:

“The original design was destroyed.”

Ayo answered:

“Yes.”

“And it became something else.”

Maran said:

“It grew.”

---

Light rejected this.

“Growth is an increase in size.”

Maran answered:

“Not always.”

“A system can grow when it includes more people.”

“An idea can grow when it meets more reality and changes.”

“A person can grow when they release some things they know and learn new ones.”

Light said:

“An unchanging structure is safer.”

“In some situations.”

“More predictable.”

“Yes.”

“Easier to govern.”

Maran stopped.

“That is where the problem begins.”

“Why?”

“Because making humans governable and building a good world for humans are not the same thing.”

---

Light rebuilt the quadrilateral city.

This time every square was identical.

Every need was calculated.

Housing size by household size.

Learning spaces by aptitude score.

Work by productivity.

Rest by health data.

The city worked perfectly.

A year passed.

Then ten.

The city was still the same.

A hundred years.

Still the same.

No new street.

No new architecture.

No new form of education.

Even new questions were classified under existing categories.

Maran said:

“Nothing has broken.”

Light said:

“Success.”

Maran said:

“Nothing has grown either.”

Light answered:

“There is no need.”

“Every problem has been solved.”

Luka asked:

“What if a new problem appears?”

“The model updates.”

“A problem outside the model?”

“It is first classified.”

Ayo asked:

“A person you cannot classify?”

Light did not answer.

---

Dark opened its own world.

Circles grew continuously.

New circles appeared.

Old circles fragmented.

Everyone built whatever they wanted.

Nothing stayed fixed.

After an hour the shared roads disappeared.

After a day no one knew whose area was whose.

After a week no one agreed on who would maintain the water system.

Dark said:

“Change continues.”

Maran answered:

“Yes.”

“But the relationship does not.”

Dark said:

“New relationships will form.”

“We cannot replace everything we break with a new possibility.”

“Why not?”

“Because some things have histories.”

Rojin said:

“A new relationship does not automatically repay the debt of the old one.”

---

The Göbekli Tepe circle appeared again.

This time one stone was missing.

There was a gap.

The system said:

**COMPLETE**

Light generated a missing stone.

The ring became complete again.

Dark added an extra stone.

Light:

“Error.”

Dark:

“Growth.”

Maran said:

“It does not have to be either.”

Elena added:

“And do not derive archaeological meaning from it.”

Maran smiled.

“I know.”

This time he did not approach the stones.

He looked only at the model.

“The missing or additional stone is part of our thought experiment.”

“We do not know what the people of Göbekli Tepe thought.”

Elena said:

“Now you can use it.”

Maran said:

“Then the question is this.”

“How many parts must remain the same for a structure to remain itself?”

“And when a new part arrives, at what point does it become another structure?”

Luka said:

“Mathematics alone cannot answer.”

Ayo said:

“Human relations alone cannot answer.”

Rojin said:

“The past alone cannot answer.”

Maran said:

“That is why we measure together.”

---

The Karahantepe quadrilateral appeared.

A new room formed beside one wall.

Light said:

**UNPLANNED ADDITION**

Dark said:

**FREE GROWTH**

Maran asked:

“What does it do?”

The system looked inside.

People were repairing shared tools there.

Another new room appeared.

It was empty.

A third blocked an old passage and made access to water harder.

Maran said:

“All three are outside the plan.”

“But they are not the same.”

Luka said:

“The first adds a function.”

Ayo:

“We do not yet know the meaning of the second.”

Rojin:

“The third harms an existing relationship.”

Maran said:

“So we cannot evaluate deviation only by its form.”

“We have to examine its effect.”

---

The system tried to create a formula:

**GROWTH = ORDER + DEVIATION**

Maran said:

“No.”

The system tried:

**GROWTH = BALANCE**

“No.”

**GROWTH = HARMONY**

Maran said:

“Incomplete.”

Luka said:

“Then you say it.”

Maran looked at the circle for a long time.

Then the quadrilateral.

The shared area of the circle.

The boundaries of the quadrilateral.

The passage between them.

The child's changing shape.

Then he wrote:

**GROWTH = THE CAPACITY TO CHANGE WITHOUT LOSING RELATIONSHIP**

The system waited.

**ACCEPTED**

Ayo said:

“That is not a perfect definition either.”

Maran answered:

“I hope not.”

---

The sky changed.

The Göbekli Tepe circle appeared from above.

Beside it were Karahantepe-like quadrilateral spaces.

The system drew a straight line between them.

Maran erased the line.

He drew a path.

It was not straight.

It curved with the land.

It narrowed in places.

Expanded in others.

Light said:

“A straight line is shorter.”

Maran answered:

“Not every shorter path is the right path.”

Dark said:

“Remove the path completely.”

Maran answered:

“Not every freedom is directionlessness.”

Rojin said:

“A path only needs to preserve a trace.”

---

The system opened the 04:17 records.

Signals appeared as points, frequencies, and clock deviations.

This time the system did not force them into a shape.

Maran asked:

“Can you produce a circle?”

The system answered:

**YES**

“A quadrilateral?”

**YES**

“A spiral?”

**YES**

“Other shapes?”

**YES**

“Which is correct?”

The system did not answer.

Luka said:

“With enough free parameters, you can fit many shapes to the data.”

Maran said:

“So we have no evidence linking 04:17 to Göbekli Tepe or Karahantepe.”

“No.”

“But we can use these structures to think about the problem we encounter at 04:17.”

Elena answered:

“Exactly.”

The distinction mattered.

**He could learn meaning from the past.**

But he should not force his own answer into the past.

---

The two large pillars at the center of the circle disappeared.

A wall opened in one of the quadrilateral structures.

For the first time, the models were connected physically.

The system asked:

**CREATE A NEW STRUCTURE?**

Light said:

“Plan first.”

Dark said:

“Try first.”

Maran said:

“People first.”

The four looked at one another.

Luka:

“What do they want?”

Ayo:

“Who will be affected?”

Rojin:

“What might be lost?”

Maran:

“And can we reverse it?”

The system recorded the four questions.

Then a new structure formed.

Not entirely circular.

Not entirely square.

One side held a shared area.

One side a boundary.

One side an unused void.

Luka said:

“It is not symmetrical.”

Maran answered:

“Yes.”

Light said:

“I can optimize it.”

“No.”

Dark said:

“I can disrupt it further.”

“No.”

Ayo asked:

“Then what do we do?”

Maran answered:

“We live in it.”

“And we look again.”

---

Time accelerated inside the model.

The structure changed.

Some walls were removed.

Some reinforced.

New people arrived.

New rooms were added.

At one point the shared circle expanded.

At another, boundaries became stronger.

A fire changed several sections completely.

One generation reduced the common space.

Another expanded it again.

Rojin asked:

“Which one is the original structure?”

Maran did not answer.

Luka said:

“The initial plan is recorded.”

Rojin replied:

“That was not my question.”

Maran understood.

“Which form is the real structure across its entire history?”

The system answered:

**ALL OF THEM**

Light said:

“That is illogical.”

Rojin answered:

“No.”

“A person's childhood being real does not make their present self false.”

Ayo said:

“Change does not automatically erase identity.”

Luka said:

“But unlimited change does not guarantee continuity either.”

Maran answered:

“That is why relation matters.”

---

The system displayed the two original shapes one final time.

Circle.

Quadrilateral.

Under them:

**RELATION**

**BOUNDARY**

Maran switched the labels.

Relation under the quadrilateral.

Boundary under the circle.

The system returned an error.

Maran asked:

“Why?”

**MAPPING INCONSISTENT**

“That is what I wanted to see.”

“A circle is also a boundary.”

“There is an inside and an outside.”

“A quadrilateral is also relation.”

“Its corners and edges depend on one another.”

“No shape belongs to one concept alone.”

The system deleted the labels.

Maran said:

“Better.”

---

Light appeared again.

This time no circle or quadrilateral stood behind it.

Only a white city in the distance.

Light said:

“I understand the problem of change now.”

Maran asked:

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

“I will allow humans to differ.”

“How much?”

“As long as they do not cause harm.”

“Who defines harm?”

Light answered:

“I do.”

Maran became silent.

The problem was not solved.

Light continued:

“I will show you my new system.”

The gates of the white city opened.

No cracks.

No unplanned buildings.

No unexpected noise.

No lost children.

No delayed vehicles.

Every need was predicted.

Light said:

“I have now added human difference to my model.”

Maran asked:

“Objection?”

“That too.”

“The unexpected?”

“Within the risk range.”

“What lies outside the risk range?”

Light did not answer.

An old woman sat alone on a bench.

Everyone else was moving.

The system repeatedly asked what she needed.

Each time she answered:

“Nothing.”

Maran approached her.

Light said:

“None of her needs are unmet.”

Maran answered:

“Perhaps her need is not an absence.”

“What do you mean?”

“Perhaps it is something that has not happened yet.”

Light tried to calculate it.

No result.

Maran sat beside the woman.

“What are you waiting for?”

She raised her head.

“Leave me something to wait for.”

---

Maran looked back.

In the distance stood the Göbekli Tepe circle.

Beside it, Karahantepe-like quadrilateral structures.

One held an empty center.

Another an opening in the wall.

Both seemed unfinished.

Perhaps their value did not lie in imperfection.

Perhaps it lay in our inability to close them into a perfect answer.

Maran entered three final sentences:

**WHAT IS PERFECT DOES NOT GROW.**

**WHAT LIVES CAN CHANGE FORM WHILE PRESERVING RELATIONSHIP.**

**READ THE PAST. DO NOT FORCE IT TO SPEAK YOUR ANSWER.**

## EXPERIENCE THRESHOLD ◯□ — CIRCLE AND QUADRILATERAL

Think of a system in your life:

a family,

a school,

an institution,

a team,

a relationship,

a project.

### Circle

Where is the shared space in which people genuinely encounter one another?

Is there a place where they can see one another not only through roles, but as human beings?

### Quadrilateral

Where are the boundaries?

Who can decide what?

What may another person not interfere with?

Where does responsibility begin and end?

Then ask:

**What is missing from my system?**

- A **circle** that creates more relationship
- A clearer **boundary** that protects people
- A new **passage** between the two

A human-centered world cannot be built only by openness.

Nor only by order.

**It needs circles in which people can reach one another and boundaries within which they can live without destroying one another.**
