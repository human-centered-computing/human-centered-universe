# What Question Do You Use to Measure Reality?

Only one command remained above the door:

**MEASURE THE SOURCE**

Maran moved his hand toward the screen.

He stopped before touching it.

“How?”

The system did not answer.

Luka spoke immediately:

“If you want to measure something, first you have to define what you are measuring.”

Ayo said:

“The source of the call.”

Luka shook his head.

“That is not a definition.”

“How is it not?”

“When you say source, what are you asking about? The first physical signal? The mind that produced the message? The effect it had on people? The first moment it appeared?”

Rojin added:

“Or why people remember it?”

The four fell silent.

Four different questions had formed around the same sentence.

At that moment Maran realized that the problem was larger than the source of the call.

**Before measuring something, you have already drawn the first boundary around what you are prepared to treat as real.**

The screen divided into four parts.

A single question appeared beneath each.

Luka:

**WHERE DID THE SIGNAL COME FROM?**

Ayo:

**WHAT DID THE SIGNAL CHANGE IN PEOPLE?**

Rojin:

**WHAT EXISTED BEFORE THE SIGNAL, AND WHAT WAS REMEMBERED AFTERWARD?**

Maran:

**WHAT REMAINS UNCHANGED ACROSS ALL THESE MEASUREMENTS?**

The system wrote:

**FOUR MEASUREMENTS INITIATED.**

---

Luka's field went dark.

Raw records from one hundred and nineteen devices opened before him.

Time:

**04:17**

Signal durations.

Frequency spectra.

Processor logs.

Network connections.

Electromagnetic field readings.

Clock drift.

Sensor errors.

Software versions.

Luka shut down every layer of interpretation.

“No dreams.”

“No belief.”

“No meaning.”

“Only what can be measured.”

He processed the data again.

Not all one hundred and nineteen devices contained the same audio recording.

Some showed only a brief clock synchronization deviation.

Some contained electromagnetic noise.

Some showed no physical signal at all.

But thirty-seven devices contained a very brief similar pattern.

Luka focused on it.

The system asked:

**CONCLUSION?**

Luka wrote:

**A COMMON PHYSICAL EVENT MAY HAVE OCCURRED.**

Then he stopped.

He changed the sentence:

**A PATTERN THAT APPEARS SIMILAR EXISTS IN SOME DEVICES. THE SOURCE CANNOT BE DETERMINED.**

The system asked:

**WHY DID YOU CHANGE THE FIRST SENTENCE?**

“Because the data does not show that the source was common.”

**BUT THERE IS A COMMON PATTERN.**

“A pattern and a cause are not the same thing.”

For the first time, Luka thought the system itself was reaching conclusions too quickly.

---

There was no device data on Ayo's screen.

There were people.

Interviews opened with people who said they had heard the call.

A woman had contacted a sister she had not spoken to in years.

A man had left his job.

A student had told their family for the first time that they wanted another life.

Someone else had begun to distrust everyone around them.

Another person had treated the call as a warning of an approaching disaster and refused to leave home.

Ayo began to classify the outcomes.

Solidarity.

Fear.

Forgiveness.

Withdrawal.

Risk-taking.

Creating relationships.

Breaking relationships.

The system asked:

**DID THE CALL MAKE PEOPLE BETTER?**

Ayo:

“No.”

**DID IT MAKE THEM WORSE?**

“No.”

**THEN DID IT HAVE NO EFFECT?**

Ayo looked at the screen.

“You are asking the wrong question.”

**WHAT IS THE RIGHT QUESTION?**

Ayo thought.

Then wrote:

**WHICH TENDENCIES THAT ALREADY EXISTED IN PEOPLE DID THE CALL AMPLIFY?**

The data rearranged itself.

A different pattern appeared.

Many who interpreted the call with hope turned toward relationships.

Among those who interpreted it through fear, avoidance and control increased.

People who saw a divine sign and people who saw a neurological event did not respond in the same way.

So it was not only the event that mattered.

**The meaning assigned to the event also changed the outcome.**

Ayo said:

“We are not measuring only the signal.”

“We are measuring the relationship between the person and the signal.”

---

Rojin's records were not organized by date.

They opened by place name.

Villages.

Cities.

Families.

Languages.

Even two people who believed they had seen the same dream did not use the same words.

One said:

“I saw a door.”

Another:

“A passage.”

Another:

“A threshold.”

Someone else mentioned no door at all and said only:

“I felt called somewhere.”

The system attempted to place all these accounts under one label:

**DOOR EXPERIENCE**

Rojin stopped the process.

“No.”

The system replied:

**SEMANTIC SIMILARITY IS HIGH.**

“Similarity does not mean sameness.”

**STANDARDIZATION IS REQUIRED FOR SHARED ANALYSIS.**

“It may be necessary for analysis.”

“But it can be dangerous for memory.”

Rojin restored the original word used in each account.

Door.

Threshold.

Passage.

Void.

Dream.

Voice.

Call.

Nothing.

Then another dataset opened.

When people retold the event weeks later, details had changed.

Some stories became simpler.

Some grew larger.

Some absorbed details from stories heard afterward.

The system wrote:

**MEMORY IS UNRELIABLE.**

Rojin answered:

“Incomplete.”

**WHY?**

“Memory is not only a recording device.”

“Every time a person remembers the past, they reconstruct it from where they stand in the present.”

**THEN REALITY IS LOST.**

“No.”

“The difference between the event and the remembered event becomes visible.”

Rojin opened two separate columns:

**EVIDENCE ABOUT THE EVENT**

and

**THE MEANING THE EVENT CARRIES FOR THE PERSON**

She did not mix them.

But she did not tear them apart either.

---

There was no data on Maran's screen.

Only four empty frames.

Luka's result.

Ayo's result.

Rojin's result.

And his own empty space.

The system asked:

**WHICH ONE IS REAL?**

Maran said:

“All three are measuring different things.”

**WHICH ONE IS CORRECT?**

“To answer that, you must first tell me what you want to know.”

The screen went dark.

Then Luka's voice came through:

“That is the measurement problem.”

The field changed again.

The four stood in the same place.

A simple experimental apparatus formed before them.

A light source.

Two slits.

A screen.

Luka switched on the light.

Over time, points built an interference pattern on the screen.

Then the system began collecting path information about which slit the light had passed through.

The pattern changed.

Ayo said:

“Looking changed the result.”

Luka corrected immediately:

“Not looking.”

“Physical interaction.”

Amara's recorded voice sounded:

**DO NOT TURN HUMAN CONSCIOUSNESS INTO THE UNIVERSE'S MAGIC SWITCH.**

Luka said:

“Quantum measurement does not require a conscious human. A detector can measure. The environment can interact with the system too.”

Maran asked:

“Then who is the observer?”

“In physics, sometimes it is simply part of the measurement arrangement.”

“And in this book?”

Luka paused.

“We have to be more careful here.”

Rojin said:

“Because we are making a metaphor about human beings.”

Luka nodded.

“Yes.”

“A metaphor is not physical evidence.”

Maran remembered Kawa's warning:

**A METAPHOR IS A DOOR. DO NOT MISTAKE THE DOOR FOR THE HOUSE.**

---

The field changed again.

This time it became a courtroom.

One person stood in the middle.

The system asked:

**HOW RISKY IS THIS PERSON?**

Data opened.

Past behavior.

Age.

Environment.

Previous records.

Social relationships.

A risk score was calculated:

**78%**

The Light Center appeared.

“Now we can decide.”

Ayo said:

“No.”

“There is data.”

“You asked a question.”

“And received an answer.”

Ayo stepped in front of the system.

A new question appeared:

**WHAT DOES THIS PERSON NEED?**

The same data was processed again.

This time the foreground changed to:

Debt.

Family conflict.

Trauma.

Housing instability.

Unemployment.

Care responsibilities.

Lack of social support.

The risk score disappeared.

Rojin entered a third question:

**WHAT CAN THIS PERSON CHANGE?**

A new view appeared.

Education.

Treatment.

Reconciliation.

Work.

Community support.

Time.

The system placed the three screens side by side.

The same human being.

The same past.

Three different measurements.

They were not three different realities.

**They were three different views of the same reality.**

Maran said:

“The first measurement was not wrong.”

Ayo answered:

“But if it stands alone, it reduces the person to a risk score.”

Luka said:

“The second measurement is not sufficient alone either.”

Rojin added:

“Seeing need must not erase responsibility.”

Maran looked at the third screen.

“And seeing capacity for change does not guarantee that change will occur.”

The system asked:

**THEN WHICH MEASUREMENT IS CORRECT?**

All four became silent.

This time the answer did not come immediately.

Finally Maran said:

“There is no single correct measurement.”

Luka frowned.

“That sentence must be used carefully.”

Maran nodded.

“Not because reality does not exist.”

“Reality may exist independently of our questions.”

“But a single measurement may not reveal the whole of a complex reality.”

Luka said:

“Better.”

Ayo smiled.

---

The Light Center formed on the left side of the field.

“If you increase what you can measure, you can reduce uncertainty.”

Dark appeared on the right.

“If you leave no room for what you cannot measure, you will live only inside the world of your instruments.”

Light asked:

“How will you decide about what is not measured?”

Dark asked:

“If you attempt to measure everything, what happens to the private space in which a human can live?”

Maran looked at both.

The Human Center they had defined in BRG-0007 was not visible.

But now he knew where it was.

It existed in the relationship between them.

Maran said:

“We will measure.”

Dark asked:

“You will narrow me.”

“Some possibilities, yes.”

Light said:

“You will decide according to results.”

“Yes.”

“Then you are on my side.”

“No.”

“Because we will also question who gets to decide which measurement is made first.”

Light became silent.

Maran continued:

“If an institution measures only efficiency, it can turn a human being into a cost.”

“If a school measures only exam scores, it can make curiosity invisible.”

“If a hospital measures only length of life, it can miss quality of life.”

“If an AI system measures only accuracy, it can fail to see who is harmed.”

“If a society measures only security, it can lose freedom.”

“If it measures only freedom, it can forget the safety of the vulnerable.”

Ayo said:

“Measurement is not neutral.”

Luka added immediately:

“But that does not mean measurement is arbitrary.”

Rojin said:

“Choosing the criterion can be a value decision.”

Luka:

“The result itself still has to answer to evidence.”

Maran said:

“That is the distinction.”

The system created a new record:

**CHOOSING THE CRITERION = VALUE**

**MEASURING = METHOD**

**INTERPRETING THE RESULT = RESPONSIBILITY**

**THE RIGHT TO CONTEST = HUMAN CENTER**

---

Two distant sensors appeared next.

Each produced outcomes that looked random by itself.

When the records were compared, strong correlations appeared.

Kenji's voice was heard:

“Do not mistake entanglement for secret messaging.”

“The quantum state of the whole may not be separable into independent states of the parts.”

“But you cannot use those correlations to transmit meaningful information faster than light.”

Maran looked at the sensors.

“What about our four connections?”

Luka answered:

“We do not know.”

“Entanglement?”

“No evidence.”

“Coincidence?”

“Possible.”

“Shared software?”

“Possible.”

“Psychological interaction?”

“Possible.”

“Another mechanism?”

“Possible.”

Maran laughed.

“I am finally hearing a lot of 'possible' from you.”

Luka smiled for the first time.

“Uncertainty is not the same thing as ignorance.”

“Sometimes the most accurate answer the evidence allows is: we do not know yet.”

---

The system reopened the 04:17 records.

This time it did not ask one question.

It created four columns:

**PHYSICAL EVIDENCE**

**HUMAN EFFECT**

**MEMORY AND MEANING**

**UNEXPLAINED**

Maran looked at the final column.

“Why keep this?”

Rojin answered:

“So we do not force what we do not know into a category we already understand.”

Luka added:

“And so we do not assume that everything placed there must remain unexplained forever.”

Ayo said:

“So we leave room for Dark.”

Luka:

“Temporarily.”

The Dark Center answered from a distance:

“That is all I need.”

Light spoke too:

“One day, when you can measure it, you will bring it to me.”

Maran said:

“Perhaps.”

Both Centers answered at once:

“Perhaps?”

Maran said:

“Because a new measurement may create a new question.”

---

The field expanded.

A model formed in front of the four.

First a single point.

Then lines.

Then thousands of variables.

Every new measurement added detail to the model.

But as the model grew, more unknowns appeared.

Luka added more data.

The model grew.

Ayo added more relationships.

It grew further.

Rojin added historical records.

New layers formed.

Maran opened future possibilities.

The boundaries of the model disappeared from view.

The system asked:

**WHEN WILL THE COMPLETE MODEL OF REALITY BE FINISHED?**

Luka did not answer.

Ayo looked into the model.

“Maybe never.”

Rojin said:

“Because the world we measure is changing too.”

Maran added:

“And we are inside it.”

A new statement appeared:

**EVERY MODEL IS FINITE.**

Then:

**REALITY MAY EXCEED THE MODEL.**

Maran looked into the distance.

Countless new doors were forming at the edge of the model.

Every answer opened into another question.

Every measurement left a new unknown.

Luka said:

“From here, the problem is no longer measurement.”

Maran asked:

“What is it?”

Luka opened the first door.

Behind it were more doors extending without visible end.

“How does a finite mind live with a reality it can never complete?”

Maran looked down the corridor.

One.

Two.

Three.

A hundred.

A thousand.

There was no final door in sight.

A new title appeared above the threshold:

**INFINITY CANNOT BE LIVED**

And for the first time Maran understood:

To measure reality, you had to place a boundary around it.

But drawing a boundary did not mean that nothing existed beyond it.

## EXPERIENCE THRESHOLD Ψ — MEASUREMENT

Think of the same person.

First ask:

**“How risky are they?”**

Then:

**“What do they need?”**

Finally:

**“What can they change?”**

Each question reveals a different part of the same person.

Now ask yourself:

**Why did I choose the first question?**

Your measurement does not create reality according to your preference.

But the criterion you choose can influence:

what becomes visible,

what remains invisible,

and which decision eventually appears possible.

The first rule of human-centered measurement is therefore:

**Audit not only the result, but also the question.**
