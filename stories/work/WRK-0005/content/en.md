# Emptiness

When I was told the file was incomplete, I would first count the pages.

One.

Two.

Fly.

Adds.

Signatures.

Dates.

Seals.

If there was an empty space between the pages, I would look at that too.

Because sometimes what was missing wasn't paper.

The chief wanted a list of the missing items. He did not ask why the person who brought the file came for the third time. I didn't ask first either. When he finished the list, the man standing at the door said, "Every time I come, I am told a different card." The boxes in front of me suddenly no longer look organized. I only wrote to him what I knew; Since I didn't know, I said, "I need to verify this." The man didn't say thank you. He wanted to know at least if he would come for the fourth time in vain.

A man came to my table.

He had a thin file in his hand.

He left the file on the table.

"Missing."

"What is missing?"

He opened the file.

One page.

Two pages.

Three pages.

Adds.

Everything was in place.

The man looked at the file: it had been returned as incomplete again. Nobody had said what was missing. When he closed it, he still did not know what to add.

A red mark was placed on the file.

MISSING

There was nothing else underneath.

The man showed the sign with his finger.

"What does this mean?"

“That it was missing.”

“I see that too.”

I looked at the file.

The pages were in place.

The attachments were spot on.

The signatures were in place.

The dates were all filled in.

The man took the file.

“I'll take it again.”

"To where?"

“Where it came from.”

He walked towards the door.

Then he stopped.

“How does one know where to start if something is missing?”

I didn't answer.

The man came out.

A file arrived.

Everything looked okay.

Checklist.

Approval.

History.

Signature.

Additional documents.

In capital letters on the last page:

COMPLETED

it said.

I was about to close the file.

Someone came in.

“Why is this file still here?”

“It's done.”

"What happened?"

I looked at the file.

“It says here.”

The man opened the file.

“I'm not asking if the file is complete.”

“What do you ask?”

“What happened?”

He turned the pages.

There was no answer to any of them.

He looked at the last page again.

COMPLETED

The man closed the file.

"Ok."

he said.

He took the file.

After a while, the same file appeared on another table.

There was a new note on it:

“The result is awaited.”

I opened the file.

Everything was completed again.

There was only one thing in the file.

What was expected.

A man sat next to me during lunch.

“Some files never end.”

“Are there any missing documents?”

"No."

“Signature?”

"No."

"What?"

The man took a sip of his tea.

"The person gives the file. Then the file continues."

"How?"

"He goes one place. Then he goes somewhere else. Then he goes somewhere else."

"Later?"

“And then one is still in the same place.”

He left his tea glass on the table.

"Sometimes the file is completed. People are waiting."

We were silent for a while.

Then the sound of chairs came from another table.

A woman came.

He put his file in front of me.

“I stopped talking about it.”

“Telling what?”

“Everything.”

He opened the file.

“When I first came, I explained it at length.”

He turned the pages.

“Then they sent it somewhere else.”

He turned another page.

“I explained it again there.”

There were only a few sentences at the end of the file.

“What do you want now?”

The woman looked at the file.

“Get my job done.”

“Have you said this before?”

"Yes."

"Alright?"

The woman lifted her shoulders.

“I stopped talking about it.”

He closed the file.

“It doesn't change when you explain it.”

There was a small piece of paper on my desk.

On it only:

“It's missing.”

it said.

I put another piece of paper next to it.

Then:

“What's missing?”

I wrote.

I left the two papers side by side.

I looked for a while.

Then I put the first paper in the file.

I left the second one on the table.

A report came.

At the top:

“There doesn't seem to be a problem.”

it said.

There were several paintings underneath.

Numbers.

Rates.

Controls.

Dates.

I read the report.

A person sat across from me.

“Is this true?”

“That's what the report says.”

“I'm not like that.”

“What do you mean?”

“What I experienced does not exist here.”

He pulled the report towards him.

He looked at the paintings.

“This could all be true.”

Then he closed the report.

“But I'm not here.”

I went to the table by the window.

There was an old file on it.

A few papers inside.

An unfinished sentence at the edge of one of them:

“If we do it this way…”

An empty space underneath.

On another page:

“Who said that?”

Again there was no answer.

I turned the pages.

There was a final form.

Every box was full.

A small gap at the bottom.

I took the pen in my hand.

I didn't write.

A woman came to my table.

He held out the file in his hand.

“Can you check this?”

I opened the file.

There was everything.

“It's not missing.”

I said.

The woman looked at the file.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

The woman waited for a while.

“So why isn't it happening yet?”

I looked at the file.

I didn't answer.

The woman sat on the chair.

He took the file in front of him.

He started turning the pages.

First page.

Second.

Third.

Adds.

Signatures.

Dates.

Last page.

Everything was in place.

The woman closed the file.

“I just want my job done.”

I've heard this sentence before.

But I couldn't find it in the file.

I opened the file.

I looked at the last page.

I thought about the sentence the woman said.

The pages were full.

There were signatures.

There were dates.

There were eclairs.

But that sentence was not there.

The woman got up.

He left the file on the table.

Gone.

I looked at the file for a while.

Then I opened the first page.

There was a small space between the pages that no one had filled.
