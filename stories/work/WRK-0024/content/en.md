# The Extra

It was easy to give a new file to someone eager to work. It was becoming more and more difficult for me to distribute tasks without knowing which of the papers on the table would be useful to a person.

After a file was closed, a few papers remained on my desk.

The new person I was working with that day collected them.

“Should I do these too?”

I liked the question; For years, when asked, I said "Do it" without thinking. I thought sharing the work was help. But one of the papers I left on the table was merely a new reproduction of the cover text. If I gave it to him even though I knew it wasn't necessary, I would be using someone else's diligence to clear my own desk. “Let's find out what it will do first,” I said. This answer did not diminish his enthusiasm; He was admitting that I owed him a reason.

He showed me the papers. I said one of those tasks did not need to be assigned to him. He was willing to help; I was afraid of passing on pointless work.

I looked at him for a moment.

I used to feel comfortable when I heard this sentence.

The more someone had work to do, the more the work progressed.

“Is it necessary?”

I asked.

The newcomer looked at the papers.

"I don't know."

In the afternoon, a voice came from another table.

“Can you look at this too?”

The newcomer looked up.

"Of course."

The file came to the table.

“Can you fix this too?”

"It is possible."

Another paper arrived.

“Let's add this too.”

The newcomer put it all in front of him.

After a while, his table was full.

I went to him.

“Which one first?”

"I don't know."

“What is supposed to happen in the end?”

He opened the papers one by one.

Nothing was written on one of them.

In the other just:

Necessary action will be taken.

In another:

It's urgent.

“I can do all of these.”

he said.

“Do you know which one is for what?”

He looked up.

"No."

There was a small note in a file:

It should be completed today.

The new person opened the file.

“What needs to happen?”

“Completion of the file.”

"In conclusion?"

There was no answer.

Person who brought the file:

“Can you?”

he asked.

The newcomer looked at me.

I looked at the file.

“Let's find out what needs to happen first.”

The man took the file back.

“We'll see later.”

The file left the table.

The same file came back the next day.

This time on:

Expected result: Informing the applicant.

it said.

The new person opened the file.

“It's obvious now.”

"Yes."

“Let's do this.”

He worked on the file.

When the job was done, he closed the file.

He stopped as he reached for another file.

"Finished."

"Yes."

“What else should I do?”

I looked at the files on the table.

“Don't do anything for now.”

He smiled.

“This is the first time you've said that.”

The person at the next table came during lunch.

“Can you help me with these files too?”

The new arrival looked at the files.

“Is it my job?”

“You do it faster.”

“What should happen at the end of these files?”

The man stopped.

“Things need to be done.”

"In conclusion?"

“The result is the same.”

"What?"

The man did not answer.

The new person handed the files back.

“Let's clear this up first.”

The man took the files.

“You used to do it.”

The newcomer looked at me.

“I used to do it.”

There was only one file left on the dinner table.

The new person opened the file.

“We can do this too.”

“How long will it take?”

"Half an hour."

"Later?"

“It ends.”

“What will change?”

He looked at the file.

He thought for a while.

"Nothing."

He closed the file.

“Then let's not do it.”

We remained silent for a while.

Then he brought another file.

“Let's do this.”

"Why?"

“I know what will happen in the end.”

He opened the file.

Expected result: Delivery of the application to the relevant person.

it said.

It had his own name underneath it.

There was another name.

“We will do this together.”

he said.

I nodded.

At the end of the day, there was still work to be done on his desk.

Some were waiting.

It was not clear what some of them would be used for.

The new person turned off the computer.

“I'll leave these for tomorrow.”

"Leave."

“What if someone asks?”

He thought for a while.

“I did not complete the job whose expected outcome was unclear.”

He did not touch the papers left on the table.

I didn't touch it either.
