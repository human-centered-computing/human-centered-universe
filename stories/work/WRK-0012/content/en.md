# The Waiting One

The file numbers of those waiting in the corridor were following each other; Why they came was a separate story. No chart showed whether a person would consider himself heard when the number was called.

There were a few chairs at the end of the aisle.

Nobody was calling it a waiting area.

But those who came were sitting on chairs.

Those who were standing were sitting down after a while.

Others were coming to replace those who left.

The chairs were not left empty.

One of the waiters came without a file in his hand. His name did not appear in the system. “I came here last month,” he said. I didn't remember him at first. Then I took out from the same coat that he had been sitting at the end of the corridor the previous time. He asked me which officer was talking to him. I didn't know. It was a small part of my day; I was the door to the answer he had been waiting for for a month. No timetable showed the time difference between us.

A man came to me.

He had a file in his hand.

“Is it over?”

he asked.

"What?"

He showed the file.

"This."

I opened the file.

On the first page:

The process has been started.

it said.

“Initiated.”

I said.

“When will it end?”

I looked at the file again.

"I don't know."

The man waited a few seconds.

“So what am I waiting for?”

This time I couldn't answer.

After a while, I saw the same man in the corridor.

He still had the same file in his hand.

“Are there any results?”

I asked.

“It didn't work for me.”

“In the file?”

“It appeared in the file.”

He opened the file.

He showed the last page.

It has been concluded.

There was history underneath.

A signature.

A seal.

The man looked at me.

“Do you know what this means?”

"No."

“I don't know either.”

He closed the file.

“They told me to wait.”

One of the chairs in the aisle was vacant.

The man sat there.

He put the file on his knees.

After a while someone else came.

“Where is this row?”

he asked.

The man did not answer.

I showed him the paper on the wall.

It had numbers on it.

Next to a number:

waiting

it said.

Waiting for his turn, the applicant looked at his own number.

"This is mine."

"Yes."

“How long will I wait?”

Nobody answered.

A file arrived on my desk this afternoon.

On:

Pending

it said.

There was history next to it.

Below:

Necessary evaluation will be made.

I opened the file.

There were documents inside.

Signatures.

Dates.

Notes.

Adds.

Everything was in place.

On the last page:

The result is awaited.

it said.

I asked the person who brought the file which department owed us an answer. The relevant department, he said, though he could not name it.

One day, I went to the woman sitting in the waiting chair.

“What are you waiting for?”

The woman looked at her file.

"Reply."

“What answer?”

“I don't know about that.”

“What is your application about?”

The woman opened the file.

“I told you this.”

“Is it in the file?”

“There is.”

"Alright?"

“Nobody read it.”

He closed the file.

“I'm waiting now.”

When I returned to my desk, a list was open on the screen.

There was a different situation in every line.

It has been launched.

It is under review.

It is being evaluated.

Waiting.

It has been concluded.

There was another column off to the side.

Waiting time.

Numbers were written.

Three days.

Twelve days.

Twenty-eight days.

Sixty-four days.

There were no numbers on a line.

Just:

uncertain

it said.

I stared at that line for a while.

The person next to me leaned towards the screen.

“Why are they keeping this waiting?”

"I don't know."

“Is it missing?”

"No."

“Is there a signature missing?”

"No."

“Is a decision needed?”

"I don't know."

He opened the file.

“Everything is okay.”

"Yes."

He looked at the last page.

The result is awaited.

He remained silent for a while.

Then he closed the file.

“So why is he waiting?”

This time I didn't answer.

One day, the pending files were discussed at the meeting.

A long list was projected on the screen.

“Many of these have been waiting for a long time.”

said someone.

“Are there any missing documents?”

“Some don't have it.”

“Has the decision been made?”

“In some.”

"Conclusion?"

There was silence.

Someone:

“It seems to have concluded.”

he said.

Another:

“Is it visible?”

"Yes."

“Did it really work out?”

Nobody answered.

The list on the screen has not changed.

After the meeting, the new employee came to me.

He had one of the files in his hand.

“I brought this for you.”

"Why?"

“Waiting.”

“What is he waiting for?”

"Conclusion."

“Where is the result?”

"I don't know."

He opened the file.

“All transactions have been completed here.”

He turned the pages.

“Look.”

I looked.

It really had everything.

Signatures.

Controls.

Dates.

Notes.

“And now?”

“He's waiting now.”

He left the file on the table.

“I guess finishing the job and seeing the results are not the same thing.”

I didn't answer.

In the afternoon I went out into the corridor.

The chairs were full again.

A man stood up.

He took his file.

“Did they call?”

I asked.

"No."

"Where are you going?"

"Home."

“Your file?”

The man looked at the file.

“He will stay here.”

"What about you?"

“And I will continue to wait.”

"How?"

The man laughed.

“As for it.”

In the evening I returned to my desk.

I opened the folder containing the pending files.

Next to a file:

waiting

it said.

I opened the file.

My name was on the first page.

I turned the pages.

Signatures.

Notes.

Dates.

Transactions made.

A single line on the last page:

The result is awaited.

There was another area underneath.

Pending:

My name was written on it.

I looked at the screen for a while.

Then I closed the file.

The same word appeared next to other files in the folder:

Waiting.
