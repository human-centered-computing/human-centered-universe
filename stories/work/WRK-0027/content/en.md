# Outside

A person can also stop when the exit door is open. That evening, as I waited with a piece of paper in my hand, I tried to discern whether it was work or habit that kept me inside.

When I stood in front of the door, there was no sound from inside.

The door was open.

When I thought about going out, I knew I would walk through this door again the next morning. I would scan my card again and sit at the same table. I was scared as if there was no other life waiting for me outside. Then I thought that walking out the door does not mean not coming to work the next day. I didn't have to answer every question I had as off-duty. If a colleague came to me with a file tomorrow, I could ask him who will receive the file, without giving him a ready-made answer. Even thinking about this changed the length of the corridor.

Nobody had closed it.

There were a few chairs in the corridor.

There was a file on one of them.

One jacket over the other.

There was an old box next to the wall.

On the box:

TO BE DELIVERED

it said.

Beyond the door was another corridor.

There was no table there.

There was no file.

Nobody was telling me where to go.

I stood on the threshold for a while.

Someone came behind me.

“Are you going to come out?”

"I don't know."

“The door is open.”

“I see.”

“Then get out.”

I passed through the door.

After a while, I heard the door closing behind me.

I didn't look back.

The first thing I noticed outside was that no one was waiting.

People were walking.

Some are fast.

Some are slow.

There were people who got somewhere.

And those who don't know where they are going.

Nobody gave me a file.

Nobody:

“Do this.”

He didn't say.

I stood on the sidewalk for a while.

Then I started walking.

I didn't know where I was going.

I passed a street.

Then another street.

I stopped in front of a shop.

I looked at my reflection in the glass.

There were tables inside.

People were sitting.

Someone was working.

A man was typing something at the computer.

A woman was arranging the papers in front of her.

A customer was waiting elsewhere.

There was a small inscription next to the door:

Please wait your turn.

I looked at the article.

Then I walked.

When I got home, there were some papers on the table.

The due date for payment of an invoice.

The paper a child brought from school.

A mechanic's trick.

A shopping list.

And a blank notepad.

I took them all in my hands one by one.

The bill had to be paid.

Answering the child's paper.

Searching for a repairman.

Getting what's on the list.

The blank paper said nothing.

I took the pen.

I left it without writing anything.

I went out the next day.

Not to go anywhere.

To walk.

After a while my phone rang.

It was a number I didn't recognize.

I opened it.

“I'm calling about a job.”

“What kind of job?”

The other person explained what to do.

I listened.

Finally:

“Okay,” I said.

I hung up the phone.

I took a few steps.

Then I stopped.

I was told what to do.

But I didn't know what would change when the work was done.

I picked up the phone again.

I called the number.

“Let me ask you something.”

“Here you go.”

“What should happen at the end of this?”

The other party remained silent.

"How?"

“What will have changed when the work is completed?”

He remained silent a little longer.

“We will get back to you.”

"When?"

"I don't know."

The phone is turned off.

I looked at the screen for a while.

Then I put the phone in my pocket and continued walking.

I entered a cafe.

I sat at an empty table.

The waiter came.

"What would you like?"

I ordered coffee.

Coffee has arrived.

After a while, I asked for the bill.

The waiter left the receipt.

I paid.

As I got up, I saw a small stain on the table.

I wiped it with the napkin in my hand.

The waiter looked from afar.

"Thank you."

"It is nothing."

I went out.

After a while, I thought why I was wiping the table.

Nobody wanted it.

No one had said it.

But I was pleased to see that the table was clean when I got up.

A few days later I passed by the old building.

I didn't go in.

I looked out the window.

The tables were still there.

Someone was working.

There were new people.

One of the old chairs was empty.

There was a file on a table.

A man opened the file.

A woman said something.

The man wrote.

Then he gave the file to someone else.

Everything was familiar.

I stayed in front of the window a little longer.

The door opened.

Someone came out.

He saw me.

“Aren't you going to come in?”

"No."

"Why?"

“I have no job.”

After saying that, I stopped.

“I have no business there right now.”

The man laughed.

“There's a lot of work out there.”

"I know."

“So why are you waiting?”

I didn't answer.

The man returned inside.

The door is closed.

I stood on the sidewalk for a while longer.

Then I walked.

One day, a friend asked me for help.

He had a long list in his hand.

“Can you handle these too?”

I got the list.

I read the first articles.

“What will happen at the end of all this?”

“Things will be over.”

"Later?"

“Then we can relax.”

“What will change?”

He looked at me.

“You started asking a lot of questions too.”

I smiled.

"Habit."

We looked at the list again.

We deleted some items.

We combined some of them.

Opposite one of them:

Result: information will be given.

we wrote.

One against the other:

Result: payment will be completed.

We stopped at another one.

“Why would we do this?”

"I can't remember."

We looked at each other.

I went back to the list.

“Let's leave it for now.”

Closed the list.

“Did you use to do this?”

"Probably."

"Now?"

I didn't answer.

I left the list on the edge of the table.

While returning home in the evening, it started to rain.

I stood under the eaves of a shop.

There was another person with me.

He also escaped from the rain.

“Will the rain stop?” he asked.

"I don't know."

We looked outside for a while.

“Should we wait?”

"If you want."

We waited.

After a while the rain decreased.

The man came out.

So I left.

I stopped in the middle of the road.

Nobody:

“Go now.”

He didn't say.

Nobody:

“Wait here.”

He didn't say.

I stood on the sidewalk for a while.

Then I walked home.

The next morning I sat at my desk.

There was a blank sheet of paper in front of me.

I looked for a long time.

Then I wrote three questions:

What will I do?

What will change?

How will I understand this?

I wrote a few words under the first question.

Underneath the second question was a slightly longer sentence.

I stopped at the third question.

I put down my pen.

I didn't know the answer yet.

I did not fold the paper.

I didn't put it in the drawer.

I left it on the table.

The door was open.

People were walking outside.

I watched them for a while.

Then I took the paper with me.

I stood up.

I went out the door.

I didn't know where to go this time.

But I didn't wait for someone to call me to walk.
