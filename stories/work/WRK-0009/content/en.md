# Another Name

In that room, a work that everyone had worked hard for was being introduced. It was not simple enough to explain who started, who changed and who concluded in the same sentence. As I listened from the back row, I felt that my own silence would also be a part of this narrative.

I didn't ask who had the idea at the meeting.

There were files on the table.

One of them had a computer open in front of him.

Another person had a pen in his hand.

I was sitting in the back.

The speaker was suggesting an order in which they could see what the files were at the end, not where they were.

What he said sounded familiar to me.

The three columns I drew to track where an application was, who had it, and with what outcome were on the screen. Amir was telling them in his own words. For a moment I thought I might have heard wrong; Maybe we really came up with the idea together. Then I saw that crooked header on the painting that I had only added when I was home. Even though I recognized my own writing, I felt like denying it. While the chief was talking, he turned to me and said, "You scanned a few files too." The relief in his voice hurt me more. The question I extracted from the files I scanned remained as a small aid in his presentation, not a beginning. If I object at that moment, I will diminish the work done; I was afraid that if I kept silent I would diminish myself.

I listened for a while.

“Good idea.”

said one of the men at the table.

“We can try this.”

Another nodded.

The speaker continued.

I remained silent.

When the meeting ended, the files remained on the table.

People came out one by one.

So I got up.

I was approaching the door when the speaker called after me.

“Do you want to add anything?”

I'm back.

"No."

I said.

Then I stopped.

"Maybe."

The man waited.

"What?"

"I don't know."

He smiled.

"We will speak later."

The woman waiting for me in the hallway had heard the last part of the meeting. “Wasn't that the picture you drew?” he asked. I told him that the lines belonged to me and that the information in them came from many people. “Tell him that too,” he said. When I heard his voice, I remembered that I didn't say the same thing about him. He showed me why the first column needed to be opened. Even when I wanted to ask for the idea back, I was about to appropriate it solely for myself. Before leaving, the woman handed over the old copy of the paper. On the back was his note, and on the front was my three columns. I thought about how to explain these without substituting the beginning of one for the other.

That afternoon I waited at the chief's door. “Our old paper was not in the presentation,” I said. He did not get up from his chair. “It's everyone's job now,” he said. “Yes,” I said, “who does what is part of it.” He looked at the meeting note in front of him. "Why are we having this conversation now? Are you against seeing results?" He had not raised his voice; He didn't expect me to raise it either. I suddenly felt like I had to answer that question. When I left, I still had the paper the woman gave me. I couldn't win the conversation. But for the first time, I heard clearly that my desire was not just about myself.

When the work started, everyone had something else to do.

Someone dug up old files.

Someone prepared the tables.

Someone edited the computer side.

Someone checked the records.

I also examined some files one by one.

I marked the missing lines.

I corrected the wrong parts.

I rewrote some sentences.

Sometimes I went back to the beginning of a file and changed a previously written note.

Nobody was talking about whose idea it was.

The work was progressing.

Person who brought the file:

“They added this too.”

he said.

“It was discussed at the meeting.”

“Who said that?”

"I can't remember."

A few days later, I saw the same file again.

This time there was a name opposite the "Contribution" section.

Below:

idea

it said.

I recognized the name.

It was the name of the person who spoke at the meeting.

I looked for a while.

Then I closed the file.

The work continued.

One day I rearranged a painting.

The next day, the same picture appeared before me in a different form.

Some of the changes I made were preserved.

Some were removed.

New lines have been added.

“I've said this before.”

he said.

A few handwritten notes in the margin.

“Who prepared this?”

I asked.

“I told you.”

“You have no name here.”

"I know."

“Then someone developed it.”

“And the idea.”

Then:

“It's nice to have the job done.”

he said.

“I just don't want the beginning to disappear.”

He said he changed some parts.

“Who developed this part?”

he asked.

he said.

“I will say something.”
