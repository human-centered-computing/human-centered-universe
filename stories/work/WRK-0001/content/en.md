# Threshold

While I was waiting in front of the door, I could hear voices coming from inside.

The door was not completely closed. There was a gap in between, so thin that light could pass through.

People were talking inside.

I couldn't understand what they were saying.

Sometimes a word was chosen.

"Signature."

On the first day, I set my watch according to the time of the device at the entrance. The device was a few minutes ahead. Noticing this, the man said, "You'll get used to it." Getting used to it was a task given to the person himself, not to a disorder that needed to be corrected. I scanned the card; My name appeared on a screen. I didn't know what I was going to do for whom yet, but I was definitely here. It would be for sure if I went out in the evening. The device said nothing about the hours that fell between those two certainties.

Then:

“Waiting.”

After a while:

“It's missing.”

Then the sound of chairs.

Papers rubbing against each other.

The closing of a door.

Then silence again.

I looked at the paper in my hand.

It had my name on it.

A few more lines below.

An empty section at the bottom.

I didn't know what should be written there.

The man beside me realized it was my first day and said they would call me soon. He did not know what for; I would find out inside.

Then he turned to the papers in front of him.

As if that answer was enough.

Maybe those were enough answers here.

There was a small table next to the door.

Numbers on it.

Some were taken.

Some were waiting.

There was a number on the paper in my hand.

I folded the paper again.

Then I opened it.

I folded it again.

I didn't know why I did this.

He wanted to give people something to do while they waited.

Someone came out.

He had a file in his hand.

The man next to me immediately stood up.

“Is it over?”

The man nodded.

“Not yet.”

Then he sat down again.

The person leaving walked a few steps down the corridor.

He stopped in front of the door.

He looked inside.

Then he returned.

It was like he didn't know where to go.

My number was called.

I stood up.

I opened the door.

I went in.

The first thing I saw was the table.

Files on the table.

Computer next to files.

Pen next to the computer.

A blank sheet of paper next to the pen.

A man looked up.

“Come.”

I approached.

I handed over the paper in my hand.

He looked at the paper.

Then to me.

“Leave this here.”

He showed me where to leave it.

I quit.

"Wait."

"How much?"

He looked at me.

"A little."

I sat on a chair.

There were other people in front of me.

Who is speaking?

who writes,

Who was he waiting for?

Nobody was talking loudly.

But there was constant movement in the room.

A file is opened.

A file is being closed.

A name is said.

A signature is made.

A chair is pulled out.

A phone rings.

One person stands up.

Another person is sitting.

After a while, a file appeared in front of me.

“Sort this.”

"How?"

“By number.”

I received the file.

I turned the pages.

There were numbers.

Some were missing.

Two numbers on one page were the same.

I said this.

“Which one should I put first?”

The man without looking at the file:

“I don't know.”

he said.

"Alright?"

“Continue for now.”

I continued.

I came again after a while.

"Finished."

He took the file.

He turned the pages.

“That's missing.”

"Which one?"

He pointed somewhere with his finger.

“There is nothing that should be here.”

“What needs to happen?”

“Find him.”

I received the file again.

I called.

I found it in another file.

I brought it.

I replaced it.

This time:

“Okay.”

he said.

The job was done.

But I didn't know what was completed.

Towards noon, the movement in the room decreased.

Someone went out.

Someone:

“Food?”

he asked.

Nobody answered.

After a while, three people came out together.

So I followed them.

We sat at a table.

The food has arrived.

People started talking about things they didn't talk about inside.

Child.

House.

Rent.

Car.

Someone's mother.

Someone's exam.

Moving someone.

Someone laughed.

It was hard to believe that the silence inside and the conversations here were coming from the same people.

The woman at the table said to me:

“Are you new?”

he asked.

"Yes."

“You'll get used to it.”

“What do you get used to here?”

The woman put down her fork.

“To everything.”

Then he smiled.

“Most of all, the waiting.”

We came back after dinner.

Same tables.

Same files.

Same chairs.

It's like we've never been outside.

They gave me another piece of paper.

“Sign this.”

I read.

Something was written.

But I couldn't understand what exactly I was approving of.

“What am I signing here?”

Man:

“The document was received.”

he said.

“I didn't take it.”

“He came before you.”

“Is this the same thing?”

The man looked at me.

Then he took the paper back.

“Wait for now.”

After a while, waiting started to feel like a chore.

I was sitting on the chair, holding the file in front of me, waiting for the next sentence that no one had said.

I found a paper on my desk.

There was only one word on it:

Incomplete.

Space underneath.

There was nothing else.

I asked who left the paper.

Nobody knew.

I looked at the word for a while.

Missing.

I didn't know what was missing.

Is it a document?

A signature?

Any information?

A result?

Or is it something else?

I left the paper on the edge of the table.

Then I opened another file.

A man came.

“Where do I give this?”

he asked.

I looked at the file.

“Over there.”

"Later?"

“You will wait.”

"How much?"

"I don't know."

The man looked at me.

Then:

“Okay.”

he said.

Gone.

I looked after him for a moment.

A strange feeling came over me.

I had just said the sentence that was said to me to someone else.

You will wait.

I returned to my desk.

On paper:

missing

I looked at the word.

I wrote a small question next to it:

What?

Then I left the paper in front of me.

As I was walking among the tables, I saw a drawer open.

There were old papers inside.

Someone's handwriting.

Some notes.

Short sentences.

Somewhere:

“Who will see the result?”

it said.

There was no answer underneath.

On another paper:

“Why is this so?”

In another:

“Checked again.”

And at the bottom:

“Who checked?”

There was no answer to this question either.

I closed the drawer.

I didn't tell anyone.

My table has changed.

“Get over here.”

they said.

I didn't ask why.

There were other files in the drawer of the new desk.

A pen.

A blank sheet of paper.

And an old chair.

I sat on the chair.

I looked at the table.

It was no different from the previous table.

But after a few hours I noticed.

People came to me differently.

Some were speaking directly.

Some were waiting at the door.

Some were leaving files without saying anything.

The table's location had changed.

I hadn't changed.

But in people's eyes, I seemed to have changed.

Someone came.

“Who am I going to give this to?”

I looked at the file.

"To me."

he said.

He handed over the file.

“When will the result come out?”

I didn't know.

But I thought I should answer.

“Wait.”

The man came out.

That word came out of my mouth on its own.

Wait.

It's like I've been there for a long time.

One evening, after everyone had left, I was left alone in the room.

The computers' lights were on.

There was a file open on a screen.

Next to a file:

The process continues.

it said.

In another:

Pending.

In another:

Incomplete.

In the last one:

Completed.

I looked at the screens.

I couldn't tell which one was actually finished.

I took the blank piece of paper lying on my desk.

On:

What to do here?

I wrote.

Then below:

It is expected.

I wrote.

I looked for a while.

I crossed out the second sentence.

I walked towards the door.

There was someone new in the hall.

He was holding a paper in his hand that looked like my first day's paper.

He looked at me.

“What is being done here?”

he asked.

I couldn't answer for a moment.

Then this came out of my mouth:

“Wait a moment.”

I stopped as soon as the word left my mouth.

The newcomer sat on the chair.

I walked towards the door.

I stopped at the end of the corridor and looked back.

The voices inside were the same again.

"Signature."

“Waiting.”

"Missing."

“It's done.”

Then the newcomer's voice was heard:

“What will I do?”

This time, no one from inside answered.

I stopped in front of the door.

I put my hand on the door handle.

I could go back inside and tell him what to do.

But I didn't know what to say.

I looked through the crack in the door for a while.

The newcomer was also looking at me.

“What shall I ask?” he said.

I opened my mouth.

I didn't answer.

Then:

“I don't know.”

I said.

I left the door open a little longer.

"Sensor."

And I walked.
