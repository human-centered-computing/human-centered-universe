# Paper

The paper had a voice.

It would rub slightly as it was removed from the file, and make a short sound when placed on the table.

Sometimes several cards were turned over at the same time.

The new papers had rough edges.

The corners of the old ones would have softened.

Fingerprints would remain on some of them.

On the right edge of a piece of paper was a tiny house drawing made with a pencil. It was crossed out and then redrawn. The child of the woman who prepared the file started school that week; I heard this from him during lunch. When I turned the paper over, the drawing disappeared and was replaced by a transaction date. The record would show that the woman was working there that day. What he missed while waiting would not be mentioned in any line.

Some have coffee stains.

Some have erased pencil marks.

When I looked at a piece of paper, I would no longer think only about what was written, but also about how many hands it had passed through.

A man came to my table.

He had a folded paper in his hand.

“Can I leave this here?”

“You can let it go.”

He opened the paper.

“I gave this away before.”

“Do you have a copy?”

He took out another piece of paper from his pocket.

He put the two side by side.

The text was the same.

But one of them had a little note in the bottom corner.

The other one didn't have it.

“Which one is correct?”

I looked at the two papers.

“They are both the same.”

The man nodded.

"Not."

He showed the note in the bottom corner.

“That's what I wrote.”

I took the paper in my hand.

The note stood out from the rest of the text.

It was smaller.

It was written more hastily.

“Why did you write this?”

“Because the other part doesn't explain what I want.”

“What do you want?”

The man looked at the paper.

“Get my job done.”

Then he showed the note.

“If I don't write this, I'm just a file.”

He put the paper in the file.

He closed the file.

"Now?"

“In your file now.”

The man looked at me for a while.

"Me too?"

I didn't answer.

He took the paper.

When I returned to my desk, there was a small form among the files.

There were boxes on it.

Ordinary.

Address.

Date.

Request.

Attachments.

His signature.

There was a place for everything.

At the bottom:

Description:

I looked inside the box.

It was hard to think about how what happened to a person could fit in here.

A woman came.

He put the form in front of me.

“What do I write here?”

"Explanation."

“What will I explain?”

“Your situation.”

The woman looked at the box.

“My situation does not fit here.”

He drew a small line inside the box.

“Only one sentence fits here.”

“You can use additional pages.”

The woman took out a piece of paper from her bag.

It was folded.

He opened it.

He started writing.

After a while, he took out the second piece of paper.

Then the third one.

The description box of the form remained empty.

The actual stories were moved to other papers.

There were dozens of pages in a thick file.

Signatures.

Dates.

Check marks.

Additional documents.

Correspondence.

There was a different hand under each page.

On the last page:

“What was necessary was done.”

it said.

I closed the file.

To the person who brought the file:

“Who did what here?”

I asked.

The man looked at the file.

“Everything is written.”

“There are names.”

"Yes."

“Where are their works?”

The man fell silent.

He closed the file.

There was a single page in another file.

A name on it.

A few lines below.

There is a place reserved for signatures at the bottom.

The man handed me the paper.

“Do I need to sign this?”

I looked at the paper.

“Do you know what it is?”

"No."

“Then read it first.”

The man took the paper again.

He looked for a while.

"I read."

“Do you understand?”

He did not nod.

“If I don't sign, there will be no transaction.”

He left the paper on the table.

“Even if I don't sign it, it's mine.”

He took the paper.

Gone.

Papers sometimes spoke instead of humans.

In a file:

“The requester has been informed.”

it said.

I saw the owner of the file.

“Have you been informed?”

The man nodded.

"No."

I showed him the paper.

“Given here.”

The man looked at the paper.

“I don't have a signature.”

“No signature required here.”

The man moved closer to the paper.

“Then how did they inform me?”

I looked at the paper.

The answer was there.

It wasn't on the man's face.

After a while, I started making small marks on the edges of the papers.

A line.

One point.

A question mark.

I wasn't explaining these to anyone.

One day, I saw the mark I had made in a file.

Another person wrote a note next to it:

“Checked.”

He didn't know what my sign meant.

I didn't explain either.

I found another piece of paper on the table by the window.

On:

“If we do it this way…”

it said.

There was another page underneath.

There was no continuation of the half sentence.

But a few calculations were made on the edge.

Someone had tried something.

Then he left.

I replaced the paper.

In another file:

“Who said that?”

It was written.

There was no answer underneath.

I put the paper among the others.

One day I had a blank page in front of me.

There was nothing on him.

The woman next to me:

“What will you write?”

he asked.

"I don't know."

“Write something.”

I took the pen in my hand.

In the middle of the page, I wrote the first word that came to my mind at that moment:

Human.

The woman looked at the word.

“You can't write this here.”

"From where?"

“There's no place for that here.”

I left the pen on the table.

I put the page in the file.

On the last page of a file:

Prepared by:

it said.

It was empty.

Below:

Controlled by:

There was a name in front of him.

One below:

Approved by:

Another name.

I looked at the “Prepared by” section.

I knew who the person who prepared the file was.

But his name wasn't there.

I took the pen in my hand.

I looked at the empty space.

Then I put down the pen.

One day they brought an old file.

“Can you separate this?”

I opened the file.

There was a date on the first page.

A signature on the second page.

A short handwritten note on the third page.

Someone else's correction on page four.

A section drawn on page five.

Rewritten version on page six.

I turned the pages one by one.

Each page bore the imprint of a different hand.

On the last page only:

“It will be re-evaluated.”

it said.

There was no name underneath.

I closed the file.

After a while someone came.

“Whose file is this?”

I looked at the file.

He had his number.

It had history.

They had signatures.

But there was no answer to the question.

“The file is here.”

I said.

The man took the file.

“I didn't ask for that.”

He looked at the first page.

Then to the others.

He remained silent for a while.

“Where is the human?”

he asked.

He opened the file again.

He turned the pages.

Name.

History.

Signature.

Notes.

Correction.

Seal.

Then he stopped somewhere.

He placed his finger on the small gap at the edge of the page.

“I didn't touch this place.”

He closed the file.

He left it on the table.

Gone.

I looked at the file for a while.

Then I opened the first page.

There was a small space at the edge of the paper that no one had filled.
