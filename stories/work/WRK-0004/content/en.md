# The Form

## The Paper

The form had boxes.

Name. Date. Reason. Signature.

I filled them.

One box was unnecessary.

I asked why it existed.

“It has always been there.”

I remembered the rule.

## The Missing Signature

One day everything was ready.

The work was complete.

The decision was clear.

But a signature was missing.

Nothing could move.

“Who needs to sign?”

“Someone.”

“Why?”

“Because the form requires it.”

The purpose had disappeared.

The procedure remained.

## Correcting Reality

I returned the form.

A line was wrong.

The information was correct, but it was written in the wrong place.

I corrected the line.

The matter became acceptable.

That day I learned that a system can reject a correct answer because it is written in the wrong box.

## More Forms

Then came another form.

Then another.

We began filling forms about forms.

Someone asked whether the process could be simplified.

The answer was not no.

It was another form.

## The Empty Box

Years later I found an old form.

One box was still empty.

It asked:

“What is this for?”

Nobody had filled it.

Perhaps because there was no place for the answer.
