# The Chair

## A Place

There were many chairs.

They looked almost identical.

Almost.

One was larger. One was closer to the window. One stood behind a different desk.

Nobody explained the difference.

I learned by watching who sat where.

## Your Place

One morning I sat in a chair that was empty.

A voice behind me said:

“That is not your place.”

I looked around.

There was no sign.

No name.

Only a chair.

I moved.

The chair remained empty for the rest of the morning.

## Height

Some chairs were higher.

People sitting in them spoke less, yet their words traveled farther.

Others leaned forward when they spoke, as if asking permission from the room.

I began to understand that hierarchy does not always need a uniform.

Sometimes it only needs furniture.

## The Empty Chair

Years later, a person left.

His chair stayed empty.

For weeks people continued to look at it before entering the room.

Nobody sat there.

The position remained after the person had gone.

That was the first time I understood that institutions can preserve a place more faithfully than they preserve a person.

## Sitting

I eventually found my own chair.

I stopped noticing its shape.

That frightened me more than being told where to sit.

Because once the chair felt natural,
I no longer needed anyone to tell me where I belonged.
