# The Chair

There were many chairs. They looked almost identical. Almost.

One was larger. One was closer to the window. One stood behind a different desk. Nobody explained the difference. I learned by watching who sat where.

One morning I sat in an empty chair.

“That is not your place.”

There was no sign. No name. Only a chair. I moved.

Some chairs were higher. People sitting in them spoke less, yet their words traveled farther. I learned that hierarchy sometimes needs only furniture.

Years later someone left. His chair remained empty. Nobody sat there.

The person had gone, but the place remained.

Eventually I found my own chair. I stopped noticing its shape.

That frightened me most: when the chair feels natural, nobody needs to tell you where you belong.
