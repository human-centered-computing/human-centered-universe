# Voice

Before I heard the door open, I heard the chair being pulled inside.

Then came the sound of paper.

A file has been opened.

A page has been turned.

The pen was left on the table.

The door is closed.

Once I heard a subtle objection from the next room. The new employee was asking about the purpose of the text he had prepared. “Do as directed,” the chief said without even turning his face. The employee lowered his voice: “Then how will I know if I'm wrong?” I stood in the corridor waiting for an answer. There was the squeak of a chair and the sound of a drawer being pulled from inside. Sometimes not answering was an answer. The next day, the same employee did not ask his question; He wrote the text twice and left them both on the table.

I didn't know which of these was important at the time.

Here the voices were not easily separated from each other.

I recognized some people's footsteps.

As I approached in the hallway I would realize who it was.

There were people walking fast.

Those who drag their shoes.

Those who slow down in front of the door.

Those who regulate their breathing before entering.

Some people would knock on the door twice.

Some people would never steal.

Some people open the door:

“I'm not disturbing you, am I?”

he would ask.

Most of the time they would come in without waiting for an answer.

One day, two people were arguing inside.

Their voices got louder as they passed the door.

“I didn't say that.”

“You said it.”

"No."

"I heard."

“Who heard?”

There was a short silence inside.

Then came the sound of chairs.

“Get it in writing.”

The discussion is over.

After a while, I saw the same conversation written down on paper.

There were two sentences.

In one:

"The necessary discussion on the issue has been held."

In the other:

“The parties have been informed.”

There was no telling who said what.

There was no debate itself.

There was only a meeting.

Some sentences on paper turned into other sentences.

“I didn't want this.”

instead of:

“The request has been evaluated.”

“No one told me.”

instead of:

“The relevant person has been informed.”

“Why did this happen?”

instead of:

“This is how the process was carried out.”

As the words changed, the speaker stepped back.

A woman came to my table.

He had a file in his hand.

“I've told you this before.”

He opened the file.

“Not here.”

"What?"

“What I was talking about.”

He turned the pages.

“Only my application appears here.”

“You have an application.”

“I just didn't apply.”

The woman closed the file.

“The person listening to me said it was important.”

I looked at the paper.

“It's not written here.”

"I know."

The woman took the file.

He stopped in front of the door.

“So I didn't say it.”

Gone.

There was a small tape recorder on the table.

It was brought to be used at a meeting.

When the meeting started, everyone spoke more carefully.

“Are we recording this?”

"Yes."

After that the sentences became shorter.

No one gave a long example.

Nobody interrupted anyone else.

Everyone fell silent after completing their sentences.

The meeting is over.

The device is turned off.

The conversation in the room seemed to end with him.

I listened to the recording later.

There were voices.

Movement of chairs.

Someone coughing.

Papers rubbing against each other.

A long silence.

Then someone:

“Let's not do it like this.”

he said.

The sentence was on record.

But he didn't know why he said it.

The conversation before that was incomprehensible.

The sentence was appropriate.

It lacked meaning.

One day someone said to me:

“You said that.”

he said.

"What?"

“This is how it should be done.”

"I can't remember."

“I heard it.”

“Who was there?”

He counted the names.

Then he put a piece of paper on the table.

“Let's put this in writing.”

He handed me the pen.

“What will we write?”

“What you said.”

“That's not what I said.”

The man looked at me.

“You just said it.”

“I didn't say what I meant.”

“Does it matter?”

He left the paper in front of me.

“Here you will have your sentence.”

Some words alone became sufficient.

Ok.

Waiting.

Sent.

Missing.

Suitable.

Again.

It was not necessary to know who the person who said these things was.

When INCOMPLETE was written on a file, the file was returned.

It wasn't always obvious what was missing.

When SUITABLE was written, it was progressing.

There was no question of who found it appropriate.

When it was written AGAIN, the same work was done again.

For those asking why it is necessary again:

“For control.”

It was said.

One day I saw that the same file was written AGAIN three times.

The first one had history.

Another signature on the second.

In the third, only the seal.

Person who brought the file:

“What's missing?”

he asked.

“It says here again.”

"Why?"

I looked at the paper.

I didn't answer.

The man took the file.

“We will do it again.”

he said.

Gone.

At lunchtime people talked differently.

Those who were silent at the table were interrupting each other here.

Someone was talking about their children.

One is from debt.

Someone was saying he couldn't sleep last night.

The meal someone will make when they get home.

Same people.

Same sounds.

But outside the tables, the sentences were getting longer.

Nobody:

“Appropriate.”

He didn't say.

Nobody:

“Again.”

He didn't say.

A man told a long story about an incident.

The woman next to him:

“So what did you do?”

he asked.

The man fell silent.

"Nothing."

"Why?"

“I didn't know what to do.”

No one gave him another sentence.

I found a note in a file.

I didn't know who wrote it.

It was like this:

“Who said that?”

There was no answer underneath.

I left the note in the file.

A woman approached the same question from a different place on another day.

He left the file in front of me.

“There is nothing I said here.”

“What did you say?”

He started to explain.

This time I didn't interrupt him.

When he finished, he pointed to an empty area.

“Can we write this here?”

I handed him the pen.

He didn't take it.

“You write.”

"What?"

“What I said.”

“I don't know how to write.”

“I don't know either.”

We remained silent for a while.

Then the woman said:

“Leave it blank.”

he said.

Gone.

I did not cover the free space.

One day, the man at the next table didn't speak at all all day.

He did his job.

He took the files.

He completed the signatures.

He sent the papers.

When leaving in the evening:

“You haven't talked at all today.”

I said.

The man put on his coat.

“There's nothing to talk about.”

“Not at all?”

He paused for a moment.

“There is.”

"What?"

The man looked at the door.

“No one is listening.”

He opened the door.

Output.

I was left alone at the table.

The computer had sound.

Footsteps were coming from the corridor.

The phone rang somewhere.

A chair was pulled out.

A file is closed.

Then there was silence.

One of the files from that day was sitting on my desk.

There was only one word on it:

SUITABLE.

I turned the paper over.

Another sentence was written in small letters on the back:

“Who said that?”

There was no answer underneath.

I pulled the paper in front of me.

I took the pen in my hand.

I kept it for a while.

Then I left.

Footsteps came from the corridor.

A door opened.

A voice spoke.

I couldn't hear what he said.

The door is closed.

The paper remained on the table.
