# Signature

Everyone knew what the signature looked like.

Your name.

Your last name.

Sometimes a few lines.

Sometimes an illegible shape.

Some people would sign their names for longer periods of time. Some used only two letters. Some people would write differently every time.

One day, a place was made for my name at the end of the text that was brought to me. I didn't write the text. In it, it was said that a person was given information; I didn't know whether information was given or not. “Only as a matter of procedure,” they said. When I picked up the pen, what I feared was not discipline. Even if I found out that sentence wasn't true, I would still feel obligated to believe it because of my signature. I put down my pen and asked for the call recording. The silence in the room weighed more than an objection.

But when you stood under the paper, they all came to the same place.

Under your name.

When I first signed, I read the paper several times.

There were too many sentences on it.

I understood some of them.

I didn't understand some of them.

At the bottom:

Signature:

it said.

I took the pen.

The person next to me pointed to the ground with his finger.

"Here."

I signed it.

He took the paper.

"Ok."

He put it in the file.

“What did I do?”

I asked.

The man closed the file.

“You signed.”

Then I learned that the signatures were not the same.

Somewhere:

I approve.

Elsewhere:

It is within my knowledge.

In another document:

I received it.

Sometimes:

I did what was necessary.

There was the same line underneath them again.

Signature.

I was asked to sign the document in front of me. I read it: someone else held the power to approve, but my signature would appear beneath it. When I pointed this out, I was told the signature would be the same.

He handed me the pen.

I re-read the sentence at the bottom of the document.

It is within my knowledge.

“Is knowing the same thing as accepting?”

The man was silent for a moment.

“It doesn't say that here.”

“What does it say?”

“It is within my knowledge.”

I left the pen on the table.

Man:

“Aren't you going to sign?”

he said.

“First I want to understand what I signed.”

He took the document.

“Come later.”

A woman was holding another document with both hands.

“I signed this.”

He showed me the document.

“Then they changed it.”

He opened the first page.

Then he took out another piece of paper.

The two sentences were different from each other.

“The signature is mine on both.”

he said.

"Yes."

“Which one do I accept?”

I looked at the papers.

I didn't answer.

The woman showed her own signature.

“I recognize my signature.”

Then he showed the sentence.

“I don't recognize this.”

He put the documents back into his bag.

“I want to know what I wrote, not just the signature.”

Gone.

There was an old man.

He would sign very slowly.

He would put the paper in front of him, take the pen in his hand, and carefully draw the first letter.

Then he would wait.

He would move on to the second letter.

One day:

“Why are you typing so slowly?”

I asked.

The man completed his signature.

He did not give the paper immediately.

“To not forget my name.”

he said.

Seeing that I don't understand what I mean:

“To not forget yourself among the things you sign.”

he added.

He gave me the paper.

His signature was small.

But it took up more space on the paper than that.

I saw my own signature on a file.

I was remembering its history.

I also remembered the pen.

But when I looked at the sentence below it, I stopped.

It has been done accordingly.

The signature was mine.

History was mine.

The sentence was right above my signature.

I asked the person who prepared the file:

“Did I do this?”

“The signature is yours.”

“I'm not asking for the signature.”

The man looked at the file.

“What are you asking?”

“What was the point?”

He examined the paper for a while.

Then:

“It's written in the file.”

“What does it say?”

“What was necessary was done.”

He closed the file.

My name was in another file.

The signature was not mine.

“Who signed this?”

I asked.

“In your name.”

“I didn't sign it.”

“Your name is correct.”

“The signature is not mine.”

The man took the document in his hand.

He looked at the signature.

Then to the name.

“It needs to be fixed.”

he said.

"How?"

“A new document is prepared.”

“Old document?”

The man did not answer.

He put it back in the file.

The name was correct.

The signature was wrong.

Elsewhere the signature was correct.

But the sentence above the signature had changed.

I had prepared the work somewhere.

Someone else had signed it.

I signed it somewhere else.

But it was not clear which part of the job I was doing.

All of these could be in the same files.

One day, I prepared a document consisting of four parts.

Above:

Prepared by

controlling

Approved by

recipient

it said.

There was room for four different names.

There were more than four hands in the file.

Someone changed the sentence.

Someone had added a new row to the table.

Someone had fixed the bug.

Someone had redone the last page.

But there were only four names in the document.

The person next to me:

“Is there a problem?”

he said.

“Who did this job?”

“Four people.”

“Only four people?”

“There are four people in the document.”

“It's in the document.”

The man nodded.

"Yes."

He closed the file.

Then another document arrived.

Below:

I received it.

it said.

“Signature required.”

said the person who brought it.

“What did I receive?”

“The file.”

“I already see the file.”

“Sign.”

I took the pen in my hand.

“That I got the file?”

"Yes."

“That I accept the information in it?”

The man looked at me.

"No."

“So what does the signature show?”

“That you received it.”

I looked at the paper.

There was a blank line under the sentence.

I moved the pen closer to the line.

Then I pulled it back.

“I got the file.”

I said.

“I'll sign it later.”

The man took the file.

"However you like."

After a while, the same file came back.

This time there was another note on it:

It has been delivered.

Another name underneath.

I didn't have a name.

I opened the file.

Some of the pages inside had changed.

The signing places were the same.

But the texts were different.

There was a small correction mark in the corner of one page.

My handwriting next to it.

I made the correction.

I didn't have a signature.

At the end of the file was someone else's signature.

I looked at that page for a while.

Then I saw more clearly that a signature does not indicate the entirety of a work.

But work done without a signature could also remain invisible.

There was a place in between.

A woman left a document in front of me.

“There is my signature here.”

"Yes."

“There is nothing I did here.”

I looked at the document.

“You have your signature.”

“I know that.”

“What do you mean?”

“I did not write this sentence.”

“Who wrote it?”

"I don't know."

He showed his signature at the bottom of the document.

"Mine."

Then he showed the sentence above it.

“Not me.”

He left the paper to me.

“Sometimes a person looks at his own signature and reads the writing of a stranger.”

In a file:

Prepared by: blank.

Controlled by: name.

Approved by: name.

Receiver: name.

The prepared section was empty.

“Who will we write here?”

I asked.

“The one who prepared it.”

"Who?"

He looked at the file.

"I don't know."

“Who prepared the file?”

"I don't know."

“So why is it empty?”

The man squared his shoulders.

“If we find it, we write it down.”

I closed the file.

As I saw the pages containing the signatures, something else began to catch my attention.

A signature could connect a business to a person.

But not always what he does.

Sometimes just one sentence.

Sometimes to a delivery process.

Sometimes for information.

Sometimes a confirmation.

Sometimes to a result he had never seen before.

One day, a blank document came to me.

The upper part was not yet filled.

Below:

Signature:

it said.

I took the pen in my hand.

I did not sign.

“Why are you waiting?”

they asked.

“I don't know what to write.”

“Then it is filled.”

“Let it be filled first.”

“You have to sign.”

"Why?"

The man fell silent.

Then he took the document.

"Ok."

he said.

“Let's write first.”

The document has been filled out.

Sentences were written.

Eclairs were added.

Date added.

The signature area is at the bottom again.

This time I read the document cover to cover.

Once.

Then once again.

I took the pen.

I signed it.

Slowly.

I didn't put the pen down when I finished signing.

I looked at the sentence below.

This time I knew what I was signing.

But there was another question in the file.

Written in pencil on the margin of the page:

Who did it?

There was no answer.

I closed the file.

After a while the same man came.

“Did you sign it?”

"Yes."

"Ok."

He stretched out his hand to take the file.

Before giving the file, I asked:

“Where is the result of this work recorded?”

The man stopped.

"Conclusion?"

"Yes."

He looked at the file.

He didn't answer.

Then he took the file.

Only the pen remained on the table.

After a while someone else came.

He opened the document in front of him.

He looked at the signature.

He took the pen.

I looked at him.

He looked at me too.

“Here?”

he asked.

In the past:

“Yes.”

I would say.

This time I looked at the document.

"One minute."

I said.

I turned the document towards him.

“Let's see what it says first.”

The man started reading.

The pen was still in his hand.

So I waited across the table.

The signing place was waiting.
