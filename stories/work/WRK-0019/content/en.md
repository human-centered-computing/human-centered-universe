# Number

An institution was counting how many transactions were made for it. For a person who did not receive the results of his application, the same numbers showed the doors he had to come to more than once.

A chart was open on the screen.

There were names in the first column.

Work done in the second column.

Times in the third.

Numbers in the last column.

Next to a line:

72

it said.

Below:

68

In another line:

51

Next to:

43

I looked at the table for a while.

In one report, three applications from the same person were counted as three separate successes. The number was not wrong. After the first application, he did not get any results and came again. On his third visit, another employee listened to him. The supervisor who pointed out the rising line on the graph was pleased; I was also pleased at first moment. Then I thought about the path the man took each time he came. In our opinion, three processes; According to him, it was walking the same path three times. I did not delete the number from the report. Next to it, I wrote that it was a re-application.

Then the person at the next table said:

“We're doing well today.”

he said.

“According to what?”

He showed the numbers.

“Accordingly.”

The same table was projected on the screen at the meeting.

“There is a twelve percent increase compared to last period.”

said someone.

The line was going up on the chart.

“Higher than we expected.”

Someone:

“Good.”

he said.

I looked at the line on the screen.

“How did this number come about?”

“From the work done.”

"I know."

Nobody said anything else.

The meeting continued.

In a file:

Processing time: 4 minutes

it said.

In another file:

Processing time: 11 minutes

The first file was faster.

But the owner of the first file came back twice.

The owner of the second file came once.

Two transactions appeared in the first file.

One in the second file.

I looked at the table.

The faster line looked better.

When I looked at the files, I couldn't think the same thing.

I saw the doctor at lunchtime.

He had a list in his hand.

He compared the numbers on the list: seventy-eight people today, seventy-three yesterday. For him, the increase was result enough.

A patient came down the hall.

The doctor opened the file.

The man started talking.

The doctor asked a few questions.

Then he looked at the computer screen.

"One minute."

he said.

He looked at the people waiting outside the door.

He turned to the patient again.

The man started telling the same thing over again.

This time the doctor took notes.

After a while he got sick.

The doctor looked at the list.

He put a check in the box next to him.

Then:

“Seventy-nine.”

he said.

“One more person.”

In the evening I saw the same doctor in the corridor.

“Did you have a good day today?”

I asked.

“He passed according to the list.”

“According to the list?”

“Seventy-nine.”

Then he stopped.

“I listened to one of them twice.”

He wasn't looking at the list when he said this.

When I returned to my desk, the person who started working in the unit that day was preparing a table.

"What are you doing?"

“I'm counting jobs.”

"Why?"

“To see how many we made.”

"Later?"

“We will compare.”

“With what?”

“With yesterday.”

I looked at the table.

“Things done twice?”

“Two transactions.”

“What if a job is done twice?”

“Two again.”

“Is it really two jobs?”

He thought for a while.

"I don't know."

We opened a file.

The same person applied three times.

There were three separate recordings.

Three transaction numbers.

Three dates.

In the table:

Number of applications: 3

it said.

Newcomer:

“Three jobs.”

he said.

“There are three applications.”

“Are there three outcomes?”

We looked at the file.

In the first:

It has been concluded.

In the second:

He applied again.

In the third:

The investigation continues.

The newcomer looked at the painting.

“How do we count this?”

"I don't know."

After a while, he opened a new column.

Reapply

To the first file:

1

To the second:

1

To the third:

1

wrote.

Then he opened another column.

Have you seen results?

He left them all blank.

“Why is it empty?”

“We don't know.”

He nodded.

“This picture looks worse this way.”

"From where?"

“Because it no longer just shows what has been completed.”

I looked at the table.

"Yes."

The next day, a new table was opened at the meeting.

One person:

“The number of applications is high.”

he said.

Another:

“The number of transactions is also high.”

Then he looked at the new column.

Apply again.

The numbers underneath were visible.

"What are these?"

“Those who come again.”

“Why are they coming again?”

Nobody answered.

Someone:

“Too much detail for this painting.”

he said.

The newcomer looked at the painting.

“Still here.”

he said.

Everyone in the room fell silent.

After the meeting, the newcomer came up to me.

“Did I do wrong?”

"No."

“Someone got angry.”

"I know."

“Everything was easier when you looked at the numbers.”

"Yes."

“He's confused now.”

I looked at the table.

“Some things become easier when they are counted.”

"Some?"

“And some of them disappear.”

In the evening I found an old report on my desk.

Title:

Monthly Performance

Below:

Work completed: 1,284

Average processing time: 6.4 minutes

Backlog: 87

There was a small note at the bottom of the report:

Complaints: 19

On the next page:

Reapplication: 43

Further below:

Application with no results: —

There were no numbers next to the line.

I took the pen.

I stared into space.

Then I closed the report.

The next morning the new arrival brought a piece of paper.

There were three questions on it:

How many?

What happened?

Did it happen again?

He added a fourth question below:

What's left that doesn't count?

He left the paper on the table.

I looked at the questions.

Then I opened the table.

The numbers were spot on.

I couldn't say any of it was wrong.

There was a blank space under the last question on the paper.
