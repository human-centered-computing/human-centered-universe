# Place

The tables appeared to be no different from each other.

Same surface.

Same drawer.

Same chair.

Same lamp.

Still, no one was sitting at the wrong table.

One day I sat at one of the empty tables. I could see the tree in the garden from the window. The employee passing by me stopped and said, “That's the manager's office.” “The manager is not here.” “That's why it's empty.” I got up. What seemed strange to me at that moment was not that I was displaced. The chair was considered to belong to someone even when it was empty, and I could not have my own hours even though I was here every day. I did not say this thought; If I did, it would look like I was starting a fight over a chair.

Sometimes someone would approach someone else's chair, put their hand on the back, then give up. Even if the chair was empty, it was treated as if it belonged to him.

Once a newcomer sat at an empty table.

Nobody said anything right away.

The man opened his file. He took out his pen. He looked at the papers in front of him.

After a while, the woman at the table opposite raised her head.

“That's not your place.”

The man did not get up from the chair.

“Isn't it empty?”

The woman looked at the table.

"Empty."

Then he added in a slightly lower voice:

“But it's his.”

The man looked around.

“Who is here?”

The woman did not answer.

The man did not ask further.

He collected his file. He moved to the table next to him.

That table was smaller.

One leg of his chair was shaking slightly.

Nobody said anything.

My desk drawer was difficult to open.

He didn't have a key. I had to lift the drawer up a little and pull it towards me.

Inside were a few pens, two paper clip boxes, an old stapler, and a small key with nothing written on it.

One day I put the key on the table.

The man at the next table saw it.

“Don't use it.”

"From where?"

“It's sitting there.”

I put the key back in the drawer.

After a while someone else came.

He opened the drawer.

He saw the key.

“Is this still here?”

“Do you know?”

The man took the key in his hand.

He translated it.

“It belonged to a table.”

“Which one?”

He lifted his shoulders.

“No one knows which one anymore.”

He left the key in its place.

He closed the drawer.

He continued his work as if the matter had been resolved.

There were files floating around the tables.

If there was a name on the file, that name usually determined where the file would go.

Sometimes the file would end up on the wrong desk.

Sometimes, even though he came to the right table, no one would touch him.

Sometimes, when moving from one table to another, the order of the papers would change.

A file came before me.

It didn't have my name on it.

There was a sentence in it that I wrote.

There was someone else's signature under the sentence.

I closed the file.

I showed it to the person next to me.

“Where did this come from?”

He looked at the file.

"From here."

“Who prepared it?”

He turned the paper over.

He looked at the last page.

“Signature here.”

“It's someone else's signature.”

"Yes."

He handed me the file again.

“At your table.”

I received the file.

It stood on the table for a while.

Then he was sent to another table.

There was a table by the window.

Nobody was working at that desk.

But it wasn't completely empty either.

There was a glass on it. There was a dried line left inside the glass.

There was a pen.

It had no cover.

A few papers were stuck in a file.

On the top sheet of paper there was a single sentence:

“If we do it this way…”

The sentence ended there.

There was no continuation.

One day I approached that table.

I took the paper in my hand.

I looked at the pages below.

Some had drawings.

Some have numbers.

Several names on one page.

Just two words on another page:

“Think again.”

I looked at the table.

I pulled the chair a little.

I didn't sit down.

The man across the street looked at me.

“Are you going to work there?”

"No."

"Good morning my baby."

"From where?"

The man did not answer.

After a while:

“This place is not empty.”

“Who uses it?”

The man looked at the table.

“There is someone who knows.”

This time I didn't ask the question.

When my desk changed, they put the same file in front of me.

No one at the previous table had asked me.

Everyone here was asking.

The file was the same.

The questions had changed.

A woman placed the file in front of me.

“Can you check this?”

I looked at the file.

“Is it my duty?”

The woman sat in her chair.

“At your table.”

I looked at the file again.

“There was the same file on the previous table.”

"I know."

“No one asked me there.”

The woman raised her head.

“We ask here.”

I opened the file.

It contained the same documents.

Same dates.

Same signatures.

Same shortcomings.

I added a note at the end of the file.

“Checked.”

The woman looked at the paper.

“Why did you write this?”

“I checked.”

"Ok."

He took the file.

Gone.

One day I sat at someone else's table.

The chair was comfortable.

The table faced the window.

There was a computer in front of me.

There were three files on the table.

After a while, someone came in and stopped when he saw me.

“Do you work here?”

"I am her."

The man looked at the table.

Then to me.

“This is his table.”

"Who?"

The man did not answer.

He took one of the files.

“Leave that to him.”

“Will he come?”

The man put the file back on the table.

“He won't come.”

"Then?"

“The file will stay here.”

Some people's tables remained after they left.

The table was emptied, files were distributed to other places, pens were put in drawers, glasses were put away.

But sometimes something remained.

A scratch.

A tape mark.

An unerased ink stain on the edge of the table.

A small tear in the chair back.

Nobody found these important.

However, when a new person sat down, he would sometimes put his hand on that mark.

Maybe he didn't know why he did it.

One day, I put my finger through the scratch on the edge of the table.

It wasn't very deep.

It looked like it was made with nails.

The person next to me saw it.

"What happened?"

“A scratch.”

"It's not important."

“Who did it?”

“Nobody remembers.”

Then he returned to his file.

The scratch remained on the table.

The tables he passed through were listed in a file.

Numbers.

Names.

Signatures.

Recipients.

Those who sent it back.

Those who control.

As I turned the page, the number of places the file was visited increased.

From one table to another.

From one drawer to another.

From one signature to another.

It was clear where the file went.

But it was difficult to find out who put down the first card.

There were a few names on the last page.

Some of them had signatures next to them.

Besides some, it's just history.

One line was left blank.

I looked into that space.

I took the pen in my hand.

I didn't write.

I closed the file.

One day, another chair was brought to my table.

“Who will sit here?” I asked.

"I don't know."

They put the chair next to the table.

It remained empty for a while.

Then someone came.

He had a few files in his hand.

“Can I sit here?”

"You know best."

He looked at the table.

Then to the chair.

“Is this yours?”

“I'm here right now.”

The man pulled out the chair.

He looked at the line at the edge of the table before sitting down.

He ran his finger over it.

"What's this?"

“A scratch.”

“Who did it?”

“Nobody remembers.”

The man nodded.

He left his files on the table.

Then he asked again:

“Is this place empty?”

I looked at the table.

File.

Pen.

Glass.

Drawr.

I didn't answer.

The man did not sit either.
