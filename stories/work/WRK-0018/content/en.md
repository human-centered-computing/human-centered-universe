# Result

The applicant was waiting at the door. Did the stamp on the last page of the file give the answer he expected? When I opened the file on my desk, I saw that these two results were considered the same thing.

On the last page of the file:

The transaction is completed.

it said.

There was a signature underneath.

There was history.

There was the name of the person controlling it.

In the next line:

It has been done accordingly.

it said.

I closed the file.

There was another file on my desk.

There was the same sentence on its last page.

A man came in the afternoon.

He left the file on the table.

“Is it over?”

I opened the file.

“It's complete here.”

“I'm not asking that.”

“What are you asking?”

"What happened?"

I looked at the file again.

There were transactions.

There were dates.

There were signatures.

“It's not written here.”

Man:

“They didn't tell me either.”

he said.

He got the file back.

In another file:

The process is completed.

it said.

There was a small note next to it:

He applied again.

I opened the file.

I saw that the same person applied before.

There were two dates.

Two transaction records.

Two separate signatures.

In the conclusion:

It has been done accordingly.

it said.

I looked at the new application date.

There wasn't much time in between.

The new person I was working with that day came to my desk.

“A file has been completed.”

he said.

"Yes."

“So what happened?”

“It's done.”

“This is not the answer.”

"I know."

He looked at the file.

“Then why does it say so here?”

“Because the transaction has been made.”

“Does the result occur when the procedure is performed?”

I was silent for a while.

"I don't know."

At the meeting, the files were lined up on the table.

One person:

“All transactions have been completed.”

he said.

Another:

“How many files?”

“One hundred and twenty.”

“How many were completed?”

“One hundred and twenty.”

“Where are the results?”

They looked at the files.

Nobody answered.

Someone:

“Marked as finalized.”

he said.

“I'm not asking about that.”

The silence in the room stretched.

Then another file was opened.

Inside the file:

Application has been received.

It has been examined.

It has been evaluated.

Approved.

It has been sent to the relevant unit.

It has been done accordingly.

It was all there.

On the last page there was a small field:

Result:

The bottom was empty.

The newcomer ran his finger over the gap.

“What should be written here?”

"I don't know."

“Who will write it?”

"I don't know."

“So why does this field exist?”

This time I didn't answer.

I saw the same man again at lunchtime.

“What happened to my file?”

“Transaction completed.”

“I know that.”

"Another?"

“Nothing has happened to me yet.”

He was silent for a while.

“I came again.”

"I saw."

“Because I didn't get results last time either.”

I opened the file.

Next to the first application:

It has been concluded.

it said.

The man looked at the file.

“I didn't know it was finalized.”

“It says here.”

“I'm not here.”

He closed the file.

“I'm out.”

I stared at the door for a long time after the man left. I knew why I found it so easy to tell him “case closed.” It was saving me from a question I didn't know the answer to. The file might not have been lying; There were traces of work being done inside. But I was the one who put it in place of the change the applicant would experience. That day, for the first time, I thought that I could hide my part by talking about the evil of the system we had established. I went to the door and called the man back. I said I would investigate the outcome. He didn't ask for any assurance from me; He just wrote my name on a piece of paper.

I thought about this sentence for a long time.

Everything was completed in the file.

The man was still outside.

The file and the man were not saying the same thing.

After a while, another file arrived.

This time on the last page:

Result: The applicant was informed.

it said.

The applicant came back a few days later.

“Who gave me information?”

he asked.

I looked at the file.

There was no name.

"I don't know."

“I didn't talk to anyone.”

“It says that information was given in the file.”

“Nobody talked to me.”

He remained silent for a while.

“So whose result is this?”

I looked at the file.

There was no answer.

When I returned to my desk in the evening, there were three files in front of me.

In one:

Completed.

In the other:

It has been concluded.

In the third:

It has been done accordingly.

I opened the last page of all three.

There was a space in the first file.

There was another application in the second file.

There was no signature of the applicant in the third file.

I put the three side by side.

I took the pen.

To the edge of the first file:

What has changed?

I wrote.

To the second:

Who saw it?

To the third:

Who did it reach?

I looked at the three of them for a while.

Then I put down the pen.

The next day the newcomer came to my table.

In his hand was the file we looked at together yesterday.

“I added something.”

he said.

He opened the file.

There was a small gap on the last page.

Above:

Conclusion

it said.

He added a single question below:

How do we know?

“Why did you write this?”

I asked.

“Because what is in the file may not be the same as what is outside.”

I looked at the file.

I remained silent for a while.

“Leave this in the file.”

I said.

When the file arrived again, there was no answer to the question.

Conclusion

The field was standing.

Below:

How do we know?

it said.

The answer was still blank.

I kept the last page of the file open for a while.

Then I didn't turn it off.

I left it on the table.

I looked at the Completed stamp on it.

This time I turned the page.
