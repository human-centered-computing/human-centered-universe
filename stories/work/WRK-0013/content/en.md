# Missing

There was a red stamp on the first page of the file.

MISSING

The stamp was so large that some of the writing underneath was not visible.

It was easy to tell the owner of the file what was missing; The list was in front of me. It was harder to say why we still couldn't do the job he expected because of the incomplete record. “I will bring a document,” he said, “and then will it be over?” I opened the papers again. The space showing the answer to this was empty. I could send him out the door on a new mission. This time, I asked the supervisor before leaving. “We don't need to know that now,” he said. The man was still standing in the corridor. I returned to my desk, thinking for whom it was not necessary.

The man who brought me the file:

“What's missing?”

he asked.

I opened the file.

There was an application on the first page.

Adds on the second page.

Identity information on the third page.

Signature on page four.

Date, approval, control and delivery records on the following pages.

Everything was in place.

"I don't know."

I said.

Man:

“They didn't tell me either.”

We took the file to the relevant table.

The person at the desk looked at the stamp.

"Missing."

he said.

"What is missing?"

He opened the file.

He turned the pages.

“It says here.”

“What does it say?”

"Missing."

“What's missing?”

The man looked at the first page again.

"I don't know."

“So why is it missing?”

“Because it's missing.”

He closed the file.

“Complete.”

The man took the file.

He went out into the corridor.

He came back after a while.

There was another paper next to him.

“I added this too.”

I opened the file.

He put the new paper at the back.

"Now?"

I looked at the first page.

MISSING

The mark was still there.

The man took out the paper again.

“What else should I add?”

"I don't know."

The file was talked about at the table during lunch.

“Some files are like this.”

said someone.

"How?"

"Missing."

"What is missing?"

“You never know.”

“How is it completed?”

The man stirred his tea.

“One day it will be completed.”

“What is changing?”

The man did not answer.

Another woman came to my table.

He had a thick file in his hand.

“They said this was missing too.”

He opened the file.

There were pages inside.

Lots of pages.

Adds.

Reports.

Photos.

Signatures.

Woman:

“I don't understand what they want.”

he said.

“Did they tell you?”

“They said.”

“What did they say?”

“That it was missing.”

"Another?"

"No."

The woman closed the file.

“I no longer think about what I'm missing.”

“What do you think?”

“What the file wants from me.”

After a while I saw a small form.

At the top:

Missing document:

it said.

An empty line underneath.

I received the form.

For the first time there was a place to write down what was missing.

But it was empty.

I put the form into the file.

In the evening the same woman came back.

“I found something.”

he said.

"What?"

“An old file.”

He put the papers in front of me.

On the first page:

Missing: Description

it said.

Woman:

“They want this.”

“What explanation?”

"I don't know."

“Who do they want from?”

"From me."

“What will you explain?”

The woman remained silent for a long time.

"I don't know."

In another file just:

Missing: —

it said.

There was nothing next to the line.

Person who brought the file:

“What does this mean?”

he asked.

"I don't know."

“How will this be completed?”

I didn't answer.

He closed the file.

The new employee came to my desk.

"What does this mean?"

he asked.

He showed the file.

"I don't know."

“Why doesn't it say what's missing here?”

"I don't know."

“So how will we complete it?”

I looked at the file.

“Maybe we need to know what's missing first.”

I looked up.

"Yes."

The next day the file arrived again.

The first page had the same red stamp.

MISSING

This time a small section was opened underneath:

What is missing:

It was empty.

Person who brought the file:

“They told me to fill this place.”

he said.

“What will you write?”

"I don't know."

He took the pen in his hand.

He looked at the empty space.

Then he left the pen on the table.

“I just want my job done.”

I looked at the file.

This sentence was not written anywhere.

There were other things the woman said.

There was history.

There was an application.

There were documents.

There were signatures.

But this sentence was not there.

Before closing the file in the evening, I looked at the first page again.

MISSING

There was a stamp.

Below:

What is missing:

it said.

It was still empty.

I took the pen.

I waited for a while.

Then I put the pen down without writing anything.

I closed the file.

The stamp remained under the cover.
