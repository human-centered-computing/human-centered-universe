# Desk

The location of the tables in a room also determined how far people's words could reach. Just because two people doing the same job sat close to each other did not mean they could say the same thing.

The tables looked the same.

Same width.

Same color.

Same drawers.

The same computers, the same files, the same pens on them.

But people knew where the tables were.

The man at the next table never objected to the work. He would finish everything requested early and look at the files in front of me in the remaining time. One day I suggested a small solution; He listened and said, “Maybe, but they didn't give it to me.” “It will work.” "I know." Then he added: "If I do the job given to me poorly, it is my fault. If I do the job that was not given to me well, they will ask why I did it." He wasn't saying this out of laziness. He had his house rent and his son's school bus. He saw the table order as well as I did, yet he chose to stay within it. That day I realized how comfortable I was in judging him in my own way.

Even a table where no one was sitting could tell who it belonged to.

One day they changed places.

No one explained why.

My desk was moved closer to the window.

Another person was sitting in front of the window before.

There was a small scratch on the table.

I found a dried pen tip in the drawer.

And a folded paper.

I didn't open it.

When they put the first file in front of me in my new place, I did not understand the question.

“Examine this.”

said someone.

I opened the file.

I've seen the same file before.

He passed me when I was at my old table.

Then just:

“Checked.”

I wrote.

Now the person who opened the file sat across from me.

"What are you thinking?"

he asked.

"What about?"

“About the file.”

I looked at the file.

Same pages.

Same signatures.

Same dates.

I said I had checked the same work before. I would check it again now, though I did not know whether the file had changed.

The man nodded.

“Your view has changed.”

I didn't understand why.

The meeting call came towards noon.

When I entered the room, the tables were arranged differently.

There was a long table.

Everyone's place was clear.

Some of them had files in front of them.

Some people only have paper in front of them.

I sat in an empty chair.

The meeting has started.

A woman opened the file.

“There is a problem here.”

he said.

A man:

“No, it's okay.”

he said.

Woman:

“This conclusion is not correct.”

Man:

“The file is complete.”

The woman fell silent.

Then he looked at me.

"What do you think?"

I didn't know what to say for a moment.

“I need to look at the file.”

I said.

The man immediately said:

“The file is here.”

he said.

The woman handed me the file.

I opened it.

I read for a while.

“The action taken is written here.”

I said.

“Is there a result written?”

the woman asked.

I turned the pages.

"No."

Man:

“The result is already clear.”

he said.

The woman looked at the file.

“I don't think so.”

Nobody said anything.

After the meeting, the same woman approached me in the hallway.

“Would you say that if you were at your old desk?”

he asked.

"I don't know."

“I know.”

“What do you know?”

“Probably not.”

"Why?"

The woman lifted her shoulders.

“No one was asking you there.”

I returned to my desk.

The old paper I took out of the drawer was still there.

I opened it.

There was an unfinished sentence inside:

If we do it this way...

There was no continuation.

I turned the paper over.

With someone else's writing on the back:

Think again.

it said.

I didn't know who wrote it.

Another meeting was held the next day.

This time everyone was sitting somewhere else.

The file has been opened.

A suggestion was discussed.

The person who made the suggestion was sitting at the end of the table.

“If we do it this way, we'll wait less.”

he said.

Someone at the table:

“It's been tried before.”

he said.

"What happened?"

"It didn't happen."

"Why?"

“That's what I was told.”

The person who made the suggestion fell silent.

After a while:

“Any other suggestions?”

It was said.

Nobody spoke.

After the meeting ended, I saw the same suggestion on the side of the file.

The sentence spoken at the meeting was longer.

Only three words remained in the file:

Waiting time will be reduced.

Question mark next to it.

Below it is another handwritten text:

Does it work?

Later:

Let it be tried.

I looked for a while.

The sentence on the paper was similar to the sentence I heard in the meeting.

But it wasn't the same.

Someone came to my table this afternoon.

He was a new employee.

He had a file in his hand.

“Do I leave it here?”

he said.

"Yes."

He left the file on my desk.

Then he pulled out his chair.

"Can I ask something?"

"Of course."

“What do I need to do with this file?”

I looked at the file.

“It says here.”

"I read."

“What didn't you understand?”

“What should happen in the end.”

I opened the file again.

There were instructions on the first page.

Process steps on the second page.

Check boxes on the third page.

Signature section on the last page.

I looked for a while longer.

"I don't know."

I said.

The employee was surprised.

“You've been working here longer.”

"Yes."

“How don't you know?”

I closed the file.

“Maybe we should ask.”

The next day the same employee came again.

He had a paper in his hand.

“I wrote down what you said yesterday.”

he said.

He handed me the paper.

On:

What should happen in the end

it said.

One sentence below:

Clearly informing the applicant of what will be done.

“Can we put this in the file?”

he asked.

I looked at the paper.

“Let's ask first.”

“From whom?”

"I don't know."

There were several files on the table.

In one:

He will rest.

it said.

It was not clear who would listen underneath.

In another file:

Necessary action will be taken.

There was no need.

In a third file just:

It is expected.

it said.

It was not clear who was waiting.

One day I went to my old desk.

Someone else was sitting now.

The scratch on the table was still there.

There was no dried pen tip in the drawer.

There was a new pen.

“This table used to be mine.”

I said.

The man looked up.

"Really?"

"Yes."

“I didn't know.”

I looked at the table.

“It's hard to sit by the window.”

he said.

"From where?"

“People are always looking out.”

“Are you looking?”

"Sometimes."

Then he handed me a file.

“Will you look at this?”

I received the file.

There was a sentence on the first page.

It looks familiar.

But I couldn't figure out who wrote it.

When I returned to my desk in the evening, there was a file in front of me.

Meeting notes.

Who sat where?

Who spoke?

Who answered?

Who signed it?

There was a small note next to the decision on the last page:

Whose life will this decision change?

There was no name under the note.

I turned the paper over.

There was another sentence behind it:

Listen.

I moved the chair back a little.

Someone's voice came from the corridor.

"Can I ask something?"

I looked up.
