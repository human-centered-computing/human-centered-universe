# WRK-0011 — The Table

At mealtime everyone changed.

I did not understand this in the first years.

When we sat at the table, the files became silent.

For a while the clock lost its importance.

Someone talked about a child.

Someone about home.

Someone about money.

Someone about illness.

A man broke his bread in two.

Another shared his soup.

Nobody counted these things.

Nobody asked:

“How many people did you help today?”

Perhaps that was why it was easier to talk.

One day the old man asked:

“When do you remember most clearly that you are a human being?”

“At meals.”

“Me too.”

He was silent for a while.

“After the work is finished too.”

“Why?”

“Because then nobody is asking you for anything.”

Just then the bell rang.

Everyone stood.

Chairs moved.

Conversations ended halfway through.

People returned to their places.

Faces that had been smiling became serious again.

That day I understood:

Sometimes all it takes for a person to become visible as a person is for the system to stop measuring them for a while.
