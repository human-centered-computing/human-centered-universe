# Applause

Completed work was visible on the screen in the meeting room. Before entering the hall, a woman showed me the crumpled piece of paper on which she had previously written her suggested method. There were traces of that method on the screen; On paper, it is a beginning that no one has named yet.

When the meeting ended, no one got up immediately.

The last chart was open on the screen.

The lines were full.

There were numbers.

There was work completed.

There was work waiting.

A green sign burned in one corner.

“It happened.”

said someone.

There was a short silence.

Then a voice came from the back of the room:

“Well done.”

One person applauded.

Then another.

Then a few more people.

So I clapped my hands together.

In the middle of the applause, the chief put his hand on my shoulder. “We did it as a team,” he said. There was something right about this. The painting would not work without the corrections of others. But the word “team” did not change the name they showed when they asked who brought the idea. Still, I kept applauding. It is strange that someone who has been treated unfairly hears the sound of his own hands at the award ceremony; I learned that day. I didn't tell anyone. If I told you, it would be called jealousy, and I would believe it after a few hours.

I didn't know exactly what we were applauding for.

I looked at the table on the screen again.

There were jobs completed in one line.

Durations on another line.

Comparison with previous period elsewhere.

Everything seemed measured.

At the meeting, no one mentioned old files.

Nor the papers waiting on anyone's desk.

Only the result was on the screen.

"How many?"

he was asked.

A number was said.

“How soon?”

Another number.

“Compared to before?”

One percent.

Then he was applauded again.

The same topic was discussed during lunch.

“The President was satisfied.”

said someone.

“The team worked well.”

Another:

“That friend in particular tried so hard.”

he said.

The person in question was not at the table.

After a while, someone else's name was mentioned.

He wasn't there either.

The names spoken have changed.

The work done has not changed.

The papers remained on the table.

One day I went to another room.

There was a doctor inside.

There was a long list in front of him.

Someone compared the numbers on the list: seventy-two people today, sixty-eight yesterday. He took the increase as a good sign.

The doctor looked up.

“Is he okay?”

The person in front of him looked at the list.

“The number increased.”

The doctor did not answer.

He opened one of the files in front of him.

“How long did you meet with this patient?”

“It took a long time.”

"How much?"

The doctor looked at the clock.

"I don't know."

There was a knock on the door from outside.

The person inside was still talking.

Doctor:

“Tell me more.”

he said.

The number of people waiting behind the door was increasing.

After a while, I looked at the list in front of the doctor again.

The number had increased.

His pen never left his hand.

He was closing one file and opening another.

He would put a small sign next to each closed file.

One person came out.

Someone else entered.

The list progressed.

At one point, the doctor closed the door.

The person inside talked for a long time.

One of the people waiting in the corridor looked at the clock.

Then someone else.

The doctor continued to stay inside.

The list was not progressing at that time.

At the next meeting, the same table was reopened.

The line was going up on the chart.

“There is a serious increase here.”

said someone.

Everyone looked at the screen.

“We can put that in the report.”

Another:

“Okay.”

he said.

As I looked at the chart, I thought about the doctor's table.

Then the people waiting.

Then the closed files.

I didn't know in which line it was written that a person should rest for a long time.

Nobody asked.

After the meeting, a file was left on my desk.

On the first page:

expected result

it said.

There were a few lines underneath.

There was a small section opened on the last page.

Contribution

Some of its places were filled.

Some were empty.

There was a name on one line.

Next to:

idea

In another line:

Application

Two names.

Elsewhere only:

correction

it said.

There was no name.

Person who brought the file:

“These will be completed later.”

he said.

“Who will complete it?”

"I don't know."

“So why did they put it in now?”

“Someone asked.”

“What did he ask?”

The man looked at the file.

“Who did it?”

I closed the file.

I thought of old paper.

The old piece of paper an employee showed me before the meeting.

There was no name next to the first sentence.

Then someone developed it.

Someone else did it.

Someone else corrected it.

At the end of the study, everyone only looked at the result.

In the evening, I met the woman who hid that paper in the corridor.

He had the old paper in his hand again.

"Have you seen?"

he said.

"What?"

“The result is out.”

"Yes."

“Did they applaud?”

“They applauded.”

The woman smiled.

"Good morning my baby."

Then he opened the paper.

“I kept this too.”

"Why?"

“Because it existed before the results came out.”

I looked at the paper.

The edges were worn.

Some lines were deleted.

There was a note somewhere that I added later.

The woman pointed to it.

“You wrote this.”

"Yes."

“I didn't remember that.”

He remained silent for a while.

“I remember now.”

He closed the paper.

Another question was asked at the meeting the next day.

Names appeared on the screen.

There were long lists next to some names.

Just a few lines next to some.

Someone:

“According to this list…”

he said.

He didn't finish his sentence.

Another:

“The list does not show everything.”

he said.

Nobody objected.

The names on the screen have not changed.

I reopened the file.

The contribution section was still not completed.

In one line:

idea

In one:

Development

In one:

Application

In one:

correction

One line opposite was blank.

Next to:

Source could not be verified.

it said.

Nobody had deleted that line.

My pen hit the table as I closed the file.

A small voice came out.

At that moment, applause came from the corridor again.

This time it was short lived.

I didn't get up.

There was old paper on my desk.

The new file is next to it.

The corner of one of them was curled.

The other one's pages were still clean.

They both had one of the same sentences.

One is in old handwriting.

The other is on the computer.

One of them had a name next to it.

The other one didn't have it.

It was not enough to look at the papers to understand which came first.

I took the pen.

I wrote a question in the margin of the new file:

Who heard?

I looked for a while.

Then I put down the pen.

The file remained open.

The applause coming from the corridor stopped.

In the silence in the room, only the sound of the pages touching each other remained.
