# The Corridor

I eventually memorized the corridor.

Which door opened when.

Where people waited.

Where they spoke.

Where they became silent.

When I was called somewhere, I no longer thought about the route.

My feet knew it.

One day a young person walked beside me.

“Where are we going?”

“I do not know.”

“How long will it take?”

“I do not know.”

We kept walking.

Through one door.

Into another corridor.

Through another door.

We were carrying a file.

The file moved from one room to another.

Eventually it returned to the first room.

Nothing had changed.

Only we were tired.

The corridor created movement.

Movement looked like progress.

At the end, an old man was sitting.

“How long have you been here?”

“A long time.”

“Waiting for what?”

“To be called.”

“Who will call you?”

He looked up.

“I do not know.”

I walked past him.

After some time I found myself in the same place.

Then I thought the corridor was not only between rooms.

Sometimes it stretched inside a person.

You could keep returning to the same place and still believe you had moved forward.
