# The One Who Remembers

The table I saw from the window of the old building reminded me of what was once said there. The same surface might have been just another work day for another employee.

While passing by the old building, I saw one of the tables inside.

It wasn't right next to the window.

It was a little bit in there.

There were three files on it.

The woman working that desk had written her own question on the back of a chart so she could find out what happened to people's applications. I saw that this question was included in someone else's presentation at the meeting, and I did not mention the woman's name. One day, the woman took out the paper and showed her own handwriting. Since I remained silent at that moment, it would be easy to remember him today only as someone whose rights were violated. He came back to ask the question again; I didn't stand next to him the first time. I remembered this when I saw the table. Now I knew better that I should write down who said the words in a new file.

A pen.

One glass.

And a chair turned towards the wall.

I looked.

I recognized the table.

I couldn't immediately figure out where I knew him from.

Then I remembered the small scratch on the edge of the drawer.

The same scratch was still there.

I stood in front of the window for a while.

Someone sat at the table inside.

He opened the drawer.

He looked for something.

Then he took one of the files.

He arranged the papers on the table.

I didn't think of any of this as belonging to me.

But I knew the table.

The next day, I received an old file.

I didn't remember who sent it to me.

There was a name on the cover.

It wasn't my name.

I opened the file.

Date on the first page.

Reference on the second page.

Notes on following pages.

Control.

Signature.

Corrections.

A small sign next to a sentence.

I read the sentence.

I remembered.

I wrote it.

There was another handwriting on the margin of the page:

Look again.

I remembered that too.

On another page:

There may be a problem here.

This was from me too.

There was no name in the file.

I closed the file.

Then I opened it again.

That day, the new person I was working with came to me.

I showed the newcomer a sentence I had written in the file. He looked at it for a while.

“You have no name.”

"I know."

“How do you know for sure?”

I thought for a while.

“I remember how I thought.”

He looked at the file.

“Shall we change this?”

"No."

"From where?"

“Let's see if it's available elsewhere first.”

We found the same sentence in another file.

There was no name there either.

There was a modified version of the same idea in another file.

One word was omitted.

Another word was added.

Then a correction was made.

Newcomer:

“Are these all yours?” he asked.

"No."

“Which one is yours?”

“First sentence.”

“Next ones?”

"I don't know."

“So how do we separate this?”

I looked at the file.

“Maybe we won't.”

We sat at the same table during lunch.

The new person put the file in front of him.

“What happens if a person doesn't remember what he did?”

“Sometimes the file remembers.”

“How will the file remember?”

“It stays inside.”

“Without a name?”

“Even without a name.”

He was silent for a while.

“Is this enough?”

I thought about the question.

"I don't know."

I opened the file.

I looked at the first sentence again.

I recognized my own handwriting.

But knowing was not the same as proving.

I didn't write this in the file.

In the evening, one of the former employees came to the building.

We met in the corridor.

He stopped when he saw me.

“Were you here?”

"Before."

"Now?"

“I'm out.”

He smiled.

“How's it going out?”

"Strange."

We talked for a while.

Then I showed him the file.

“Do you remember this?”

He looked at the file.

After a while he smiled.

“I remember.”

“Who wrote it?”

He looked at the page.

"You."

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

"How?"

“You said it at the meeting.”

I closed the file.

“There is no name in the file.”

"I know."

“How do you remember?”

He lifted his shoulders.

“Because I was there.”

He said it that simple.

Then he started talking about something else.

I still had the file.

The new person came to us.

“He wrote the sentence in this file.”

The former employee looked at the file.

"Yes."

“How do you know this?”

“I remember.”

The newcomer looked at me.

“Should we add this to the file?”

I thought for a while.

“Let's add it.”

He put a little note at the end of the file:

First suggestion — source verified.

He wrote my name underneath.

The former employee looked at the page.

“Then someone else changed it.”

The newcomer looked at me.

“Should we write that too?”

“Let's write.”

Another name added.

“There were also those who implemented it.”

We couldn't remember them one by one.

Only one record regarding the application was found in the file.

The newcomer also added this.

In one line:

Contribution whose source cannot be verified.

it said.

It was empty.

Newcomer:

“Should we delete this?” he asked.

"No."

"From where?"

“If we don't know, we don't know.”

He put down his pen.

The next day the file arrived on my desk.

There were new notes.

First sentence.

Next change.

Name with verified source.

Application registration.

But some things were still clear only from records.

Some of them were remembered even though no one had written them down.

I closed the file.

Newcomer:

“Okay now?” he asked.

I looked at the file.

"No."

“What's missing?”

"I don't know."

This time he smiled.

“Shall we write this?”

"No."

"From where?"

“We don't know what we don't know yet.”

He left the file on the table.

As I was leaving the building, I passed the old desk.

The scratch was still there.

There was another file on the table.

Another glass.

Another pen.

Another person.

I stopped for a moment.

The scratch was the same.

The table had not changed.

The person had changed.

Someone inside called out:

“Are you looking for something?”

"No."

"Then?"

I looked at the scratch.

“I remember.”

"What?"

I didn't answer for a while.

Then:

“One sentence.”

The person inside didn't understand.

I didn't explain either.

I did not approach the table.

I looked at the scratch again.

Then I walked.

There's a table behind me.

The file remained.

The sentence remained.

And there is someone left who remembers him.
