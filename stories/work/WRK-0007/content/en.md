# Wall

At first I didn't understand where the wall started.

He didn't appear in front of me as I walked around the room.

I was passing through the tables.

I was carrying files.

I was going in and out of the doors.

Then one day, I realized that I was standing in a place that everyone knew but no one had described.

During lunch, an employee said from the other side of the wall: "When the work that is discussed there comes to our table, it becomes someone's opinion." He smiled as he said this. A smile appeared in me too; because I knew immediately who he meant. Then I felt ashamed of myself. We explained the order without naming names, and by explaining it, we obeyed it. When we heard the supervisor's steps from the corridor, we both started to stir our teas. The impact of the spoon on the glass filled the space left by the conversation.

There was a wall in front of me.

There were tables on one side of the wall.

The other side was not visible.

The top part went up to the ceiling.

It had no windows.

There was no writing on it.

Just the wall.

The man next to me:

“What's on the other side?”

he asked.

"I don't know."

“Is there a door?”

I looked at the wall.

"I didn't see."

The man nodded.

“Then there is no need.”

He walked.

After a while, I noticed that the files had moved to the other side of the wall.

The file was taken from a table.

It was left in a compartment next to the wall.

Then he would come back from somewhere else.

Sometimes there would be a new note on it.

Sometimes a new signature.

Sometimes it's just another date.

We knew where the file came from.

And where you're going.

But no one was explaining the part in between.

A woman carrying a thick file wanted to deliver it herself. The clerk invoked procedure and refused; when she asked what would happen if she did, he merely repeated himself.

The woman waited for a while.

“My file.”

"Yes."

“There's stuff in there about me.”

"I know."

“Then why can't I take it?”

Nobody answered.

The woman left the file on the table.

The file was placed in the compartment next to the wall.

It disappeared after a while.

The woman looked after her.

There were voices on both sides of the wall.

Sometimes conversations could be heard.

The voice of one side would reach the other side.

But the words were always incomprehensible.

One day, a voice came from the other side.

“This file is missing.”

The man here:

“It's not missing.”

he replied.

The voice came again.

"Missing."

“There is everything.”

There was silence for a while.

Then:

“Send it back.”

The file is back.

There was only one word on it:

MISSING

The woman opened the file.

He checked the pages one by one.

They were all there.

"What is missing?"

he asked.

The man looked at the file.

“That's what it says.”

After a while, a small piece of paper was hung on the wall.

On:

It is not necessary to go to the other side of the wall.

it said.

I didn't see who hung the paper.

Nobody asked.

Sometimes a long queue would form near the wall.

People would hand over their files.

An officer would take it.

Then:

“Wait.”

he would say.

No one would approach the wall.

As a child, I wondered what was behind a wall.

Here, people were not curious.

At least it wasn't obvious.

One day a man came too close to the wall.

He placed his hand on its surface.

"Thick."

he said.

“How do you know?”

“From your voice.”

He hit the wall.

A deep voice came out.

Then he put his ear to it.

“There's a table on the other side, too.”

“How do you know?”

“There's a sound coming.”

He listened for a while.

“Human voice.”

An officer saw him.

“What are you doing here?”

“I'm looking.”

"Whereat?"

“To the wall.”

"There's no need."

The man withdrew his hand.

"Ok."

Gone.

One day I saw the same man again.

This time he was sitting next to the wall.

“I'm not trying to get by.”

he said when he saw me.

"What are you doing?"

"I am listening."

“What do you hear?”

“Sometimes something.”

"What?"

"I don't know."

He put his ear to the wall again.

So I stood next to him for a while.

It was really loud.

Something was being moved.

A chair was being pulled out.

A door was closing.

Someone was talking.

I couldn't make out the words.

A file arrived.

On:

It has been forwarded to the other party.

it said.

“Who is the other party?”

I asked.

Man:

“Other side.”

he said.

“Doesn't it have a name?”

“That's what it says here.”

I opened the file.

There was another paper inside.

Necessary evaluation will be made.

There was no name underneath.

I closed the file.

There was a small window next to the wall.

I hadn't noticed before.

The window did not open.

It was just showing the two sides to each other.

There were other tables opposite.

Other people.

Other files.

A man was sitting in front of the computer.

A woman was organizing papers.

Another stood up and carried a file.

They saw us.

We saw them.

Nobody waved.

The woman standing in front of the window:

“They are just like us.”

he said.

"Who?"

“Those over there.”

"I don't know."

“Their tables are the same.”

It was really the same.

Table.

Chair.

File.

Computer.

Pen.

We looked for a while.

Then we left in front of the window.

One day I saw that there was a door in front of the wall.

I hadn't noticed before.

Or I didn't know it was there.

There was no writing on the door.

He had an arm.

There was no keyhole.

Someone stopped in front of the door.

He put his hand on the arm.

Then he pulled back.

"What happened?"

I asked.

"I don't know."

“You didn't try.”

“Trying is different.”

He looked at the door.

“What if it opens?”

The door opened.

Someone came out.

He had a file in his hand.

He looked at us.

“Where do I leave this?”

he asked.

Nobody answered.

The man put the file on the table.

He passed through the door again.

The door is closed.

After a while, no one answered the door.

A woman was waiting by the wall.

He had a thin file in his hand.

“I need to go to the other side.”

Officer:

“There is no need.”

he said.

“I need to take the file.”

“Sent from here.”

“They called me.”

“Sent from here.”

“Who called?”

The officer took the file.

“Other side.”

The woman smiled.

“And I wanted to find out who the other party was…”

He didn't finish his sentence.

The officer put the file in the compartment.

The woman did not leave her place for a while.

Then he left.

A new note was hung on the wall.

This time just:

DO NOT PASS.

There was no signature underneath.

Someone read the note and said:

“Who wrote it?”

he said.

Another:

“It doesn't matter.”

he said.

“What if it's important?”

“The wall is here.”

The man walked.

One day I saw a small sentence on the edge of one of the files.

“If we do it this way…”

The sentence ended there.

There was no continuation.

It was not clear who wrote the file.

I looked at the sentence for a while.

Then I closed the file.

I thought about what separates the two sides of the wall.

Voices were passing.

Files were passing.

Instructions were passing.

Signatures were being passed.

People were staying in their places.

One day I stood in front of the window again.

A man was sitting in front of the computer on the opposite side.

There was a file in front of him.

He turned the cover of the file.

He opened the first page.

The cover of the file in front of me was also open.

I couldn't read the title.

The man across from him raised his head.

We looked at each other.

Then we both turned to the next page.

When I came to the wall, there was a file on the floor.

There was a title on the cover.

I took it in my hand.

I opened it.

On the first page was the sentence I saw on my desk.

Same line.

Same words.

The signature at the bottom of the page was not mine.

I closed the file.

I held it in my hand for a while.

The wall remained in place.

The file continued to circulate between the two parties.
