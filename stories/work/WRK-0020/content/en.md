# Error

The number marked in red needed correction. At the time, the unit was looking for who wrote it before the number. I opened the file and tried to understand that they were not the same problem.

There was a small red sign on the table.

Next to:

ERROR

it said.

Below it was the file number and date.

A small number had changed at the end of a line.

We had to fix it before asking who made the mistake. The supervisor asked whose session it was processed first. It was my session. I might have written the number wrong; I wasn't going to deny it. But the record did not separate the line I sent from the line that was changed later. For a while, I wavered between defending myself and solving the problem. Even this hesitation took time. For the person waiting in front of us, who was right was not as urgent as when the correct number would enter the system.

The old number was crossed out and the new one was written next to it.

There was an initial underneath it.

The case was closed.

The new person I was working with that day took the file.

“What was the mistake here?”

“A number was misspelled.”

“Who wrote it?”

I looked at the file.

There was money.

But it was not clear to whom the article belonged.

"I don't know."

“Who fixed it?”

I showed the initials at the bottom.

“This person.”

“Who made the first mistake?”

I looked at the file again.

This time I didn't answer.

The number was crossed out.

The old version was still readable.

But besides:

Error reason:

There was no such area.

After a while, the same file appeared again.

This time another line was marked.

Red pen again.

Again:

Corrected.

The newcomer looked at the old correction first.

Then to the new one.

“This is the second time.”

"Yes."

“Is it the same as the first mistake?”

"Not."

He placed the files side by side.

In one case the date was wrong.

Number on the other.

“So why was it a mistake again?”

I looked at the files.

Both had the same name of the institution.

The same form was used.

The same boxes were filled.

But there was no sign in the file indicating this.

The error list was opened at the meeting.

There were lines on the screen:

Wrong date.

Missing document.

Incorrect number.

Wrong record.

Process again.

At the meeting they counted corrected records. The number of errors had fallen from eleven last month to three, and they called it good news.

The number on the screen had changed.

Eleven to three.

Someone:

“This is an important development.”

he said.

Nobody opened the files.

After the meeting, the newcomer opened the remaining three files on the screen.

“Are these all different?”

"Some."

“Which ones are the same?”

We started to open the files.

The same date was wrong in two different files.

In one case, the application date was entered incorrectly.

In the other case, the same field was entered incorrectly again.

The two people had different names.

The file numbers were different.

Newcomer:

“Two people made the same mistake.”

he said.

"Maybe."

"How?"

“Maybe they both misunderstood the same thing.”

“So how do we know?”

I looked at the file.

We didn't know.

On the edge of another file:

Corrected.

it said.

Below:

Checked.

Newcomer:

“There was a mistake here.”

"Yes."

“Fixed.”

"Yes."

“Then it was checked.”

"Yes."

“So do we know why the same error occurs?”

I looked at the last page of the file.

It was empty.

Someone during lunch:

“Which error occurs most?”

he asked.

“Wrong date.”

said another.

"Why?"

"Carelessness."

“Every time?”

"Generally."

“Could there be another reason?”

Nobody answered.

After a while someone else said:

“People make mistakes.”

he said.

He took his tea.

“It will be fixed.”

The topic has changed.

In the evening I found an old note on the edge of a file.

It was written in pencil:

The same mistake was repeated.

Below it is another handwritten text:

Corrected.

A little below:

Will check again.

At the bottom:

Checked.

The case was closed.

There was no name next to the first note.

It wasn't in the second note either.

The last line had only one initial.

There was an empty space at the edge of the page.

Why did it happen?

The next day, the new person brought a paper.

He left it on the table.

There were four titles on it:

Where is the mistake?

Who noticed?

Who fixed it?

Why did it happen?

“Are we going to fill these?”

he asked.

"I don't know."

“We can find the first three.”

I looked at the paper.

"Yes."

“The last one?”

I was silent for a while.

“We can't always find it.”

The newcomer took the pen.

Why did it happen?

He put a small line next to the line.

“If we don't know, do we leave it blank?”

“Let's leave it at that.”

Another error occurred that day.

There was incorrect information in a file.

One person noticed.

Someone else corrected it.

Someone else checked again.

All three's names appeared in different places.

The new person opened the paper.

Where is the mistake?

filled it.

Who noticed?

filled it.

Who fixed it?

filled it.

He stopped at the last line.

Why did it happen?

He held the pen in the air.

Then he left it on the table.

“We don't know.”

I looked at the file.

"Yes."

He put the paper into the file.

This time we didn't fill the gap.

We closed the file.
