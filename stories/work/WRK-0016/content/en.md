# The Same

When the new employee in the unit sat at his desk, he had a few papers in his hand.

He placed the papers in front of him.

He took one.

Then the other one.

He looked at me.

“What do I do now?”

I showed him how to swipe the card first and then turn on the screen. He looked at the paper in his hand for a while and said, "I thought we were going to help people here." I didn't give him a chance to correct himself. “We will,” I said. They gave him three lists that day. He rewrote the first in the second list, and the second in the third. In the evening, he turned to me and asked, "Which list of people did we help?" he asked. I opened my mouth; I couldn't find a sentence. Before that, I remembered that I was the one here asking the same question.

I opened my mouth to answer.

“Wait for now.”

After saying that, I kept silent.

I've heard this sentence many times before.

I didn't remember who said it.

The man didn't say anything either.

He returned to his desk.

A file arrived.

The newcomer opened his cover.

He read the first page.

He turned the second page.

“Why are we doing this?”

“This is the way.”

“Why is the procedure like this?”

I looked at the file.

I waited as if I would find an answer.

I couldn't find.

"I don't know."

I said.

The man nodded.

He continued reading the file.

After a while he came back with another piece of paper in his hand.

“Something is missing here.”

"What?"

"I don't know."

He handed me the paper.

On the file:

It will be checked again.

it said.

“Checked.”

he said.

"Yes."

“So why again?”

“We'll see again.”

“What will we look for?”

I didn't answer.

I left the paper on my desk.

Someone during lunch:

“The new arrival asks a lot of questions.”

he said.

"Yes."

“He gets used to it.”

"Maybe."

“Were you like this too?”

I thought for a while.

"I can't remember."

He took a sip of his tea.

“People get used to it.”

Then:

“After a while he doesn't ask questions.”

he said.

I did not answer.

In the afternoon, the new person left a piece of paper on my desk.

He drew three columns:

work to be done

expected result

Who will

The first column was filled.

The other two were empty.

“Why did you do this?”

“To understand the file.”

“Why didn't you write down the expected result?”

"I don't know."

Then he looked at me.

“Do you know?”

I looked at the paper.

"No."

“So how do we start?”

This time I didn't answer right away.

“Let's ask first.”

I said.

“From whom?”

I thought for a while.

"I don't know."

The man smiled.

“Neither of us know.”

"Yes."

We opened the file together.

On the first page there was work to be done.

Signatures on the second page.

Control in the third.

I'm sending it on the fourth.

On the last page:

Necessary action will be taken.

it said.

The newcomer pointed to the last line.

"What does this mean?"

“What is necessary will be done.”

“For what purpose?”

I didn't answer.

After a while:

“We should ask this too.”

he said.

I nodded.

We took the file to the relevant table.

We showed the paper.

“There is no expected result here.”

I said.

The person on the other end looked at the file.

“Not necessary.”

"From where?"

“This is the way.”

I fell silent when I heard the sentence.

I said the same sentence a while ago.

Now someone else was saying it.

The newcomer looked at me.

I looked at the file.

When we return to the table:

“Should we leave it at that?”

he asked.

"No."

"What are we going to do?"

"I don't know."

"Then?"

I pulled the paper in front of me.

“Let's write down what we want to happen.”

“Do we know?”

“Not exactly.”

"Then?"

I thought for a while.

"Let's write what we know. Let's leave blank what we don't know."

On paper:

Expected result:

I wrote.

I left the bottom empty.

Then:

Who will:

I wrote.

The newcomer wrote his own name.

Then he looked at me.

“You do it too.”

he said.

He wrote my name.

I didn't take the paper back from him.

The next day the same file arrived again.

On the first page there was a small section:

expected result

Below is what we wrote.

Next to:

It will be followed.

it said.

Newcomer:

“Who added this?”

he asked.

"I don't know."

“Does it matter?”

I looked at the paper.

"I think."

"Why?"

He placed his finger on the new section on the page.

“He wasn't here yesterday.”

I looked for a while.

"Yes."

“There is now.”

I nodded.

There was no name underneath.

Another day newcomer:

“If we did it this way, we would wait less.”

he said.

As soon as the sentence was finished, an old answer came to my mind:

It's been tried before.

I didn't say.

“What do you expect to change?”

I asked.

“Waiting time will decrease.”

"Another?"

“The person who applied will see the result sooner.”

“Let's write this.”

I put the paper in front of him.

He wrote.

Then:

“Shall we try?”

he said.

“Let's write down who will do it first.”

We worked together.

He corrected one line.

I am a line.

We changed the old table somewhere.

We added a small note elsewhere.

An error occurred in a file.

We couldn't find out who did it.

We didn't write anything next to it.

We just fixed the bug.

Then we closed the file.

In the evening I found an old paper on my desk.

There was only one sentence on it:

This is the way.

I held the paper in my hand for a long time.

I didn't fold it.

I didn't put it in the drawer.

I left it on my desk.

The newcomer stopped by the door as he left.

“Should we continue tomorrow?”

he said.

“Let's do it.”

“Aren't you going to say wait for now?”

I smiled.

"No."

The man came out.

Old paper remained on the table.

This is the way.

I drew a small line underneath it.

I left the paper where it was.
