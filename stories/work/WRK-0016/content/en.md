# WRK-0016 — Inside

Eventually I left.

I do not know how much time had passed.

Years?

Decades?

I did not calculate.

The door opened.

I went outside.

Nobody followed.

Nobody said:

“Come back.”

I walked for a while.

Then I stopped.

I did not know where to go.

For years I had known when and where I was supposed to be.

Now nobody was telling me.

I went home.

The next morning I woke at eight.

Nobody had called.

I got up anyway.

I dressed.

I sat at the table.

Eight ten.

Eight twenty.

Eight thirty.

Nobody came.

The door was open.

I stood in front of it.

I looked outside.

Then I turned back.

The room had not changed.

The clock.

The desk.

The chair.

The paper.

Everything was there.

Then I understood:

Leaving the inside takes longer than passing through the door.

For years I had been taught rules.

Then I realized that most of the rules had never been written.

I corrected myself.

I came without being called.

I became silent without being told.

I thought about extra work without anyone asking.

I measured myself without anyone measuring me.

I had left.

But the order inside had come with me.

I took the blank sheet from the table.

For the first time I wrote a question nobody had asked me to write:

**What was all this for?**

There was no answer.

I waited.

Then I stopped waiting.

I folded the paper.

Put it in my pocket.

Opened the door.

This time I went outside.

Behind me was a door.

Ahead was a road.

Inside me there was still the house.

I did not want to destroy it.

Because when some houses collapse, they bury the person living inside them.

I wanted to rethink its walls.

Move the clock.

Change the direction of the door.

Remove the chair when it was no longer needed.

Ask the paper what it was for.

See the person behind the number.

And, when necessary, say:

“No.”

For the first time I began walking inside my own house.

I did not know where I was going.

But I was no longer lost simply because nobody had told me where to go.

Perhaps freedom was not outside.

Perhaps it began when the meaning of the door changed.
