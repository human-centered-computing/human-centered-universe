# The House Inside

I arrived at eight even when nobody asked.

That was when I understood: the clock was no longer on the wall. It was in me.

I followed rules nobody mentioned. I corrected myself before anyone corrected me. I measured my own work. I compared my numbers. I heard the supervisor's voice in an empty room.

One evening I stood at the door. For the first time I did not know whether I was leaving the house or carrying it with me.

Outside, nobody asked for a number. Nobody told me where to sit. Nobody gave me a form.

I waited for an instruction. None came.

“Who am I when nobody is measuring me?”

I did not answer. The question was enough.

I began with small things: choosing when to stop, asking why, listening without counting, remembering names, giving credit before taking it.

The house did not disappear. It had entered me too deeply.

But a house inside a person can be changed. A room can be opened. A wall can be moved. A door can be made wider.

I no longer wanted to destroy the house.

I wanted to rebuild it.

And the person doing the rebuilding had to be the person who lived inside it.
