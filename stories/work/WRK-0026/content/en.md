# The Last Task

There were traces of processing in the file with LAST written on the cover in capital letters. We could only find out if the applicant actually received a response by talking to him.

There was only one word on the file:

THE END

Below it was the file number, date and a few initials.

I turned the pages.

The chief wanted the file closed immediately. When I listened to him for the first time, I also realized that his intention was not just to produce a number to be displayed. The superior unit was waiting for a report from him; The account for each missing line would come to his desk. This didn't make him right, but it did explain why he heard my question as a threat. I showed him separately the process required to close the case and the work required for the person to actually get answers. First he said we couldn't do both. Then he asked the time and called the relevant unit.

The application was received.

The documents had been checked.

It was sent to the relevant unit.

The necessary investigation was carried out.

Approval was received.

An information record was created.

On the last page there was a box:

Completed.

There was a signature area next to it.

There was no signature.

I left the file on the table.

The new person I was working with that day sat in the chair across from me.

“Is it over?”

“That's what it looks like here.”

“I'm not asking about the file.”

“What are you asking?”

“Is the job done?”

I looked at the file again.

“All procedures have been completed.”

“This is not the answer.”

We remained silent for a while.

Then I handed the file to him.

"What do you think?"

He looked at the last page.

“No results are written.”

"Yes."

“So it's not finished.”

I got the file back.

I looked at the space on the last page.

There was a large area there that I had not noticed before.

Under the title:

Result:

The line had been drawn.

There was no continuation.

I took the pen in my hand.

I left it without writing anything.

The same file came back in the afternoon.

There was a new note on it:

It has been done accordingly.

There was history underneath.

There was a signature.

There was a control initial.

The newcomer looked at the file.

"Now?"

“What now?”

“Is it over now?”

“What was necessary was done.”

“What was done?”

I opened the file.

I found the relevant page.

“It says here.”

He read it.

“It has been sent to the relevant unit.”

"Yes."

"Later?"

“What was necessary was done.”

"What does this mean?"

I didn't answer.

I looked for another page in the file.

I couldn't find.

“Maybe the consequences are outside.”

“Where is it outside?”

“The applicant has it.”

“Do we know this?”

I closed the file.

"No."

The newcomer nodded.

“So it's not the end.”

After a while, the owner of the same file came.

He waited at the door.

He showed the number in his hand.

“They told me it was done.”

I looked at the file.

“It looks complete here.”

"What happened?"

I've heard this question before.

But this time, none of the sentences in the file were enough.

“The transactions have been completed.”

“I'm not asking that.”

He waited.

“What is the result?”

I turned the last page of the file.

Completed.

There was nothing else underneath.

“No results visible here.”

“They told me it was done.”

"Yes."

“So what is completed?”

This time I didn't answer.

The man looked at the file.

Then to me.

“Did what I wanted happen?”

I looked at the file again.

"I don't know."

He bowed his head.

“I don't know either.”

I put the file in front of him.

“What was supposed to happen?”

He thought for a while.

Then he told.

It wasn't long.

It was a few sentences.

A few sentences not in the file.

I listened.

I didn't take notes.

As the man gets up:

“Where will you write this?” he asked.

I stopped for a moment.

“I don't know yet.”

He headed for the door.

Then he returned.

“At least now you know what's missing.”

The door is closed.

The file remained on the table.

In the evening, the new arrival came to me.

“What did he say?”

“What the outcome should be.”

“Did you write it?”

"No."

"From where?"

“We need to think about how to know first.”

“How will we know?”

This time it didn't seem strange to hear the question from him.

I bought a piece of paper.

I drew three lines.

work to be done

expected result

How will we know?

The newcomer looked at the paper.

“The third line did not exist before.”

“There is now.”

“What will we write?”

"I don't know."

“Are we going to leave it blank again?”

"Now."

We wrote the operation on the first line.

In the second line, the result that the applicant expects.

The third line was left blank.

We both stared into that space for a while.

Then the newcomer said:

“We can ask the applicant,” he said.

"Yes."

“What if he answers?”

“We will write it in the file.”

“Can we say that the result has been confirmed?”

“Let's write down what we see first.”

He nodded.

He put the paper into the file.

The next day we called the applicant again.

This time the file was open before us.

The newcomer spoke.

“Did what you told me the other day happen?”

The man thought for a while.

"Yes."

“Exactly?”

"Yes."

"When?"

He said the date.

“Have you been informed?”

“Given.”

“Do you understand?”

The man nodded.

"Yes."

The newcomer looked at me.

I looked at the paper.

He wrote the date across the third line.

Then he waited for a while.

“What do we write now?”

“What we saw.”

“What did we see?”

“What the applicant wanted has come true.”

“I'm writing this.”

He wrote.

After a while, he wrote under the paper:

The result is confirmed.

wrote.

This time the sentence did not stand alone.

There was history underneath.

There was who to talk to.

There was what was said.

The file came back to my desk.

I turned the pages one by one.

There were transactions as before.

Application.

Control.

Sending.

Approval.

To inform.

But this time there was something else on the last page.

Expected result:
Informing the applicant and finalizing the request.

How will we know:
Informing the applicant and explaining the result of the request to him/her.

Result:
It happened.

Verification:
Verified by the applicant.

There were names underneath.

The name of the person who came up with the idea.

The name of the person who developed the work.

Names of those who made the application.

The name of the person who made the final correction.

In one line:

Contribution whose source cannot be verified.

It was empty.

This time, no one looked for a name to fill the gap.

I returned to the first page of the file.

There was a small correction on the edge.

This was the fix I made.

My name wasn't there.

The newcomer noticed this.

“Shall we add this too?”

"What?"

“To fix it.”

I looked for a while.

“Write down what you did.”

He wrote:

Correction: elimination of date error in application record.

Then he added my name to it.

He gave me the pen.

“Can you check?”

I read.

"Ok."

He did not close the file.

"Another?"

I looked at the pages.

"No."

This time he closed the file.

By evening, only one file was left on the table.

On it again:

THE END

it said.

I opened the file.

The checklist was completed.

The expected result was written.

Contributions were visible.

The result was recorded.

Verification had been made.

But there was still a place for signature at the end of the file.

I took the pen in my hand.

I waited a moment.

I turned to the first page before signing.

Then I looked at the middle pages.

Finally, I came to the last page again.

I touched the pen to the paper.

I signed it.

I put down my pen.

The new arrival was standing near the door.

“Is it over?”

I looked at the file.

"Yes."

“How do you know?”

I turned the last page to him.

“We know what needs to happen.”

He looked for a while.

Then to the date on the page.

“And it happened.”

I nodded.

He closed the file.

He left it on the table.

A voice came from the corridor.

The cover of a file was closed.

Then another voice.

A chair was pulled out.

The door opened.

The new arrival went out.

So I walked behind him.

A closed file remained on the table.

On:

THE END

it said.

Looking at this word for the first time, I didn't wonder what ended.
