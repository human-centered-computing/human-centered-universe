# Nameless

The name of the person who prepared it was not on the cover of the file left on my desk. As I read the sentences in it, I thought about the hours someone spent creating them. The last signature was visible; The initial labor was not visible.

There was an old file on my desk.

There was no name on the cover.

There were names in the file.

Signatures.

Dates.

Notes.

Control records.

On the first page:

Prepared by:

it said.

It was empty.

I closed the file.

Then I opened it again.

The void was still there.

There was a table on the first page.

I read one of the lines.

The sentence sounds familiar.

I also remembered which evening I wrote it. At that time, I crumpled the paper, thinking it wouldn't work, and then I opened it and corrected the sentence. Even the original flaw was preserved in the file. I could give this as proof; Still, I wanted to hide it first. Asking for an idea back felt like I was denying it to someone else. I didn't know where this embarrassment came from. I thought, why should a person be ashamed to recognize his own efforts?

I moved on to another line.

It was like I'd seen him before too.

I took the pen in my hand.

I looked at the line under a word.

Then to the letters.

I stayed like that for a while.

I closed the file.

In the afternoon, my colleague who started work that day stopped by my desk.

He showed the file in his hand.

It was not clear who had prepared the file. Many names appeared beside its transactions, but not the preparer's.

He opened the file.

There was a correction on one page.

“Who did this?”

"I don't know."

He turned another page.

There was another handwriting there.

"This much?"

"I don't know."

He closed the file.

“What happens when this happens?”

“The file remains.”

“What about the person who did it?”

I didn't answer.

I took the file to the section where the old files are located.

Some of the files lying side by side had the name of the person who prepared them.

In some cases, it's just the controller.

Some have the final signature.

In a file:

Prepared by: —

it said.

Below:

Controlled by:

A name.

Approved by:

Another name.

I turned the pages.

I stopped at a sentence on the first page.

I knew the sentence.

After a while I asked someone sitting at one of the old tables.

“Do you remember this file?”

He looked at the file.

"No."

“That sentence?”

He read it.

"I don't know."

“Have you seen it before?”

"It could be."

“Who could have written it?”

He looked at the first page of the file.

“It's not written here.”

I took the file in front of me again.

This time I looked at the text rather than the sentence.

Some of the letters looked similar to each other.

The letter k in a word.

r elsewhere.

The place where the pen presses at the end of the line.

I knew these.

I took out one of my own papers.

I rewrote the same sentence.

I put the two articles side by side.

It wasn't the same.

But they both belonged to me.

I left the pen on the table.

The new person came to me again.

“Did you find it?”

"I think."

"Who?"

"I."

He looked at the file.

Then to me.

"Are you sure?"

“I wrote the sentence.”

“Why isn't your name on the file?”

"I don't know."

“When did you write it?”

"I can't remember."

“So how do you know for sure?”

I looked at the sentence.

“Because I remember how I thought.”

The man didn't say anything.

He looked at the file again.

The file went to the meeting after a while.

When he came back, a new note had been added to the first page:

The idea has been reconsidered.

Underneath was someone else's name.

I read the rest of the sentence.

The thought I had written before was included in other sentences.

I recognized my own sentence.

But my name wasn't on it.

I closed the file.

I sat at my table in the evening.

I took out old papers from the drawer.

Some of them had dates.

Some just a few words.

A small scratch on the edge of one.

An incomplete sentence in another person.

I spread them all out on the table.

Then I put the pages in the file next to them.

Some sentences matched each other.

Some words.

Some fixes.

But none of them had my name on them.

The next day the new person said:

“Did you think of this?”

he asked.

"Yes."

“Why didn't you say so?”

“I told you before.”

"Later?"

“Then it went into the file.”

“With your name?”

I looked at the file.

"No."

He was silent for a while.

"Alright?"

“The job is done.”

“Is this enough?”

I didn't answer the question.

After a while, the same file appeared in front of me again.

On the first page:

Prepared by:

It was still empty.

There were other people's names underneath.

There were my sentences in the file.

I had corrections.

But I didn't have a name.

I ran my finger over the first sentence.

I waited for a while.

Then I took the pen.

I looked for a place where I could write my name.

I couldn't find.

I left the pen on the table.

I wrote a small note on the margin of the file:

There was this sentence.

I looked for a while.

I did not delete the note.

I closed the file.

There was still no name on the cover.
