# Absence

The first thing that would indicate an employee's absence was not their chair, but their ongoing work. There were notes on his desk; The person who would ask why he wrote those notes was nowhere to be seen.

The chair at the table was empty.

No one was sitting on it.

The table was not empty.

The man at the desk was no longer coming to work. That's why they said "he couldn't stand it". Nobody asked what he couldn't stand. Sometimes he would stay up late at night, come back early the next day and complete the work left behind by others. One day, while he was explaining his ideas, his superior interrupted him and said, "Finish what you have first." After that I only saw him finishing work. It was easy to look at his absence and imagine myself in his place; Then it was harder not to listen to what he was saying while sitting next to him.

There was a file.

There was a glass.

There was a pen with an open cap.

A window remained open on the computer screen.

The new person I was working with that day stood next to the table.

The newcomer asked who had sat at the empty desk. I paused. I knew the worker's name, but not enough about the things they had left there. I told him the name.

The newcomer looked at the computer.

“When will he arrive?”

I didn't answer.

The next day the chair was empty again.

The file was in the same place.

There was a dried mark inside the glass.

The pen was left on the edge of the table.

Newcomer:

“I need to look at the file.”

he said.

“Look.”

“Is this his file?”

“The file he made.”

“What's the difference?”

I opened the file.

There was a name on the first page.

Other names on the following pages.

His initials appeared somewhere.

A correction on another page.

“Who owns the file?”

"I don't know."

“His name is here.”

“Having a name is different.”

The newcomer looked at the file.

He didn't say anything.

The file was passed from table to table.

At each table something else was added.

Someone changed a date.

Another person completed an incomplete document.

Someone else noted.

Then the file came back to the old table.

The chair was still empty.

They left the file on the table.

Nobody sat down.

During lunch:

“What happened to him?”

I asked.

"I don't know."

“Who knows?”

“Someone knows.”

"Who?"

He lifted his shoulders.

Then another topic was discussed.

A week later, the glass on the table was removed.

There was no pen.

The file was still there.

The new person came to the table.

He pulled the chair.

He opened the drawers before sitting down.

There were some papers inside.

In one:

Checked again.

In another:

There may be a problem here.

A little note at the bottom:

Let's see the result first.

The newcomer took the note in his hand.

“Who wrote this?”

"I don't know."

“Is his name on file?”

“Look.”

He looked at the file.

There was no name next to the note.

The newcomer sat at the table.

He turned on the computer.

He found the file that remained open.

There was a table on the screen.

One line was left unfinished.

Newcomer:

“Should I finish this?”

“Do you know what to write?”

"No."

“Then wait.”

After a while:

“What did he want to do here?”

he asked.

I looked at the screen.

"I don't know."

“So why did he leave it?”

"I don't know."

The newcomer leaned back in his chair.

“He completed everything.”

“It seems so.”

“Only this place is empty.”

We looked at the empty cell on the screen.

One day I had to take the file to another room.

Someone near the door:

“Whose file is this?”

he asked.

"I don't know."

“Who is following?”

I said a name.

“He's not here anymore.”

“So who?”

I couldn't answer.

I left the file on the table.

“Someone gets it.”

“Who is someone?”

"I don't know."

The next day the file was on another table.

A new note was added:

It has been taken over.

Underneath was the new employee's name.

The former employee's name was not available.

His initials were on the previous pages of the file.

The newcomer looked at the file.

“Mine now?”

“You will follow.”

“So who was following before?”

“You see it in the file.”

“I see.”

He ran his finger over the old initials.

“So what did this person do?”

He opened the file.

He looked at the pages.

“It's not written here.”

In the evening I passed by the old table.

The table was now completely empty.

The drawer had been cleaned.

The computer had been removed.

There was nothing on the chair.

There was a small scratch left near the wall.

I've seen it before.

It must have occurred while pulling the table.

I put my hand on the line.

The new arrival called from the corridor:

“Who was at this table?”

I'm back.

"Someone."

"Ordinary?"

I said your name.

“What was he doing?”

I thought for a moment.

I remembered the files.

Corrections.

Notes.

The painting left unfinished.

Then:

“It was working.”

I said.

The newcomer laughed.

“I know that too.”

We looked at the table together.

The next day, the new person brought one of the old files.

“Look at this.”

There was an old sentence on one side of the file.

Newcomer:

“Is this his writing?”

he asked.

"Yes."

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

“It has no name.”

"No."

“How do you know?”

I looked at the pen.

Then to the sentence.

“Because he wrote the same thing before.”

The new person did not close the file.

“So he left something here.”

he said.

I didn't answer.

The file remained open.

No one was sitting at the table anymore.

But the sentence he wrote was still there.
