# The One Who Replaces Me

When the new person arrived, he was young.

He asked many questions, as I had on my first day.

“Where does this door lead?”

“Why is this record kept?”

“How long do people stay here?”

“Why is this place empty?”

I answered.

Because by then I knew how to answer.

I showed him the files.

The records.

The rules.

The people.

Then I tried to explain what was not on paper.

“Be careful with this person.”

“This file waits here.”

“They do not write this down.”

“Everyone knows this.”

One day he asked:

“What can change here?”

I was silent for a long time.

“I do not know.”

The next day he sat in my place.

I stood across from him.

The chair was the same.

The desk was the same.

The papers were the same.

The faces were the same.

Only I was gone.

After a while I went outside.

I stood at the door and looked back.

The new person was sitting inside.

The way I had sat years before.

Then I understood:

People can be replaced.

Places remain.

But the invisible memory carried by a place becomes thinner each time a person leaves.

Someone can take a person's place.

Nobody can take the years lived by that person.
