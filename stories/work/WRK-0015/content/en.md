# Punishment

In this room, punishments were not always given in the name of punishment. Sometimes a completed painting would return to the table, waiting like a new task. When I saw the file coming back, I wanted to understand who had it repeated and why.

It didn't take long to figure out what the job that was left on my desk was.

I had to prepare the same table again.

The table had been prepared before.

The lines were full.
There were numbers.
There was the name of the person controlling it.

At the bottom of the last page:

Completed.

it said.

On the paper attached to it:

It will be prepared again.

Below:

It is requested that it be rearranged completely.

I took the paper.

I knew why I was given this job. At the meeting the other day, I said that the chart did not show results. “Then arrange the numbers again,” said the Chief. There was no threat in his words; Some in the room had their heads bowed. When the name of the punishment was duty, the objector seemed to be avoiding work. Every day he made me rewrite numbers, the less time I would have to investigate why they weren't showing results. Still, the first morning I wanted to find an error in the chart. If I found a real mistake, I would be able to tell myself that this repetition was not in vain.

I opened the table.

I rewrote the first lines.

Then I moved on to the second table.

The numbers were the same.

After a while I realized.

What was requested was not new information.

It was the same information.

In another order.

The completed table would be checked again, though no one said what needed to change. The supervisor pointed to the name on the paper as the person who would decide.

Then he moved on to another file.

The next morning the painting arrived back on my desk.

A small mark had been made with a red pen.

A single word was written next to it:

Again.

There was no explanation.

This time I moved the columns.

I made the first column the second.

Second and third.

Third again, first again.

The numbers remained the same.

The file appeared to have changed.

But the information inside was the same.

I delivered.

Nobody:

“What changed?”

He didn't ask.

I met someone at the end of the corridor at lunchtime.

“Is it your file too?”

"Yes."

“How many?”

"Second."

He smiled.

“My fourth.”

“What are you changing?”

"I don't know."

"How?"

“They find something different every time.”

“What do they find?”

He lifted his shoulders.

"I don't know."

We remained silent for a while.

Then:

“You expect them to find it at some point.”

he said.

“What should they find?”

"Something."

In the evening, I saw the table of the man who did the same job for the fourth time. Four separate printouts were standing side by side. In the first one the title was in the middle, in the second one it was on the left; In the third, the numbers were arranged from smallest to largest, and in the fourth, the numbers were arranged from largest to smallest. “Which one will they use?” I asked. “None of them,” he said. Then he showed me one of the papers. He had drawn an account on the back; It explained how pending applications could be resolved in two stages. “When did you do this?” “While waiting for the second painting.” “Did you show it?” He turned the paper over. “I don't have time for this on the next mission.” That evening I looked in his face for the dullness of someone tired of work. He was still thinking about the solution. This was the terrible thing: he knew that the desire was alive within him, and every day he saw less and less room for its use.

Another day, I was asked to renumber the pages of the same file.

The numbers were already there.

I did it anyway.

When I got to the last page, I deleted the previous number.

I wrote a new one.

The contents of the file have not changed.

The numbers have changed.

On a new sheet of paper:

It has been done.

I wrote.

Then I added this paper to the file.

A new job had appeared, showing that the previous job had been done.

After a while, another task came.

“Check these again.”

“Checked.”

"Again."

“In what way?”

"General."

“What should I look for in general?”

“Is there a mistake?”

I opened the file.

I started from the first page.

Then to the second.

To the third.

To the fourth.

I looked for the same errors.

I couldn't find.

On the fifth page, the font of one word was different from the others.

I fixed it.

I submitted the file.

"Ok."

they said.

This time he didn't come back.

I looked at my evening table.

My hands were tired.

My eyes were burning.

I had done something during the day.

I knew this.

But I didn't know why the things were connected to each other.

The file was standing.

The painting was standing.

The control remained.

The signatures remained.

The work I did was also there.

I closed the file.

The next day a new mission arrived.

It was necessary to pass the same information into three separate tables.

Alphabetical order in a table.

Date order in one.

Number order in one.

The data was the same.

The tables were different.

I made a mistake in one line.

I noticed.

I fixed it.

Then I rewrote the same information into the other table.

The person next to me:

“Isn't it over?”

he said.

“It's over.”

“Speed ​​up a little.”

I accelerated.

I skipped a line.

I'm back.

I fixed it.

I continued.

In the afternoon I kept my hands motionless on the table for a while.

I opened and closed my fingers.

The person at the next table looked.

“Are you tired?”

"A little."

“It ends.”

"What?"

"Work."

I looked up.

“What if it doesn't end?”

He laughed.

“New business comes.”

After a while, another file was left on my desk.

On:

It was not done properly.

it said.

There was my name underneath it.

I opened the file.

Inside was the work I had been doing for days.

Tables.

Controls.

Numbers.

Signatures.

Notes.

They were all there.

“I did what was necessary.”

I said.

The person in front of me looked at the file.

“It doesn't look like that in the file.”

“I made the painting.”

"Yes."

“I checked.”

"Yes."

“I numbered it.”

"Yes."

“Then why wasn't necessary done?”

There was no answer.

The file remained in front of me.

In the evening, after everyone left, I opened the file again.

I turned the pages one by one.

I was looking for something.

I didn't know what I was looking for.

Not the work I do.

Not what they are made of.

Maybe there was a connection between them.

Maybe there wasn't.

I came to the last page.

I took the pen.

I wrote a small question in the margin of the file:

What will change?

I looked for a while.

Then I closed the pen.

The next morning, the file arrived on my desk again.

On it only:

Again.

it said.

I reached out to the pen.

Then I pulled it back.

I looked at the first page.

I looked at the last page.

All the transactions in between passed before my eyes.

Same paintings.

Same numbers.

Same signatures.

Same file.

I turned the file towards the person in front of me.

“What should happen at the end of this?”

I asked.

This time he didn't answer right away.

He looked at the file.

Then to me.

He left the pen in his hand on the table.

So I put down my own pen.

Nothing was done in the room for a while.

The file remained open.
