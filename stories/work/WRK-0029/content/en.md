# Without a Name

Those whose names were written in the file and those who actually produced the lines did not correspond to each other. Would just typing a name be enough to make a contribution visible?

There were names on the cover of the file.

Applicant's name.

The controller's name.

The name of the person who approved it.

Receiver name.

A few more initials on the last page.

I didn't have my name.

Inside the file, there was a three-column chart showing the location, responsible person and result of the application. I didn't just ask the supervisor to add my name to the cover. I suggested that we investigate together when I prepared the charts, who changed them, and which decision came from which line. If only one hero was found, the remaining effort would be erased again. We separated the names and evidence. We left an empty space for the line whose source we could not find. Amir stared into that space for a while. “Is this visible too?” he said. “What we don't know needs to be seen,” I said. For the first time in years, I didn't lower my voice to say that sentence.

I opened the file.

I saw a sentence on the first page.

I recognized it.

I wrote it.

There was another sentence on the second page.

It was mine too.

A correction on the third page.

Section added by someone else on the fourth page.

Everyone's name was in the file.

I didn't have mine.

The new person I was working with that day approached my desk.

“Did you prepare this file?”

"No."

“What about this sentence?”

“I wrote it.”

“You don't have your name here.”

"I know."

He closed the file again.

"How is it going to be?"

"What?"

“It's what you do.”

“We know where he is.”

“Without your name?”

“Without my name.”

We opened the previous records of the file.

There was a change history on the computer.

History.

Hour.

User name.

One line was deleted.

Another line was added.

A table had been changed.

A file has been uploaded.

One of the usernames was me.

The newcomer looked at the screen.

“You appear here.”

"Yes."

“You don't appear in the file.”

"Yes."

He looked at the screen for a while.

“The two are not the same.”

"Not."

He turned off the screen.

There was a small section in the file.

Prepared by:

A short line across.

Controlled by: a noun.

Approved by: another name.

Implemented by: two names.

Receiver: a name.

The prepared section was empty.

The newcomer took the pen.

“Let's write you here.”

“We have no record showing that I was the preparer.”

“You wrote the sentences.”

“Some of them.”

“And you made the corrections.”

“Some of them.”

“And you changed the table.”

"Yes."

"Then?"

I looked at the file.

“It's something else that prepares.”

"What?"

"I don't know."

He left the pen on the table.

The Prepared by field remained empty.

Another file arrived in the afternoon.

This time my name was nowhere.

But there was a sentence in the file that I recognized.

Someone else correcting it next to you.

On the next page is a table similar to the one I used before.

Its title was the same:

Where? / Who has it? / What happened?

Under the painting was someone else's name.

Newcomer:

“Did you do this too?”

"No."

“They compared it.”

"Yes."

“You prepared the first one.”

"Yes."

“Who developed this?”

"I don't know."

We looked at the painting for a while.

There was a difference between its initial state and its final state.

The beginning was elsewhere.

The result is elsewhere.

It was not possible to say to whom some of the changes between the two belonged.

Newcomer:

“Do we need to separate these?”

"Maybe."

"How?"

“Looking at the changes.”

He opened the screen.

We reexamined the past.

Some lines had changed with my username.

Some with other users.

Some had no records.

One of the former employees stopped by in the evening.

I showed him the file.

“My name is not here.”

He looked.

“Is it supposed to happen?”

"I don't know."

“Did you do it?”

“Some of it.”

"Then?"

“My name is not visible.”

He looked at the file again.

“Can't you see what you're doing?”

“In some places.”

“So what are you looking for?”

I thought about the question.

“Exactly what I did.”

He closed the file.

“Can you find this?”

"Maybe."

“If you find it, write it down.”

“To the file?”

“Somewhere.”

The next day, a new section was added to the file.

Title:

Contribution History

There were a few lines underneath.

Newcomer:

“What will we write first?”

I looked at the first page of the file.

“Idea.”

He wrote.

Under:

“Development?”

"Yes."

"APPLICATION?"

"Yes."

"Correction?"

"Yes."

He stopped for a while.

"Completion?"

“Let's think about that later.”

Names were written across the first two lines.

We found some names from documents.

Some from the change history.

We could not identify some of them.

There were no names across a line.

Newcomer:

“What do we write here?”

“We don't know.”

“Should we leave it blank?”

"Yes."

He left it blank.

There was the same section in another file.

This time my name was only on one line.

Correction.

Next to it was the change I made.

The idea belonged to someone else.

Development to someone else.

Application for two people.

The result was checked by someone else.

Again, my name was not on the cover of the file.

The newcomer looked at the file.

“You appear here.”

"Yes."

“It's not on the cover.”

"Yes."

“Is this enough?”

I did not close the file.

"I don't know."

He waited for a while.

“If it had happened before, we would have written his name on the cover.”

"Maybe."

"Now?"

“We write down what he did.”

He took the pen in his hand.

“Is this more important?”

I looked at the file.

"Sometimes."

Before turning off the computer in the evening, I opened the change history.

My username was there again.

One line.

Then another line.

A painting.

A fix.

A file.

There was a date next to each one.

It was clear why some changes were made.

Some of them weren't.

I opened the first version of a line.

Then the final version.

The first sentence was mine.

The last sentence is someone else's.

The few changes in between were from different users.

The username was not visible on a change.

There was only the date on the screen.

I closed the file.

There was a file on the table.

My name wasn't on the cover.

There were some lines in it.

More appeared in the past on the computer.

The Preparer area was still empty.

I took the pen in my hand.

I looked at that space for a moment.

Then I didn't write anything.

I closed the file.

I turned to the computer.

Change history was still open.

My username was on the screen.

The changes I made were visible under the dates.

I turned off the screen.

Only the file remained on the table.

On the cover:

Prepared by: —

it said.
